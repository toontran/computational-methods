Mixed-tail-sharp objective continuation handoff
==============================================

Date: 2026-04-23

Scope
-----
This note summarizes the current understanding of the `mixed-tail-sharp`
matrix, with emphasis on the `force-b1` / `force-b1b2` intervention results,
the block-2 (`b2`) failure mode, and what to test next for similar
oracle-free objectives.


Current picture
---------------
1. Forcing `b1` and/or `b2` to oracle does not fix final oracle-2 recovery.

   The cleanest evidence is in:

     - `summary/current_block_sketch_oracle_projection_intervention_report.txt`
     - `summary/current_block_sketch_oracle_checks/internal_rank_diagnostics_interpretation.txt`

   Main result:

     - the final oracle-2 direction is often present in the current
       `[sketch; block]` rowspace,
     - but the selected second slot still does not align with the projected
       oracle frame.

   This points to a selection/compression/objective problem, not just missing
   oracle signal.

2. The failure is not only an early `b1` / `b2` capture problem.

   For `force-b1`:

     - `b2` is already frame-mixed;
     - the forced second slot from `b1` is almost immediately not preserved as
       the selected second column;
     - the catastrophic drop in projected-oracle cosine happens later, around
       `b27`.

   For `force-b1b2`:

     - `b3` is better than the `force-b1` `b2` state, but still not a clean
       continuation of the forced oracle frame;
     - the later collapse happens around `b25`.

   So `b2` is an early visible symptom, but not the whole story.

3. Internal oversampling helps retention, but rank-2 handoff can still lose
   oracle-2.

   The internal-rank diagnostics show that for `mixed-tail-sharp`, internal
   rank 8/16 can keep oracle-2 nearly perfectly inside the internal frame, yet
   the final rank-2 compression can still drop it. This supports a
   "compression / objective handoff" diagnosis.


What is special about `b2` after `force-b1`
-------------------------------------------
Primary references:

  - `summary/mixed_tail_sharp_force_b1_b2_howto_diagnostic_dump.txt`
  - `summary/mixed_tail_sharp_force_b1_block2_diagnostic_dump.txt`
  - `summary/diagnostic_dumps_howto.txt`

At `mixed-tail-sharp`, `force-b1`, `b2`:

1. The optimizer beats the QR projected-oracle candidate, but only narrowly.

   From the how-to-format dump:

     - `optimizer_sum = 6.634877679379`
     - `oracle QR candidate_sum = 6.604170964137`

2. Raw oracle projections are attractive before QR orthogonalization.

     - `oracle_raw_projection_scores sum = 6.670978020505`

   So the issue is not that oracle-2 is absent or weak in raw form. The trouble
   appears when turning the oracle projection into an orthonormal candidate
   frame.

3. Entropy-like terms are saturated and do not separate the candidates here.

   This matches the broader objective-design conclusion that entropy /
   anti-spike checks are useful but incomplete.

4. The optimizer's outside component is not explained well by "top deflated SVD
   direction" or by pure complement chasing.

   Checks already done:

     - It does not align strongly with the top right singular vector of
       `[sketch; block]` deflated by `opt1`.
     - It is only weakly aligned with the current-block or `[sketch; block]`
       complement direction `c_t`.
     - The outside component still has much stronger block-only score than the
       oracle-like alternatives.

   Interpretation:

     - this looks more like a high-scoring diffuse outside direction than a
       pure "local complement" failure.


Objective-design interpretation
-------------------------------
Primary references:

  - `summary/objective_design_oracle_free_full_benchmark_synthesis.txt`
  - `summary/objective_design_block_only_recheck_synthesis.txt`
  - `summary/objective_design_repeated_block_support_synthesis.txt`

The relevant takeaways for `mixed-tail-sharp` are:

1. Raw block score alone is not defensible.

   The full benchmark synthesis shows that plain block score strongly favors
   block-SVD-like directions across the matrix suite.

2. Entropy / concentration guards help, but they are not enough.

   A non-oracle direction can still have diffuse row response. That is exactly
   consistent with the `mixed-tail-sharp b2` behavior.

3. `obj_future_hmean` is the best per-matrix objective for `mixed-tail-sharp`
   in the broad benchmark.

   This matters because it says the useful signal is not just:

     - current-block gain, or
     - old-row memory,

   but a notion of repeated/future consistency.

4. Hard complement control is still useful as a conservative guard, but it does
   not fully explain this `b2` case.

   In this matrix/block, complement alignment exists but is modest. Penalizing
   it pushes in the right direction, but does not explain why the optimizer's
   outside direction still wins on fresh support.


How to think about `future_hmean`
---------------------------------
For continuation work, the best source context is:

  - `summary/objective_design_oracle_free_full_benchmark_synthesis.txt`
  - `summary/objective_design_repeated_block_support_synthesis.txt`
  - `summary/objective_design_block_only_recheck_synthesis.txt`

Use them in that order:

1. `oracle_free_full_benchmark_synthesis`

   Use this for the high-level objective ranking and the statement that
   `obj_future_hmean` is the best per-matrix objective for `mixed-tail-sharp`.

2. `repeated_block_support_synthesis`

   Use this for the underlying motivation:

     - raw fresh-row support is real,
     - but repeated raw support alone prefers block-SVD complements,
     - the missing ingredient is consistency with oracle-like / repeated
       support rather than one-block gain.

3. `block_only_recheck_synthesis`

   Use this to keep the semantics straight:

     - "block-only score" means `||A_block v||^2 * phi_pooled(v)`,
     - not score on stacked `[sketch; block]`.


Most important open question
----------------------------
Is `b2` uniquely bad, or does the same objective mismatch appear in many blocks?

The current evidence suggests:

  - `b2` is a useful microscope because it is early and easy to inspect;
  - but the late collapse near `b25-b27` says the same kind of issue may recur
    in other blocks, not just `b2`.


Next experiment to run
----------------------
Goal:

  Check per-block scores to see whether the `mixed-tail-sharp` problem is
  specifically "b2 is bad" or whether the same objective ordering appears in
  most blocks.

Per-block comparison to log for every block:

  candidates:
    - selected `opt2`
    - `q2_vs_q1oracle`
    - raw projected oracle-2
    - current-block complement
    - `[sketch; block]` deflated-SVD challenger
    - outside component of `opt2`

  score families:
    - full current objective score
    - block-only score
    - block-only gain
    - hard-complement-adjusted block-only score
    - future-hmean-style score, if available in the probe

  geometry:
    - oracle projected cosine / oracle mass
    - complement alignment `|v^T c_t|^2`
    - alignment with deflated `[sketch; block]` singular vectors
    - row concentration / entropy

Questions to answer from that dump:

1. Is `opt2` usually losing on block-only score but winning on full score?
2. Is `b2` unusually bad, or does that pattern show up in many blocks?
3. Does the outside direction keep winning because of one-block support only,
   or does it also score well under future-consistency probes?
4. Does the late failure region (`b25-b27`) look qualitatively different from
   the early `b2` mismatch?


Implementation notes
--------------------
1. Keep the trajectory fixed.

   Do not change the optimizer first. Re-score the same saved blockwise states
   under multiple objectives/candidates.

2. Use corrected block-only semantics.

   For fresh support, use:

     `block_only_score(v) = ||A_block v||^2 * phi_pooled(v)`

   not stacked `[sketch; block]` gain.

3. Treat `future_hmean` as a diagnostic target first.

   Do not immediately turn it into the online objective. First use it to test
   whether the bad outside directions are really one-block winners or whether
   they keep future support.

4. Keep the `b2` dump as the template.

   The easiest next step is a per-block table that extends the current
   `mixed-tail-sharp force-b1 b2` checks to all blocks for:

     - `normal`
     - `force-b1`
     - optionally `force-b1b2`


Recommended immediate task
--------------------------
Add a per-block `mixed-tail-sharp` objective probe that prints, for each block,
the candidate ordering under:

  - full score,
  - corrected block-only score,
  - hard complement penalty,
  - and future-hmean-style score.

Then compare:

  - `b2`,
  - a midstream "good" block,
  - and the late failure blocks around `b25-b27`.

That will tell us whether `b2` is a special pathological block or just the
first visible instance of the same objective mismatch that later causes the
collapse.
