Why staying outside the [A_w; A_{w+1}] window space wins
==========================================================

Date: 2026-04-25 (revised)

Question
--------
The earlier `future_hmean_optimizer_investigation_synthesis` showed that a
direction inside rowspan([A_w; A_{w+1}]) achieves a future-HM score 5e3 to
8e4 times higher than the streaming optimizer's v2. Yet the streaming
optimizer's outside-window v2 leads to better long-horizon performance. Two
separate questions need answers:

(a) What is "outside the window" geometrically?
(b) Is the streaming v2 stuck in a local optimum that gradient ascent fails
    to escape, or is it the global maximum of its own objective?

Both are now answered concretely.

(a) What "outside the window" actually is
-----------------------------------------
Decomposition of v2 against the orthogonal pieces

  S1 = state V (carried 2D)         S3 = A_w residual after S1+S2
  S2 = sketch tail beyond V         S4 = A_{w+1} residual after S1+S2+S3

shows on 7 of 8 matrix families:

  matrix                  S1    S2 (sk_tail)   S3 (A_w)   S4 (A_{w+1})
  mixed-tail-sharp        0.83  0.0004         0.17       0.0000
  mixed-tail-balanced     0.81  0.0003         0.18       0.0000
  mixed-tail-soft         0.82  0.0004         0.18       0.0000
  diffuse-diffuse         0.83  0.0003         0.17       0.0000
  static-cex              0.82  0.0002         0.17       0.0000
  residual-spiky-shocks   0.83  0.0002         0.17       0.0000
  risk-residual-panel     0.69  0.0004         0.30       0.0001
  etf-basket-basis        0.99  0.0000         0.01       0.0000

The streaming v2 has essentially ZERO mass in S4. It cannot: the optimizer's
search basis Qz comes from Krylov-style residual expansions on M_gain (line
3549) and never includes A_{w+1} content. By construction the future-HM score
HM(||A_w v||^2, ||A_{w+1} v||^2) at v2 is forced to ~0 (because b ≈ 0).

The in-window optimum partitions the opposite way — ~0.50 in S3, ~0.50 in S4,
~0 in S1. So "outside the window" is precisely "inside the carried 2D state
V", not "in some random orthogonal complement".

Three concrete facts:

1. v_carry is at a local optimum. Tangent-gradient norms are at the 1e-4 to
   1e-3 level (block 3 is an exception at 0.44, still small relative to the
   inwindow 2.21 at the same block).

2. v_carry is also the GLOBAL maximum within reach. Random restarts started
   from random unit vectors and run on the full n-dim sphere with full
   gradient ascent (combined-score, orthogonal to v1) converge to scores that
   match v_carry's score to within 0.5%. The streaming optimizer's reduced
   basis Qz is not artificially limiting it — even given the full sphere,
   ascent finds the same maximum.

3. v_inwindow is NOT a local optimum of combined-score. Its tangent-gradient
   norm is 1.5–2.6 (vs ~1e-3 at v_carry). Gradient ascent started from
   v_inwindow on the FULL sphere converges to score ≈ v_carry's score, with
   alignment with v_carry rising over blocks (0.28 → 0.78). Started from
   v_inwindow_proj (v_inwindow projected onto M_gain rowspace, removing the
   S4 component), ascent converges to the same v_carry-level score.

In other words: the in-window future-HM peak is not a competing local maximum
of combined-score. It is at the BOTTOM of a valley.

The arc walk
------------
A great-circle walk from v_carry (t=0) to v_inwindow (t≈π/2) and onward to
-v_carry (t=π), parameterized as v(t) = cos(t)e_1 + sin(t)e_2 with e_1, e_2
an orthonormal frame in the plane of {v_carry, v_inwindow} ∩ v1^⊥, gives:

  t        score (block 2)   gain2     phi
  0.000    3.292             0.824     3.99
  0.314    2.956             0.785     3.77
  0.628    2.276             0.682     3.34
  0.942    1.685             0.554     3.04
  1.257    1.364             0.451     3.02
  1.571    1.306             0.412     3.17
  1.885    1.389             0.451     3.08
  2.513    2.291             0.682     3.36
  3.142    3.292             0.824     3.99

Both gain2 and phi are LOWER at the in-window direction than at the carried
direction. There is no barrier between them; the score descends monotonically
from v_carry to v_inwindow. The carried direction is a peak; the in-window
direction is a valley floor.


Concrete mechanism: where the score advantage comes from
--------------------------------------------------------
combined-score = gain2 * phi where:
  gain2(v) = ||M_gain v||^2  with M_gain = [B_top; A_w]
  phi(v)   = exp(c * (log y4_4 - 2 log y2_sq))   on pooled (A_w + memory) response
            and c = log(rows_seen/rows_ref) / (2 log rows_entropy) < 0
            so phi is increasing in entropy of the pooled response

At block t, B_top = state_(t-1)["s"][:, None] * state_(t-1)["V"].T. Concretely:

  ||M_gain @ v_carry||^2 = ||B_top @ state_(t-1)["V"][:,1]||^2 + ||A_w @ v_carry||^2
                         = state_(t-1)["s"][1]^2 + ||A_w v_carry||^2
                         ≈ 0.41 + 0.41 = 0.82 (consistently across blocks)

  ||M_gain @ v_inwindow||^2 = ||B_top @ v_inwindow||^2 + ||A_w @ v_inwindow||^2
                            ≈ 0 + 0.42 = 0.42      (B_top contribution is zero
                              because v_inwindow ⊥ state["V"])

So gain2 at v_carry is ≈ 2x gain2 at v_inwindow. The factor of two is exactly
the sketch contribution that v_inwindow forfeits by being orthogonal to S1.

  ||M_gain @ v_inwindow_proj||^2 ≈ 0.82  (recovers gain2 by reaching back into S1+S3)

So once v_inwindow is projected onto M_gain rowspace, gain2 matches v_carry.
The remaining edge of v_carry over v_inwindow_proj is in phi: at block 2,
phi(v_carry) = 3.99 vs phi(v_inwindow_proj) = 3.17. v_carry has higher
entropy of its pooled (A_w + old-memory) response than v_inwindow_proj does.

Why higher entropy at v_carry? v_inwindow_proj is the v1-orthogonal direction
that maximizes HM(||A_w v||^2, ||A_{w+1} v||^2) inside the search space. By
forcing balance with A_{w+1}, it concentrates response on a small set of A_w
rows that happen to also activate A_{w+1} rows. That concentration depresses
entropy of A_w response. v_carry has no such constraint and is more diffuse
on A_w, giving higher H and therefore higher phi.


Why the streaming v2 wins long-horizon
--------------------------------------
The combined-score objective is global-optimal at v_carry. The phi factor
specifically rewards diffuse response on A_w + memory, and this diffuseness
is what makes v_carry track the global second singular direction over many
blocks. Two concrete observations:

1. The carried 2D state forms a recursion where each block's state["V"] is
   the right-singular-vector frame of M_gain restricted to the previously
   chosen subspace (`make_state` -> `left_projected_operator_svd_factors`,
   line 2349). Even though state["s"] does not grow unboundedly with block
   count (it is recomputed each block from a rank-2 sketch + new A_cur, and
   the rank-2 SVD reframe redistributes energy), the DIRECTIONS in state["V"]
   evolve toward the principal directions of integrated row data because the
   reframe is a power-iteration-like step on the carried subspace.

2. v_inwindow is the per-pair greedy peak of HM × relH on a single 64-row
   pair with no carry. Per-block alignment with V_exact[:,1] is at noise
   floor for both choices on most matrices (~1e-3 to 1e-2), but the carry
   composes across blocks while the per-pair peak is independent each
   block. By the final block of a long run the carry has integrated enough
   evidence to be globally aligned (final mean_align of 0.71 reported in
   the future_hmean_vs_window_comparison_synthesis), while a sequence of
   per-block in-window peaks would not.

etf-basket-basis is the lone exception: a single block already contains
strong global signal, and the in-window optimum aligns at 0.68 with
V_exact[:,1] in one block. Consistent with this, etf is the only matrix
where streaming v2 already lives in S1+S3 (0.99 in S1, future-HM ratio
only 1.56x).


What this rules out
-------------------
- "v2 is stuck in a local maximum gradient ascent cannot escape" — false.
  Gradient ascent on the full sphere converges to the same score from
  v_inwindow, v_inwindow_proj, and random restarts. v_carry is the global
  max of combined-score, not a trap.

- "Expand the inner search basis to include A_{w+1} content" — would
  reach v_inwindow under the future-HM objective but would not change the
  combined-score landscape, where v_inwindow is at a valley floor and
  ascent flows away from it.

- "Tune line search or expansion tolerances to escape" — the optimizer is
  already converging to the global max of its objective. The convergence is
  not the bottleneck; the objective is.


Practical consequence
---------------------
The future-HM advantage of in-window directions is real but on a different
objective than what controls long-horizon retention. The current
`half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment` policy that uses future-HM as a
re-ranker over a small candidate pool is correctly using it as a
discriminator, not a re-optimization target.

If we want to import any of the in-window-balance benefit into the inner
loop, the right move is to mix it into the combined objective:

  score_modified = gain2 * phi * future_hm_factor(v; A_w, A_{w+1})

with a small coefficient that does not flip the v_carry → v_inwindow valley
into a peak unless the per-block evidence is unusually strong (etf-basket
regime). The current synthesis predicts such a modification will help on
etf-basket-basis and cost on the other 7 matrices unless the coefficient is
adapted per-block.
