Multi-matrix benchmark — combined / hybrid / iSVD / future_hmean_online
=======================================================================

Date: 2026-04-23 (re-run after seeding+patience refactor)


Scope
-----
Same 4-way benchmark at half_win=32 (pair=64 sliding) on all 17 matrix
families from `probe.generate_matrix_input`, same harness as the
previous pass:

  - same M_gain construction (state = diag(s)·V_prev.T stacked on A_half1)
  - same rank=2, float32, --shuffle-rows, seed=0, row_shuffle_seed=0
  - same metrics (sliding mode only; split_probe ignored in tables)
  - 16 matrices in 4-way parallel batches with OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2;
    `mixed-tail-sharp` solo. Driver: `run_bench_sweep_v2.sh`.
  - Previous outputs preserved in `summary/bench_matrix_sweep_old/`.

Policies (unchanged):
  - `isvd`                  pure streaming SVD of M_gain
  - `hybrid`                combined-greedy v1 + deflated-SVD v2
  - `combined`              combined-greedy v1 + opt2 (both from greedy)
  - `future_hmean_online`   greedy v1 + HM-online scored v2 from online
                            candidate pool, with rank-2 SVD reframe.

Raw artifacts: `summary/bench_matrix_sweep/<matrix>_win64.{json,csv,txt,log}`
for the 16 batched matrices, plus `summary/benchmark_online_vs_baselines_win64.json`
for `mixed-tail-sharp`. Full per-metric table in
`summary/bench_matrix_sweep/aggregated_table.txt`.


Changes since the previous synthesis
------------------------------------
Three changes inside the greedy/optimizer path used by combined, hybrid
and future_hmean_online (iSVD untouched):

  1) Patience-based early stop in the cex single-slot ascent
     (`basic_projected_ascent_single_reduced_{,streaming_}forget_cex`):
     break after `patience=5` consecutive iters with relative score
     change ≤ `patience_rel_tol=1e-5`. Existing single-iter absolute
     stops (1e-12 / 1e-10) almost never fired in float32 so the ascent
     used to run to maxit=120 every time; with patience it typically
     stops in 10-30 iters once converged.

  2) Two new explicit restart seeds in the cex greedy branch of
     `entropy_iter_basis_forget`, injected before
     `make_basic_restart_seeds`:
       - clean `V_init[:, k_idx]` (right svec of the row-normalized new
         half-block; previously only used inside an alpha-blend with
         random noise, never alpha=1)
       - `prev_basis[:, k_idx]` = `state_prev["V"][:, k_idx]` (the
         previous block's polished V_carried; available in scope but
         the cex branch never seeded with it — only legacy did).

  3) Default `--num-restarts` lowered from 8 → 3 in
     `half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py`. With the two explicit
     seeds plus z_warm in steady state, that fills the budget exactly
     and the SVD-of-M_gain inside `make_basic_restart_seeds` is skipped
     on every expansion after the first per slot.

Net effect on the slow-path policies: ~25-30× faster wall-clock vs the
previous synthesis, with no quality regression and small quality gains
on the tail-dominant matrices the online policy was designed for.

Matrix categorisation
---------------------

   mean_exact_cos1; small differences on the second cosine.

     crowded-strategy, execution-cost-slippage, futures-term-structure,
     intraday-liquidity-shape, macro-factor-panel, options-vol-surface,
     rates-cross-currency, realized-vol-corr, stat-arb-spreads

   on speed. The online variant pays ~3s instead of ~0.3s with no
   quality benefit on these.

Quality-per-second tradeoff (sliding, within-matrix ratios):

  - On tail-dominant matrices the online variant still delivers
    substantially better subspace quality (final_ex2 0.83-0.96 vs iSVD
    0.06-0.49) at ~10× the iSVD wall-clock, but absolute online cost
    is now 3-5s/matrix instead of 80-110s — well inside hot-path
    budgets for many use cases that used to need iSVD.
  - On the 9 well-conditioned matrices the methods tie on quality;
    iSVD's ~9× edge still makes it the clear pick when latency matters.
  - On the 2 spiky-residual matrices iSVD wins on both quality AND
    speed; the online variant remains a wrong tool.


Summary
-------
- Quality picture is essentially unchanged from the previous synthesis:
  online dominates the 6 tail-dominant matrices, ties on the 9 well-
  conditioned ones, and underperforms on the 2 spiky-residual ones.
  Within-regime improvements on tail-dominant matrices are real but
  modest (notably mixed-tail-sharp final_ex2 0.601 → 0.850).

- Wall-clock is ~25-30× lower per matrix for the three slow-path
  policies, driven by (a) cutting num_restarts 8→3 with explicit
  V_init + prev_basis seeds that fill the slot, eliminating the
  per-expansion `np.linalg.svd(M_gain)` inside make_basic_restart_seeds,
  and (b) a patience-bounded early stop in the cex ascent.

- The headline iSVD-vs-online median ratio dropped from 21.4× to 8.9×
  (algorithmic part) — the online policy is now in the same order of
  magnitude as iSVD on per-matrix wall-clock, while keeping its
  quality advantage on tail-dominant inputs.

- Spiky-residual remains the open structural problem; a row-
  concentration guard in the candidate pool or scoring is still the
  most promising direction to close those two cases without losing the
  tail-dominant wins.
