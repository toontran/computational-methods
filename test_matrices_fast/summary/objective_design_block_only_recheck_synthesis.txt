Objective-design block-only score recheck
=========================================

Date: 2026-04-23

Clarification being addressed
-----------------------------
The previous diagnostic language used `gain2` too loosely. In several existing
diagnostics, after block 1:

  M_gain = [carried sketch rows; current raw block]

and `combined_score_component_details(M_gain, A_block, ...)` computes:

  gain2 = ||M_gain v||^2

That is not the quantity we now want for the objective-design check.

Corrected diagnostic:

  block_gain2(v) = ||A_block v||^2

while entropy may still use row sampling / old row memory:

  pooled entropy rows = [A_block v; old_row_memory v]

So the corrected score for diagnostics is:

  block_only_score(v) = ||A_block v||^2 * phi_pooled(v)

where `phi_pooled` is the same combined entropy multiplier computed from the
current block plus sampled old rows.


Code added
----------
Added:

  objective_design_block_only_probe.py

The probe keeps the optimizer trajectory unchanged, then rescoring candidate
directions under block-only gain:

  probe.combined_score_component_details(
      A_block,
      A_block,
      v,
      rows_ref,
      state_prev=state,
      old_row_memory=old_row_memory,
  )

Because `M_gain` is replaced by `A_block`, the reported `gain2` is exactly
`||A_block v||^2`, while `old_row_memory` still participates in entropy.

Important semantic correction
-----------------------------
Prior claims of the form:

  "q2 wins gain2 in 32/32 blocks"

are invalid if the source was a streaming `actual_B` context where `gain2` was
computed from stacked `M_gain`. Those claims were about sketch-inclusive gain,
not raw current-block gain.

Still valid from earlier reports:

  - first-block score/gain observations, because M_gain == A_block;
  - entropy observations: phi, pooled_H, pooled_rel_H;
  - alignment / tail-mass / row-space geometry;
  - path dependence and previous-slot survival;
  - actual_B vs zero_B evidence as evidence about carried-sketch bias, not as
    evidence about raw block energy.

Readout:

  1. The previous exact `32/32` claim survives only for diffuse-diffuse and
     residual-spiky-shocks under block-only gain. It does not survive for
     static-cex or mixed-tail-sharp.

n=256 caveat
------------

The block-only empirical agent ran a smaller n=256 check and found weaker q2
competitiveness, especially for mixed-tail-sharp. My local script at n=256
showed matrix-dependent behavior:

  matrix                  q2 score wins   q2 gain wins
  static-cex              5/8             6/8
  diffuse-diffuse         7/8             8/8
  mixed-tail-sharp        2/8             3/8
  residual-spiky-shocks   7/8             8/8

This means the block-only conclusion is sensitive to scale, window count,
candidate construction, and possibly row-shuffle details. We should stop using
a single small-n result as the objective-design arbiter.


What changes in the earlier conclusions
---------------------------------------

1. "Current-block gain is the strongest separator"

  Revised:

    Current-block gain is still a necessary diagnostic and often exposes q2
    support hidden by full sketch-inclusive score, but it is not automatically
    an oracle selector. It also promotes high raw-block SVD complements.

2. "q2_vs_q1oracle wins gain in every block"

  Revised:

    Only true in some matrices under corrected block-only semantics. At n=1024
    it was true for diffuse-diffuse and residual-spiky-shocks, nearly true for
    mixed-tail-sharp, and not true for static-cex.

3. "The fake direction is mainly exposed by block energy"

  Revised:

    Block energy exposes that opt2 can be weak on fresh rows, but the strongest
    fresh-row direction may be `svd_complement`, not oracle q2. Therefore, an
    objective cannot simply maximize block-only gain.

4. "Entropy makes q2 stand out"

  Revised:

    Entropy is mixed. q2 often has lower phi than opt2 in the n=1024 run:

      static-cex:            mean opt2 phi=1.756828, q2 phi=1.720958
      diffuse-diffuse:       mean opt2 phi=1.754521, q2 phi=1.592649
      mixed-tail-sharp:      mean opt2 phi=1.747229, q2 phi=1.607641
      residual-spiky-shocks: mean opt2 phi=1.754391, q2 phi=1.392950

    q2 wins mainly because its raw current-block gain can be much larger, not
    because pooled entropy always favors it.

5. "Sketch-inclusive score is biased"

  Still true:

    In the n=1024 recheck, the full sketch-inclusive q2/opt2 score ratio is
    below 1.0 on average for every matrix, even when block-only q2/opt2 gain is
    well above 1.0. That is direct evidence that carried/sketch-inclusive score
    changes the ordering.


Updated answers to the relevant questions
-----------------------------------------

Score overestimation:

  Recheck using block-only score says the overestimation story should be split
  into two parts:

    - carried-sketch overestimation: opt2 can look competitive under full score
      even when current-block gain is weak;
    - block-only competition: q2 often beats opt2 on raw fresh rows, but raw
      block gain can also favor unrelated SVD-complement directions.

Score decay:

  Still not a reliable separator. Under block-only score, late-stream phi
  approaches 1.0 as rows_seen reaches rows_ref, so score and gain become the
  same in the final block. The time series is dominated by changing block
  geometry and the diminishing entropy multiplier.

Rank/order:

  Still useful, but now rank should be tracked twice:

    rank_by_full_score
    rank_by_block_only_score
    rank_by_block_only_gain

  A direction that rises by block-only gain but falls by full score is exactly
  the carried-memory conflict we need to study.

Oracle-vs-fake block comparison:

  Must use `||A_block v||^2`, not the previous `actual_B gain2`.
  q2_vs_q1oracle often beats opt2 at n=1024, but not uniformly across sizes or
  matrices. Also compare against `svd_complement`; otherwise we may confuse
  "fresh row support" with "oracle support."

Tail-origin:

  Still relevant. The alignment/tail-mass evidence is unchanged by the scoring
  correction. What changes is interpretation: a tail-heavy opt2 can have weak
  current-block gain while still surviving in the full sketch score.

Row-space mismatch:

  Still relevant. The corrected score makes the seen/current mismatch sharper:
  opt2 may be supported by historical/sketch rows, while q2 or another
  complement is supported by the current raw block.

Candidate mixture / memory:

  Still points toward an innovation memory, but with an important constraint:
  the innovation memory must not blindly promote the top block-only SVD
  complement. It needs oracle-consistency / repeated-support / anti-spike
  checks.

Objective-design implication
----------------------------

The next objective should not maximize block-only score alone. That would chase
raw current-block SVD complements. But the objective also should not trust the
current full sketch-inclusive score alone, because that can preserve a stale
tail/historical second slot.

The useful signal is a conflict diagnostic:

  q2-like / oracle-consistent direction:
    high repeated block-only gain
    reasonable entropy
    improving rank by block-only score
    stable alignment across randomized blocks
    not merely top current-block SVD complement

  fake/historical tail direction:
    competitive full score
    weak current-block gain
    high seen-row/sketch alignment
    persistent tail mass

  block-local decoy:
    very high block-only gain
    low repeated support
    weak oracle alignment
    high row concentration or unstable across blocks

The next diagnostic should explicitly classify directions into these three
cases before changing the objective.
