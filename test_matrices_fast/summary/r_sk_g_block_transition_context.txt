1. Where we are
---------------

S4 is implemented in `r_sk_g_score.py`:

    score(v) = HM(r_sk, raw_g1, raw_g2) · relH1(A_cur v)
        with score := 0 (and grad := 0) whenever r_sk ≤ 0.

    HM(a, b, c) = 3 / (1/a + 1/b + 1/c)        (unweighted 3-arg HM)
    r_sk(v)    = 1 - c_sk · ||A_sketch v||²
    A_sketch   = state["s"][:, None] * state["V"].T  (rank-r compression
                                                       of the past)
    c_sk       = N_sk_total_rows / ||A_sk_total||_F²   (normalization
                                                        from FULL past)

A block-1 fall-through has been added: when `A_sk is None`, the score
collapses to `HM2(raw_g1, raw_g2) · relH1` (matches `combined`'s slot-2
score on the first block). FD-checked at rel ≈ 3e-10.

S4 is wired into `half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py` as policy
`future_hmean_r_sk_g`. Streaming bench output:
  summary/bench_matrix_sweep_r_sk_g/{static-cex, mixed-tail-sharp,
                                     diffuse-diffuse}_win64.{txt,csv,json}

Diagnostic outputs:
  summary/r_sk_g_score{,_streaming_init}.{csv,json,txt}
  summary/r_sk_g_oracle_init_probe{,_b1,_b2}.{txt,json}


2. Diagnosis (the actual failure mode)
---------------------------------------

S4's per-block ranking acceptance passes (oracle_v2_proj outranks
hm_triplet_raw on score_S4 on ≥1 block per matrix), but streaming
slot-2 alignment is poor: `final_exact_cos² ≈ 0` on static-cex and
mixed-tail-sharp; only diffuse-diffuse gets traction.

Two structural problems compound:

A. **The score has the wrong global maximum.** HM3 is a 66-D → 3-scalar
   reduction; its level sets are 62-dim manifolds in the union row-space.
   The actual maximum sits on a *plateau* at `(1/r_sk, 1/r_g1, 1/r_g2)`
   ≈ `(1.0, 2.4, 2.4)`, `hm3 ≈ 0.52`. **Many** directions reach this
   plateau; the slot-2 oracle is *not* one of them. Confirmed empirically
   by:
     - the rank-2 deflated diagnostic (v1_full / v2_deflate both at
       hm3 = 0.52, deflation costs zero score);
     - oracle-init ascent (initializing at oracle_v_proj and running
       gradient ascent: every non-clipped run climbs *away* from the
       oracle to the plateau, with cos²(start, end) typically 0.1–0.5).

   The *content* of the plateau direction on counterexample matrices is
   a single dominant row of `A_fut` — `‖A_fut v‖²` is maximized over
   the unit sphere by the top right singular vector of `A_fut`, which
   on sharp-tailed matrices is dominated by ONE row (e.g. static-cex
   block 12: row#21 of A_fut absorbs `cos² = 0.518` of the v_outside
   mass; mixed-tail-sharp b31: row#12 absorbs 0.636). Diffuse matrices
   don't have this dominant-row, which is why diffuse-diffuse is the
   only one of the three where S4 streaming partially works.

B. **The block-1 → block-2 transition is a discontinuous jump in the
   score function.** At block 1 the fall-through gives HM2 (no r_sk
   term), and S4's rank-2 frame captures `oracle_v2_proj` well on
   static-cex (sub_v2pr = 0.767) and diffuse-diffuse (0.813). At block
   2 the `1/r_sk` term enters the HM denominator and immediately drives
   the optimizer toward `r_sk → 1` (sketch-orthogonal). On the SAME
   snapshots `oracle_v2_proj` has `r_sk = 0.83` (static-cex), `0.83`
   (mixed-tail-sharp), `0.98` (diffuse-diffuse) — i.e. it is NOT
   sketch-orthogonal. The gradient explicitly points away from the
   oracle.

   sub_v2pr trajectory (S4, streaming-init only):
     static-cex:        0.767 → 0.528 → 0.226 → 0.144 → 0.009  (b1..b31)
     diffuse-diffuse:   0.813 → 0.250 → 0.899 → 0.812 → 0.243
     mixed-tail-sharp:  0.061 → 0.080 → 0.042 → 0.164 → 0.002

   For comparison, the existing combined optimizer:
     static-cex:        0.206 → 0.438 → 0.770 → 0.889 → 0.969
     diffuse-diffuse:   0.016 → 0.017 → 0.009 → 0.010 → 0.220
     mixed-tail-sharp:  0.201 → 0.423 → 0.492 → 0.865 → 0.966

   So combined dominates on structured/counterexample matrices from b6
   onward; S4 is competitive only on diffuse-diffuse, and ONLY when its
   block-1 (HM2) signal is what's driving things.


3. Why r_sk specifically does the damage
-----------------------------------------

`A_sketch` after block 1 is the rank-`r` SVD compression of all
previously seen rows: `B_top = state["s"][:, None] * state["V"].T`,
shape `(r, n)` with `r = rank = 2`. So `raw_sk(v) = ||B_top v||²` is
v's energy along the rank-2 carry.

But `c_sk = N_sk_total_rows / ||A_sk_total||_F²` is computed from the
FULL past data (`A[:sk_end]`). That is a normalization for "typical
row energy of past rows" — a number that has nothing to do with what
the rank-2 compression carries. So:

  r_sk(v) = 1 - c_sk · ||B_top v||²
          = 1 - (N_full / ||A_full||²_F) · ||B_top v||²

The `r_sk = 0` setpoint corresponds to v capturing *typical full-past
row energy* through the rank-2 compression. This is impossible for any
v not aligned with the carried subspace — most full-past energy is
discarded by the rank-2 SVD by construction. So `r_sk → 1` for almost
every v outside the carry, and the optimizer trivially achieves it.

In short: `c_sk` is calibrated for the FULL past, but `raw_sk` measures
energy through the COMPRESSED past. The two are mismatched.


4. Two fix proposals
--------------------

F1. **c_sk on compressed sketch.** Replace
        c_sk = N_sk_total_rows / ||A_sk_total||_F²
    with
        c_sk = rank / ||B_top||_F²
    so that `r_sk = 0` corresponds to v capturing typical *carried*
    row energy. This aligns the normalization with what `raw_sk`
    actually measures. Pros: small, mechanical change; preserves the
    "shortfall against the carry" interpretation. Cons: changes the
    meaning of the score (vs the original spec); needs FD recheck and
    a fresh streaming bench.

F2. **Warm-up window.** Use the HM2 (block-1) fall-through for the
    first K blocks, then switch to HM3. Switching trigger: e.g.
    `N_sk_full >= K * half_win` for some K (suggest K = 2 or 4).
    Pros: zero analytical risk, just delays HM3 until the rank-r
    sketch has enough underlying data. Cons: the streaming benchmark
    is mostly post-warmup blocks anyway; doesn't help on long runs
    if HM3 is fundamentally wrong (and §10 of the redesign doc says
    it might be).

Try **F1 first**: it directly addresses the diagnosed mismatch and is
cheap to test. If F1 still fails, F2 is a useful baseline (and can
stack with F1).

If both fail to bring static-cex / mixed-tail-sharp `final_exact_cos²`
above ~0.5, the §10 prescription stands: drop scalar-per-v scoring
and move to a Stiefel-rank-2 joint score where `raw_g(V) =
||A_fut V||_F²` for a 2-frame V — that breaks the angular degeneracy
and the row-of-A_fut cheat in one move.


5. How to test (in order)
--------------------------

For each candidate fix, run the same gauntlet:

T1. **FD check.** Run `python r_sk_g_score.py --gradient-check
    --matrix mixed-tail-sharp --blocks 1 2 6 12 --dtype float64`.
    Acceptance: rel err < 1e-7 on each variant at each block (S4
    matters most; ignore S1/S2/S3 unless the code path is shared).

T2. **Per-block oracle alignment, streaming-init.** Run
        python r_sk_g_score.py \
          --matrices static-cex mixed-tail-sharp diffuse-diffuse \
          --blocks 1 2 6 12 31 --no-oracle-warmstart \
          --out-prefix summary/<descriptive_name>
    Inspect the rank-2 sub_v_pr metric (col 1 + col 2 squared cosines
    against oracle_v_pr). Acceptance: `sub_v2pr ≥ 0.4` on static-cex
    at blocks 6+ and at b31; same on mixed-tail-sharp.

T3. **Streaming benchmark.** Run
        export OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2 OMP_NUM_THREADS=2 MKL_NUM_THREADS=2
        for M in static-cex mixed-tail-sharp diffuse-diffuse; do
          python half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py \
            --matrix "$M" \
            --policies combined hybrid isvd future_hmean_online \
                       future_hmean_evidence future_hmean_r_sk_g \
            --half-win 32 --rsk-variant S4 \
            --json-out summary/<dir>/${M}_win64.json \
            --csv-out  summary/<dir>/${M}_win64.csv \
            --text-out summary/<dir>/${M}_win64.txt \
            > summary/<dir>/${M}_win64.log 2>&1 &
        done; wait
    Acceptance for S4: sliding-mode `final_exact_cos[1]² ≥ 0.6` on
    static-cex and mixed-tail-sharp, `≥ 0.7` on diffuse-diffuse,
    wall-clock budget ≤ 200 s per matrix (with all 6 policies).

T4. **Oracle-init landscape probe.** Run
        python r_sk_g_oracle_init_probe.py \
          --matrices static-cex mixed-tail-sharp diffuse-diffuse \
          --blocks 1 2 6 12 31 \
          --out-prefix summary/<dir>/oracle_init_probe
    Two diagnostic checks:
      a) Score gain Δ from oracle_v_proj should be small (oracle is
         close to a critical point).
      b) `cos²(start, end) ≥ 0.7` (the optimizer doesn't run away).
    If both hold across blocks 2+ on ≥2 matrices, the score is no
    longer a row-of-A_fut magnet.

8. Open questions
------------------

Q1. Does F1 introduce a new failure mode at block 1 (where there's no
    sketch and the fall-through bypasses c_sk entirely)? Probably no —
    the block-1 branch should remain as-is. But verify by running
    T2/T3 with --blocks 1 explicitly and checking it matches the
    current (post-fix) block-1 numbers (sub_v2pr ≈ 0.81 on diffuse,
    0.77 on static-cex, 0.06 on mixed-tail-sharp).

Q2. Does the row-of-A_fut cheat survive F1? Run T4 after F1 — if the
    `cos²(SVD_fut #0)` of v_outside is still ≥ 0.4 on static-cex /
    mixed-tail-sharp, the cheat persists and we need either F2 on top,
    or the §10 Stiefel rank-2 score.

Q3. Mixed-tail-sharp at block 1: S4 already does badly (sub_v2pr =
    0.061) even though the score is HM2 there. Why? Likely because
    the oracle's energy at block 1 is concentrated in just 32 rows and
    the scoring landscape in HM2 already has the row-of-A_fut cheat
    on this counterexample matrix. Worth checking if a HM2-with-spread
    penalty (e.g. `||A_fut v||² - max_i (A_fut[i] · v)²`) helps even
    at block 1. If it does, the same modification on HM3 might be the
    real fix — not c_sk renormalization.

Q4. The plateau height ~0.52 is suspiciously close to the upper bound
    of HM3 when r_sk = 1 and raw_g1 = raw_g2. Specifically, with
    raw_g1 = raw_g2 = 0.42, HM3 = 3 / (1 + 1/0.42 + 1/0.42) ≈ 0.53.
    So the plateau IS the analytical maximum modulo the row-of-A_fut
    structure. Any fix that doesn't change the score's analytic form
    (i.e. F2) will not lower the plateau — it will just delay reaching
    it. F1 lowers `c_sk` (since `||B_top||_F² < ||A_sk||_F²`), which
    raises `1 - c_sk · raw_sk = r_sk` for the same v — but in the
    direction of LARGER r_sk, which probably makes the cheat stronger,
    not weaker. THIS NEEDS CAREFUL THOUGHT BEFORE TESTING F1.

    Worked sketch (revisit before implementing F1):
      Old: c_sk = N_full / ||A_full||² = ~1.21 in observed data.
      New: c_sk = r / ||B_top||² = 2 / ||B_top||²_F.
      For static-cex b2: ||B_top||²_F ≈ trace(s²) where s are top-2
      singvals of past 32 rows. If s_1 ≈ 1, s_2 ≈ 0.5, then ||B_top||²_F
      ≈ 1.25, so new c_sk ≈ 1.6 — LARGER than old. So r_sk would be
      smaller (less shortfall) and HM3's `1/r_sk` term would be even
      MORE dominant. This argues F1 might make things WORSE, not
      better. Sanity-check this with a printout in a notebook before
      committing to F1.

    A safer reformulation: replace r_sk with r_sk_relative =
      1 - ||B_top v||² / ||A_full v projected onto past||²
    which measures shortfall *within* the achievable past energy.
    But this needs derivation and FD verification.


9. Why this might still fail (echoing §10)
-------------------------------------------

The structural diagnosis (point 2A above) — that HM3 has a 62-dim
plateau and the oracle is one specific point inside or below it —
is independent of any specific c_sk choice. No scalar-per-v score
that depends only on (raw_sk, raw_g1, raw_g2) can break this
degeneracy: those three numbers don't pin down the direction.

If F1, F2, and the spread-penalty variant all fail T2/T3 acceptance,
DO NOT keep iterating on per-v scalar scores. Move to the rank-2
Stiefel score:

    score(V) = HM(r_sk_subspace(V), ||A_cur V||²_F, ||A_fut V||²_F) · ...

where V is an n×2 orthonormal frame. This is invariant only under
SO(2) within-frame rotations, not under the 62-dim rotation that
fools HM3, and `||A_fut V||²_F` cannot be cheated by aligning with
a single row.

Implementation pointers for the Stiefel branch:
  - Optimizer: project gradient onto the Stiefel manifold (Cayley
    retraction or QR-based; scipy/pymanopt both work).
  - Warm starts: V_default[:, :2] (combined's frame), oracle frame
    V_exact[:, :2] projected onto union span.
  - Diagnostic: replace `align_v1_proj/align_v2_proj` with the
    principal-angle² between the candidate frame and span(oracle_v1_proj,
    oracle_v2_proj).
