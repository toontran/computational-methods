Score-design overview: streaming-SVD slot-2 (and rank-r) decision
==================================================================

Date: 2026-04-28
Audience: someone picking up the score-design line of work and wanting the
high-level picture before diving into f_hm3_score_implementation_context.txt
or hm_score_new_variant_test_context.txt.


1. What we're doing
-------------------

At each block in the streaming SVD we maintain a rank-r approximation of A's
leading right singular vectors. Per block we have:

  A_sketch = state.s · state.V^T     rank-r CARRY of A_seen (prefix)
  A_cur                               half-window of new rows
  A_fut                               peek half-window after that

The streaming bench commits a rank-r subspace each block; we measure terminal
cos²(V_carry, V_exact) at block 31 (final_exact_cos in the JSON output).

Slot-1 is essentially solved by combined-step: V_default[:, 0] is the leading
SV of M_gain = [B_top; A_cur] and is well-determined. The hard part is
slot-2 (and the rank-r extension to slot-3..r). The whole design effort is
about a value-only score(v) that, when maximized over the union span, picks
a good slot-2 vector.

Constraints on the score:
  - value-only computable (no V_exact)
  - not over-fit to A_cur (else slot-2 = A_cur top SV which double-covers slot-1)
  - not over-fit to A_fut (else "row-of-A_fut cheat" — doesn't generalize)
  - aware of the carry (else we discard past info)
  - ideally robust to spiky responses (a single row dominating ‖A_cur v‖²)

We've iterated S1 → S2 → S3 → S4 → S5 → S6 (current). Each variant is a
hypothesis about how to balance these forces. S6 is the current best on
clean-rowspace matrices but has known gaps (P4 plateau, no relH1 guard).

The implicit baseline to beat is NOT `combined`, it is `future_hmean_online`
— the production policy from summary/bench_matrix_sweep/synthesis.txt
(2026-04-23). Online uses a different mechanism (rank-2 SVD reframe over an
online candidate pool with HM-online scoring) and on the 17-matrix sweep at
half_win=32 it dominates the 6 tail-dominant matrices, ties on the 9 well-
conditioned ones, and underperforms on 2 spiky-residual ones. On the tail-
dominant matrices the gap S6 must close is large — see §6 for side-by-side.
The S6/r_sk_g line is the *score-design* alternative to online: a value-only,
analytically-differentiable, rank-r-liftable score, in contrast to online's
candidate-pool reframe. Whether the score-design path can match or beat
online on its strong matrices is the open question driving this line.

CAUTION (2026-04-28): the originally reported `future_hmean_online` numbers
in summary/bench_matrix_sweep/synthesis.txt are ORACLE-AWARE — its
candidate pool includes `q2_vs_q1oracle` built from V_exact[:,1] projected
into the row span (half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py:24,247-250,490

(see workflow §5 [INFRA-10]); the §6 table below is being repropagated

1bis. The original combined-score baseline — verified failure mechanisms
------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-27. Source: combined_obj_sketch_bias_synthesis.txt and
combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py.)

The S1..S6 family is the *replacement* for the original combined score
  score_w(v) = (‖Bv‖² + ‖A_w v‖²) · phi_w(v)
from reports/approximation/new_approx_combined.txt. Direct probing on
mixed-tail-sharp pins WHY combined's slot-2 fails, in two distinct
mechanisms. Useful as established context when motivating S6's choices.

(M1) phi explicitly de-rewards oracle-aligned slot-2 directions.
     Block-1 landscape in the deflation complement of combined_v_1, with
     gain_tot essentially flat (0.819–0.833) across all directions:
       direction              phi     score   align_o2
       combined_v_2          5.66    4.68    0.016
       oracle_v_2_proj_⊥     3.70    3.08    0.749
       random_0..4         3.0–3.9  2.5–3.2   < 0.04
     The entire score gap is phi. phi rewards entropic spread; V_exact[:,1]
     produces a CONCENTRATED response and therefore loses on phi.
     This persists at b1 with NO sketch, so it's not a sketch-reliance bug.

(M2) Once B exists, the rank-r carry pins slot 2 to span(V_state).
     state_align(combined_v_2) grows to 0.989 at b31; oracle_v_2_proj's
     state_align is 0.103. ‖Bv‖² only sees span(V_state); once
     sk_F2/cur_F2 ≈ 30 (b31), gain2 dominates and the score's
     complement-of-v_1 max is just the second retained singular vector.

(R1) Joint Stiefel-2 with the SAME combined score does NOT fix it.
     basis_selection="joint" at r=2 produces essentially identical V to
     greedy at b6/b12/b31 (state_align(joint_v_2)=0.989, identical
     alignments). At b1 it nudges algn_o2 to 0.247 but at lower score —
     it's a different stationary point, not a higher max.
     ⇒ The slot-2 failure is intrinsic to the SCORE on Stiefel(d, r),
     not to greedy deflation.

(M3) Combined is BLIND TO A_fut → over-fits to A_cur's local rowspace.
     This is the failure mechanism on diffuse-diffuse (and the original
     motivation for the future_hmean / S1..S6 family). Combined maximizes
     ‖Bv‖² + ‖A_cur v‖² with NO A_fut term, so at b1 (no carry yet) its
     slot-1 v is the top SV of A_cur — a single half-window's specific
     draw. On diffuse-diffuse the population rowspace is uniform, so
     A_cur's top SV is a noise-corrupted LOCAL direction, not the
     population top SV. The population top SV is the v that maximizes
     gain across BOTH halves, which is what HM2(u_g1, u_g2) recovers.

     Verified empirics (overview §6 b1 numbers, diffuse-diffuse):
       S6_v1 ≈ oracle_v1 cos² = 0.999 (HM2 on b1 = popSV optimizer)
       S6_v2 ≈ oracle_v2 cos² = 0.982
       Combined v1, v2 < 0.05 against either oracle column
     Streaming-bench (final cos1² on diffuse-diffuse, S6 synthesis §4):
       combined 0.030, iSVD 0.239, S6 0.005, online 0.528 (oracle-aware°).
     iSVD wins because its streaming SVD update integrates information
     across blocks, recovering the population direction. Combined has
     no such integrator and never sees A_fut.
     ° online numbers here are the oracle-aware ceiling (see §1
       CAUTION); value-only online from INFRA-10 will replace once
       it lands.

     Distinct from M1/M2: M1/M2 are slot-2 mechanisms on a matrix where
     combined's slot-1 *succeeds* (mixed-tail-sharp slot-1 cos0² = 0.785
     in the S6 bench). M3 is a slot-1 mechanism on a matrix where the
     rowspace is uniform (diffuse-diffuse slot-1 cos0² = 0.085).

Together M1, M2, M3 cover the three combined-baseline failure modes that
the score-design line was launched to address:
  - M3 (blind to A_fut)            → motivates including A_fut in score
                                     ⇒ the future_hmean family exists
  - M1 (phi penalty on concentrated)→ motivates removing entropic gating
                                     ⇒ HM-style balance instead of phi
  - M2 (carry pins slot-r)          → motivates rank-aware u_sk weighting
                                     ⇒ rank-r CARRY (sk_F2_low), Family-B
                                       carry-confidence multipliers,
                                       rank-r Stiefel lift
This is the design arc that lands at S6 = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) per §2.

Read §3 and §5 of this document with R1 in mind: "lifting to Stiefel
rank-r" means "go to higher r AND change the score to a Grassmannian-
invariant frame form". Joint optimization at the same r with the same
per-vector score is *not* the lift, and is verified not to help.

Implications consistent with §2's S6 design moves:
  - S6's HM3 BALANCE ENFORCER (§2a) targets a related-but-distinct
    pathology: combined's v has raw_g2 ≈ 0 (no future-window content)
    because combined doesn't see A_fut. M1/M2 explain WHY combined's v
    points where it does inside the past windows.
  - S6's removal of phi-style entropic gating (§2d removes S5's hard
    gate; the original combined uses phi multiplicatively) is the
    natural M1 fix — replacing the entropy-reward with HM3 balance
    means concentrated responses are no longer penalized.
  - S6's rank-r CARRY (§2c) addresses M2 partially by anchoring u_sk
    to a stationary scaling, but the rank-r carry STILL has rank-r
    information loss. Family-B carry-confidence multipliers (§7 Q5)
    and the rank-r Grassmannian lift (§5) are the structural fix.


2. Design intuitions for S6 (F-weighted HM3)
--------------------------------------------

  score(v) = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2)        sketch present
  score(v) = HM2(u_g1, u_g2)              block-1 fall-through

  u_sk = ‖A_sketch v‖² / sk_F2_low       sk_F2_low = ‖A_sketch‖_F² (rank-r CARRY)
  u_g1 = ‖A_cur v‖²    / cur_F2          cur_F2    = ‖A_cur‖_F²
  u_g2 = ‖A_fut v‖²    / fut_F2          fut_F2    = ‖A_fut‖_F²

Four design moves, each addressing a specific pathology:

(a) Harmonic mean as the BALANCE ENFORCER.
    HM3 is dominated by its smallest argument. To score high under HM3, v
    must keep ALL THREE u's bounded away from zero. This is exactly why
    combined fails: combined's v's have raw_g2 ≈ 1e-6 (essentially
    orthogonal to A_fut), so u_g2 ≈ 4e-8 and HM3 ≈ 1e-7. HM3 turns
    "balance" from a soft preference into a hard constraint.

(b) Frobenius weighting as the UNIT-FIXER.
    Raw responses ‖A_X v‖² live on incompatible scales (carry small,
    cur/fut ~26 in our setup, full-prefix sketches grow with block).
    Dividing by ‖A_X‖_F² puts every term on a [0, ~1] scale tied to the
    *fraction of available energy* v captures. Now HM3's three arguments
    are directly comparable.

(c) Rank-r CARRY (sk_F2_low), not full-prefix Frobenius (sk_F2_full).
    sk_F2_full grows linearly with block_id, so u_sk would shrink linearly
    — score becomes block-id sensitive in a perverse way. sk_F2_low is
    roughly stationary across blocks because the carry singular values
    track the matrix's true top-r. This makes u_sk ∈ [0, ~0.5] tightly,
    which is critical for rank-r generalization (the scaling stays sane
    as r grows).

(d) Smooth on the sphere wherever u's > 0 — no gate, no relH1.
    S5's hard gate (raw_sk > T → score, else 0) created a discontinuity
    that excluded the oracle from the reachable set on some blocks (P4 in
    the prior context). S6 removes the gate to make the oracle reachable
    everywhere it has positive responses. Cost: no protection against
    spiky responses (the row-of-A_fut cheat) — diagnostic confirms S6
    picks low-relH1 v's on mixed-tail-sharp. This is parked as F1, not
    fixed.


2bis. Design choice justifications and open gaps
-------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-28.) §2 lists four design moves. Each is locally
justified by a verified pathology, but the family-level choice (scalar-
per-v HM-form on F-weighted u's, equal weights) is not defended against
alternatives. The picture below tiers the moves and flags the silent
assumptions that the next iteration should test.

Well-justified (verified pathology + no plausible alternative):

  (c) Rank-r CARRY (sk_F2_low) over full-prefix sk_F2_full.
      sk_F2_full grows linearly with block_id → u_sk shrinks linearly →
      score becomes block-id-sensitive. sk_F2_low is stationary because
      it tracks the matrix's true top-r. Verified as P6 in the S6
      synthesis. No reasonable alternative.

  (d) Smooth on the sphere — no gate.
      S5's hard gate excluded the oracle from the reachable set on some
      blocks (P4 in the S5 context). Removing the gate fixes oracle-
      reachability everywhere u's > 0. Cost (no row-cheat protection on
      mixed-tail-sharp) acknowledged and parked as F1 / Q3.

Partially justified (right direction; alternatives within the direction
not compared):

  (a) Harmonic mean as the BALANCE ENFORCER.
      Justified OVER additive forms: HM's "smallest argument dominates"
      property is what fixes M1 (phi penalty) and M3 (combined blind to
      A_fut). But two sub-choices are silent:
        (a.i)  HM vs GM. Both go to zero when any argument is zero;
               HM is more aggressive at penalizing imbalance, GM is
               smoother. No ablation. GM = (u_sk · u_g1 · u_g2)^(1/3)
               also has clean log-additive gradient and may be a
               better optimization landscape (worth a single T2/T3
               run).
        (a.ii) Equal weights across u_sk, u_g1, u_g2. Why not weighted
               HM that down-weights u_sk early (carry immature) and
               up-weights it late (carry confident)? This is Q5
               (carry-confidence multipliers) — DEFERRED, not refuted.
      HM also creates the P4 plateau as a side effect: a 3-arg balance
      form has a wide high-score level set on the union span. §2 frames
      HM as a clean win; in fact §3's plateau IS the cost of (a). The
      score-ranking problem (oracle ranks high in T2) is solved at the
      cost of the optimization-landscape problem (T3 drift). This
      tension should be visible in §2.

  (b) Frobenius weighting as the UNIT-FIXER.
      Unit-incompatibility is real (raw norms span 3 orders of
      magnitude). F-weighting puts all u's on [0, ~1] tied to "fraction
      of available energy" — that's the right shape. But:
        (b.i)  F-norm vs operator norm. Op-norm weighting gives tighter
               [0,1] and may behave better on heavy-tailed spectra (e.g.
               static-cex). Not measured.
        (b.ii) F-norm vs per-row leverage weighting (Q4). §7 calls Q4
               "probably equivalent up to scale" but no comparison run.

Not justified, just inherited:

  - Block-1 fall-through to HM2 (overview §4 calls it "natural" and
    shows post-hoc equivalence to c-weighted HM-evi). Why HM2 instead
    of HM3 with a sensible u_sk prior (e.g. average of u_g1, u_g2)?
    Not derived; works empirically.
  - The HM-family hypothesis itself. §3 ("any scalar-per-v score has
    codim-1 level set") is a structural critique of the WHOLE family,
    not just S6. A rank-r Grassmannian-invariant score (§5) may not
    need an HM aggregator at all — trace forms are subspace functions
    by construction. The §5 lift uses HM3 by inheritance; whether HM3
    is the right aggregator at rank-r is open and tied to (a.i)/(a.ii).

Implications for the priority order in §7:
  - Q1 (rank-r lift) is robust to (a.i)/(a.ii)/(b.i)/(b.ii) only if the
    lift's plateau-shrinkage hypothesis holds. If it doesn't, the
    open ablations in (a.i), (a.ii), (b.i) become urgent.
  - Q5 (carry-confidence) IS (a.ii) under another name. Re-reading §7
    Q5 with this in mind: the question is not "should we add a
    multiplier?" but "is equal weighting the silent default we never
    questioned?" — the multiplier is one form of the answer.
  - HM-vs-GM ablation (a.i) is not in the §7 Q-list and probably
    should be; ~30 minutes of work given the existing harness.


3. The plateau is the deep problem
-----------------------------------

Any scalar-per-v score on the union sphere has a level set of codimension
1. With dim(B_union) ~64 that's a 62-dim plateau. The oracle sits on (or
near) the high-score level set but isn't *pinned* to it — the optimizer
routinely finds higher-score, non-oracle points.

Empirics (T2 on mixed-tail-sharp, b6, b12, b31): the S6 optimizer's vector
scores higher than oracle_v2_proj on score_S6 (Δ ≈ +1e-3 to +2e-2), but
its align_v2_proj is 0.04–0.25 — i.e., the optimizer found a different
high-score point.

This is the dominant reason S6 underperforms on the streaming bench
despite the score correctly ranking the oracle in T2. The fix isn't in
the score — it's in lifting to Stiefel rank-r so the level set has lower
dimension (see §5).


4. The block-1 fall-through and why it's natural
-------------------------------------------------

At block 1 there's no sketch yet, so we can't compute u_sk. The natural
fall-through is HM2(u_g1, u_g2). Up to a per-block constant (cur_F2 ≈
fut_F2), this is identical to c-weighted HM-evi at block 1, which is why
c_evi_v1 was the top of the rank table at block 1 in the diagnostic.
This is a feature, not a bug — at b1 the only signal is split-sample
balance between A_cur and A_fut, and HM2 is the right form for that.

Explicit equivalence (why oracle_v_proj LOSES to c_evi_v1 at b1):
  - At b1, c_sk = 0 → c-weighted HM-evi reduces to a 2-arg form over
    (raw_g1, raw_g2) with weights (c_g1², c_g2²).
  - c_g1 = half_win/cur_F2 ≈ c_g2 ≈ 1.21, so weighted HM ≈ unweighted
    HM(raw_g1, raw_g2) up to a multiplicative constant.
  - S6 at b1 reduces to HM(raw_g1/cur_F2, raw_g2/fut_F2). Since
    cur_F2 ≈ fut_F2 ≈ 26.4 (rows are unit-energy on every matrix here),
    that's the same objective as c-weighted HM-evi up to a per-block
    rescale.
  - c_evi_v1 is by construction the OPTIMIZER of that objective; the
    projected oracle is the top SV of the FULL matrix A restricted to
    rowspace(A_cur, A_fut), which is balanced (raw_g1 ≈ raw_g2) but not
    energy-maximal locally. So c_evi_v1 ≥ oracle on score_S6 at b1.
  - Empirically (mixed-tail-sharp b1):
        c_evi_v1: u_g1=0.01612, u_g2=0.01664 → HM2 = 0.01638
        oracle_v2_proj: u_g1=0.01578, u_g2=0.01583 → HM2 = 0.01580
    The (u_g1+u_g2)/2 gap is ~4%, which is HM's gap exactly because
    both candidates are already nearly balanced.


5. Generalization to rank-r (the immediate next step)
------------------------------------------------------

The score generalizes naturally:
  u_sk(V) = ‖A_sketch V‖_F² / ‖A_sketch‖_F²   for V ∈ St(n, r)
  u_g1(V) = ‖A_cur V‖_F²    / ‖A_cur‖_F²
  u_g2(V) = ‖A_fut V‖_F²    / ‖A_fut‖_F²

  score(V) = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2)

Now u_X(V) ∈ [0, r/r_max(A_X)]. Score is invariant under right
multiplication by O ∈ O(r), i.e. it's a SUBSPACE function on the
Grassmannian — which is exactly what we want.

The hard parts of the lift:
  (i)   Replace sphere-ascent with Stiefel-ascent. Tangent projection,
        retraction (QR or polar), line search.
  (ii)  Re-derive the gradient. Each column gets the same shape of grad
        we already have, but now combined into a matrix-valued grad with
        the Stiefel projection applied. FD check has to be lifted too.
  (iii) Plateau detector becomes a SUBSPACE metric: principal angles
        between span(V_opt) and span(V_oracle), not pairwise cos. The
        "score(opt) > score(oracle) but cos² ≈ 0" detector → "score(opt)
        > score(oracle) but max principal angle ≈ π/2".

Why the rank-r lift may quietly fix mixed-tail-sharp: at rank-2 there's a
single slot-2 degree of freedom on the sphere, and S6 has a flat HM3
surface in mixed-tail-sharp's union span — the optimizer drifts. With
r=3 or r=4 the plateau dimension shrinks and the optimum may pin closer
to the oracle without any score-design changes. Worth checking BEFORE
adding F1 (relH1) / F2 (soft gate) epicycles.

7. Open questions worth running before more score epicycles
------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. Does the rank-r lift pin the optimum near the oracle without F1/F2?
    Build the Stiefel-ascent variant first, run T3 with rank=3 or 4 on the
    same 7-matrix suite, see whether mixed-tail-sharp's cos1² climbs.

Q2. How wide is the plateau actually? Sample 200 random points on the
    sphere within the score_S6 ≥ 0.95 · score_S6(opt) sublevel set and
    plot align_v2_proj distribution. Will tell us whether "narrow basin"
    is the right framing or whether the basin is wide but multimodal.

Q3. relH1 added back at the END (multiplicative): does it tighten the
    basin without re-introducing S5's clipping? F1 in the spec.

Q4. Per-row/leverage-aware c-weighting instead of Frobenius F-weighting:
    F3 in the spec. Probably equivalent up to scale.

Q5. Carry-confidence multipliers (Family B in score_ideas_growing_sketch.txt).
    Multiply u_sk by s_top², spectral concentration, or carry-drift signals
    so sketch influence grows with carry maturity. NATURAL at rank-r:
    s_top² → geomean(s_1, …, s_r); spectral concentration → s_r²/Σs_i²;
    carry-drift → ‖V_t V_t^T − V_{t−1} V_{t−1}^T‖_F (principal-angle² form).
    Defer until rank-r is in place — these are more meaningful as frame-
    level multipliers than scalar.

Q6. Rank-aware threshold (Family E). u_sk = max(r·raw_sk/sk_F2_low − 1, 0)
    gates a candidate as "sketch-aligned" iff it captures more than the
    average rank-r direction's energy. This becomes naturally meaningful
    at rank-r (T_r = sk_F2_low/r is the average per-frame-direction
    energy). Sharper than S5's per-row gate.

Q7. HM vs GM aggregator ablation (NEW, from §2bis (a.i)).
    The "balance enforcer" property is shared by HM and GM — both go to
    zero when any u_X → 0 — but HM penalizes imbalance more aggressively.
    GM has a clean log-additive gradient (∇log GM = (1/k) Σ ∇log u_X)
    and may produce a smoother optimization landscape (narrower P4
    plateau). Ablation: implement S6_GM = (u_sk · u_g1 · u_g2)^(1/3),
    same FD/T2/T3 gauntlet, compare cos1² on the §6 table. ~30 minutes
    of work given the existing harness. Cheap, informative, and
    currently absent — should run BEFORE adding more epicycles to S6.

The rank-r lift goes first among the structural changes because the
dominant failure mode (T3 streaming plateau drift on mixed-tail-sharp)
is driven by the rank-1 score's wide level set; the within-rank-1
epicycles only address symptoms of that. Q7 jumps to the front of the

lifted to rank-r.

Operational success criterion (Q0). The implicit goal across Q1..Q6 is to
close the S6→online gap on the tail-dominant matrices in §6's table —
specifically slot-2 cos1² on mixed-tail-sharp/-balanced, static-cex,
diffuse-diffuse, and etf-basket-basis. The 3-matrix strict bar (cos[1]²
≥ 0.6 on static-cex AND mixed-tail-sharp; ≥ 0.7 on diffuse-diffuse) is the
*minimum*, but online clears it comfortably on those (0.819, 0.850, 0.528)
on cos1² — diffuse-diffuse is online's only sub-bar slot-2 number, and
that's where iSVD wins. So a value-only score family that cleanly beats
combined but stays well below online has not really validated the design
hypothesis. Q1..Q6 should each be evaluated on the §6 table, not just on
the 3-matrix gauntlet.
