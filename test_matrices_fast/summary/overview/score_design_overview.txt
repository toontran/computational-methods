Score-design overview: streaming-SVD slot-2 (and rank-r) decision
==================================================================

Date: 2026-04-28
Audience: someone picking up the score-design line of work and wanting the
high-level picture before diving into f_hm3_score_implementation_context.txt
or hm_score_new_variant_test_context.txt.


1. What we're doing
-------------------

At each block in the streaming SVD we maintain a rank-r approximation of A's
leading right singular vectors. Per block we have:

  A_sketch = state.s · state.V^T     rank-r CARRY of A_seen (prefix)
  A_cur                               half-window of new rows
  A_fut                               peek half-window after that

The streaming bench commits a rank-r subspace each block; we measure terminal
cos²(V_carry, V_exact) at block 31 (final_exact_cos in the JSON output).

Scope note on slot-1: the LOCAL combined-step slot-1 candidate is
well-defined — V_default[:, 0] is the leading SV of
M_gain = [B_top; A_cur]. That does NOT mean terminal streaming slot-1
recovery is globally solved. AB-03's force-oracle bench shows why:
`--force-oracle-v2` (forcing only chosen_v2 while keeping the streaming
V_default[:,0] anchor) captures only about 1.00/2.00 oracle mass on six
of seven §6 matrices, while `--force-oracle-frame` (replacing both
oracle anchors) recovers about 1.95/2.00; see
summary/score_family_aggregator_ablation/why_E2_breaks_landscape.md and
diagnostic_toolkit.txt S-4. Thus V_default[:,0] is load-bearing in the
terminal stream. When this document says "slot-2" below, read it as the
local score-selection problem under a chosen anchor, not as a proof that
the final first carried direction is correct.

The whole design effort is about a value-only score(v) that, when maximized
over B_union or lifted to rank-r, identifies the appropriate oracle target
rather than a high-scoring non-oracle surrogate. Here B_union is the right
row-space audit domain defined precisely in §1quinquies; vector tests use
slotwise v_i targets, oriented-frame tests use Z_oracle, and
Grassmann-invariant subspace tests use span(V_r) / principal angles.

Constraints on the score:
  - value-only computable (no V_exact)
  - not over-fit to A_cur (else slot-2 = A_cur top SV which double-covers slot-1)
  - not over-fit to A_fut (else "row-of-A_fut cheat" — doesn't generalize)
  - aware of the carry (else we discard past info)
  - ideally robust to spiky responses (a single row dominating ‖A_cur v‖²)

We've iterated S1 → S2 → S3 → S4 → S5 → S6 (current). Each variant is a
hypothesis about how to balance these forces. S6 is the current best on
clean-rowspace matrices but has known gaps (P4 plateau, no relH1 guard).

The implicit baseline to beat is NOT `combined`, it is `future_hmean_online`
— the production policy. Online uses a different mechanism (rank-2 SVD
reframe over an online candidate pool with HM-online scoring), in contrast
to the score-design line which is value-only, analytically differentiable,
and rank-r liftable. Whether the score-design path can match or beat online
on its strong matrices is the open question driving this line.

OPERATIONAL BASELINE (value-only). The number a value-only score family
must match is `future_hmean_online` evaluated with the value-only candidate
pool — see summary/bench_matrix_sweep_value_only_online/synthesis.txt
(INFRA-10, 2026-04-28). Pool entries: opt2, mgain_deflated_svd,
block_complement, prev_opt2 (the prior `q2_vs_q1oracle` entry was a
projection of V_exact[:,1] into the row span and has been removed from the
default pool; see half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py:31-33, 512-514).
Under the value-only baseline, on the 8 §6 matrices at half_win=32 S6
already matches or beats online cos1² on 4 of 6 tail-dominant matrices
(mixed-tail-sharp +0.063, mixed-tail-soft +0.156, diffuse-diffuse +0.081,
mixed-tail-balanced -0.041); the surviving real gap is etf-basket-basis
(-0.162). On residual-spiky / risk-residual both online and S6 sit far
below iSVD/hybrid (FAM-02 regime, not FAM-01). See §6.

CEILING reference (oracle-aware). The original numbers in
summary/bench_matrix_sweep/synthesis.txt (2026-04-23) used an oracle-aware
pool — one of five entries was V_exact[:,1] projected into the row span
(`q2_vs_q1oracle`). Those numbers are retained at that path as a labeled
upper bound only; they are NOT a ship target and NOT used in any §6 / Q0
acceptance criterion. CLI flag `--online-include-oracle` reproduces them.


1bis. The original combined-score baseline — verified failure mechanisms
------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-27. Source: combined_obj_sketch_bias_synthesis.txt and
combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py.)

(Re-read 2026-04-28 with §1ter's bias framing: phi, HM3, and relH1 are
all members of ONE inductive-bias family — reward high sample entropy,
refuse to reward low. The mechanisms below describe HOW the bias is
enforced and where it succeeds/fails, not whether the bias is itself
"correct" relative to ‖Av‖² — that question is reframed in §1ter.)

The S1..S6 family is the *replacement* for the original combined score
  score_w(v) = (‖Bv‖² + ‖A_w v‖²) · phi_w(v)
from reports/approximation/new_approx_combined.txt. Direct probing on
mixed-tail-sharp pins WHY combined's slot-2 fails in the old diagnostic
language. Treat M1/M2/M3 below as historical sub-cases, not as orthogonal
mechanisms; DIAG-05's unified reliability reading supersedes the split.

(M1) phi explicitly de-rewards oracle-aligned slot-2 directions.
     Block-1 landscape in the deflation complement of combined_v_1, with
     gain_tot essentially flat (0.819–0.833) across all directions:
       direction              phi     score   align_o2
       combined_v_2          5.66    4.68    0.016
       oracle_v_2_proj_⊥     3.70    3.08    0.749
       random_0..4         3.0–3.9  2.5–3.2   < 0.04
     The entire score gap is phi. phi rewards entropic spread; V_exact[:,1]
     produces a CONCENTRATED response and therefore loses on phi.
     This persists at b1 with NO sketch, so it's not a sketch-reliance bug.

(M2) Once B exists, the rank-r carry pins slot 2 to span(V_state).
     state_align(combined_v_2) grows to 0.989 at b31; oracle_v_2_proj's
     state_align is 0.103. ‖Bv‖² only sees span(V_state); once
     sk_F2/cur_F2 ≈ 30 (b31), gain2 dominates and the score's
     complement-of-v_1 max is just the second retained singular vector.

(R1) Joint Stiefel-2 with the SAME combined score does NOT fix it.
     basis_selection="joint" at r=2 produces essentially identical V to
     greedy at b6/b12/b31 (state_align(joint_v_2)=0.989, identical
     alignments). At b1 it nudges algn_o2 to 0.247 but at lower score —
     it's a different stationary point, not a higher max.
     ⇒ The slot-2 failure is intrinsic to the SCORE on Stiefel(d, r),
     not to greedy deflation.

(M3) Combined is BLIND TO A_fut → over-fits to A_cur's local rowspace.
     This is the failure mechanism on diffuse-diffuse (and the original
     motivation for the future_hmean / S1..S6 family). Combined maximizes
     ‖Bv‖² + ‖A_cur v‖² with NO A_fut term, so at b1 (no carry yet) its
     slot-1 v is the top SV of A_cur — a single half-window's specific
     draw. On diffuse-diffuse the population rowspace is uniform, so
     A_cur's top SV is a noise-corrupted LOCAL direction, not the
     population top SV. The population top SV is the v that maximizes
     gain across BOTH halves, which is what HM2(u_g1, u_g2) recovers.

     Verified empirics (overview §6 b1 numbers, diffuse-diffuse):
       S6_v1 ≈ oracle_v1 cos² = 0.999 (HM2 on b1 = popSV optimizer)
       S6_v2 ≈ oracle_v2 cos² = 0.982
       Combined v1, v2 < 0.05 against either oracle column
     Streaming-bench (final cos1² on diffuse-diffuse, S6 synthesis §4
     against value-only online from INFRA-10):
       combined 0.030, iSVD 0.239, S6 0.005, online_VO 0.050
       (oracle-aware ceiling: 0.528).
     iSVD wins because its streaming SVD update integrates information
     across blocks, recovering the population direction. Combined has
     no such integrator and never sees A_fut. Note: against the
     value-only baseline, online no longer dominates this matrix —
     the oracle-aware 0.528 was substantially the q2_vs_q1oracle pool
     entry winning the slot.

     Different locus from M1/M2: M1/M2 are slot-2 observations on a matrix
     where combined's slot-1 succeeds under current code; M3 is a slot-1
     observation on diffuse-diffuse. This is a diagnostic distinction, not
     evidence that the mechanisms are independent.

Together M1, M2, M3 describe three observed faces of the combined-baseline
reliability failure that launched the score-design line:
  - M3 (blind to A_fut)            → motivates including A_fut in score
                                     ⇒ the future_hmean family exists
  - M1 (phi penalty on concentrated)→ motivates removing entropic gating
                                     ⇒ HM-style balance instead of phi
  - M2 (carry pins slot-r)          → motivates rank-aware u_sk weighting
                                     ⇒ rank-r CARRY (sk_F2_low), Family-B
                                       carry-confidence multipliers,
                                       rank-r Stiefel lift
This is the design arc that lands at S6 = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) per §2,
but §1quinquies should be read as the current controlling principle:
feature identifiability, not a taxonomy of independent fixes.

(DIAG-05 update, 2026-04-28.) `probe_m1m2m3_unification.py` reran the
M1/M2/M3 ablations and tested the §1ter tail-conspiracy framing directly;
see `summary/m1m2m3_tail_conspiracy_unification/
m1m2m3_tail_conspiracy_unification.md`. The right reading is now:
M1/M2/M3 are three sub-cases of ONE reliability failure, where the
combined score rewards a direction whose visible-window evidence is not
stable in the oracle sense.

  - M1 is the within-window phi sub-case: phi rewards the score-favoured
    b1 direction's sample spread and de-rewards the projected oracle;
    the rerun reproduces phi=5.65 vs 3.70 and flat energy.
  - M2 is the carry sub-case: as full-prefix evidence dominates
    (prefix/A_cur=30.27 at b31), the score-favoured and joint-Stiefel
    slot-2 directions pin to the carry (state_align≈0.988) while the
    deflated projected oracle remains much less carry-aligned
    (0.293 in the current rerun; older note had 0.103).
  - M3 is the between-window sub-case: combined's b1 diffuse-diffuse
    slot-1 direction has current/future asymmetry≈1, while the projected
    oracle is balanced; S6/HM2 oracle-warm recovers the projected oracle
    (cos²=0.999). The mixed-tail-sharp slot-1 control still succeeds
    under current code (final combined cos0²=0.891 vs older 0.785).

Tail-conspiracy diagnostics separate the score-favoured direction from
the oracle at all three failure points by at least one of top-row share
(the task's relH1 formula), current/future asymmetry, or cross-window
replicability. Treat the mechanism list above as sub-cases under this
single "visible evidence is not reliably oracle-identifying" failure.

(M5) HM3-rank-r joint-frame mis-aligns with slot-1 capture in the
     mixed-tail streaming regime (verified 2026-04-29 by FAM-01 B0 T3).
     B0 (HM3 over u_X = ||A_X Z||_F²/||A_X||_F², joint Stiefel(d, 2)
     optimization) was tested as a DIAGNOSTIC of evidence sufficiency
     under §1quinquies. T3 streaming bench (half_win=32, seed=0)
     yields cos0² collapse on 3 of 4 high-entropy matrices —
     mixed-tail-sharp (Δcos0² = −0.7392), mixed-tail-balanced
     (Δ=−0.4192), mixed-tail-soft (Δ=−0.8275) — with tail_mass
     0.85–0.99 and final cos0² in [0.018, 0.032]. The optimizer
     completed all 31 streaming blocks on each matrix; the failure is
     not optimizer divergence but a degenerate frame that misses the
     dominant direction entirely.
     The failure mode is SLOT-1 COLLAPSE WITH TAIL_MASS CONCENTRATION,
     not the slot-2 evidence-pinning anticipated by the §1quinquies
     S-2 reread. Margin sensitivity correlates with collapse: etf-
     basket-basis (S6 cos0²≈1.00) holds; the 3 collapse matrices have
     S6 cos0² ∈ [0.44, 0.86]. Together with the 31/31 step completion
     and the 3-of-4 reproducibility, this argues structural rather
     than implementation: the HM3-rank-r objective itself is mis-
     aligned with slot-1 capture once the streaming carry feeds back
     into the joint frame.
     M5 inherits the unified reliability reading: rank-r joint
     optimization over the SAME magnitude-only u_X evidence cannot
     repair it because the failure is in the evidence model. Pivot is
     evidence augmentation (FAM-07 cross-window consistency, FAM-08
     robust row-energy, FAM-09 stability), not a different aggregator
     over the same u_X. See summary/score_family_stiefel_rank_r/
     variants/B0/03_cross_variant_synthesis.md.

Read §3 and §5 of this document with R1 in mind: "lifting to Stiefel
rank-r" means "go to higher r AND change the score to a Grassmannian-
invariant frame form". Joint optimization at the same r with the same
per-vector score is *not* the lift, and is verified not to help.

Implications consistent with §2's S6 design moves:
  - S6's HM3 BALANCE ENFORCER (§2a) targets another face of the same
    reliability problem: combined's v has raw_g2 ≈ 0 (no future-window
    content) because combined doesn't see A_fut. M1/M2 explain WHY
    combined's v points where it does inside the past windows.
  - S6's removal of phi-style entropic gating (§2d removes S5's hard
    gate; the original combined uses phi multiplicatively) is the
    natural M1 fix — replacing the entropy-reward with HM3 balance
    means concentrated responses are no longer penalized.
  - S6's rank-r CARRY (§2c) addresses M2 partially by anchoring u_sk
    to a stationary scaling, but the rank-r carry STILL has rank-r
    information loss. Family-B carry-confidence multipliers (§7 Q5)
    and the rank-r Grassmannian lift (§5) are the structural fix.

§1quater adds a related symptom — oracle u-imbalance under the current
Frobenius weights — which can amplify the same identifiability failure
when the weight choice puts u_sk on a different scale than u_g1 / u_g2 at
the oracle. AB-03 shows this symptom is not a sufficient design principle
by itself.


1quinquies. From oracle balance to oracle identifiability (controlling principle, supersedes §1quater)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-28, after AB-03 phase 1 closure.) §1quater framed the
slot-2 problem as "miscalibrated weights leak score — fix the weights so
the oracle is balanced." The AB-03 phase 1 closure (`probe_e2_landscape.py`
+ `--force-oracle-v{2,frame}` bench, see
`summary/score_family_aggregator_ablation/why_E2_breaks_landscape.md`)
refutes that framing as a SUFFICIENT design principle.

What was learned:

  1. Better oracle u-balance is NOT sufficient to recover (v_1, v_2).
     S6_E2 drives DIAG-04b's slot-2 ratio_max from 17–57× to 1.7–2.4× on
     diffuse-diffuse / residual-spiky-shocks, but T3 still regresses on
     diffuse-diffuse and residual-spiky-shocks. The seven-matrix capture
     table is mixed rather than uniformly worse, so this is evidence
     against E2 as a control principle, not proof that every balanced
     weighting must regress.
  2. Per-direction reweighting can move the score's argmax AWAY from the
     oracle while making the oracle more balanced. The optimizer correctly
     finds the new (non-oracle) argmax. At b31:
       Δ_S6_E2(slot-2 winner − oracle_v2_proj) = +0.041 (diffuse-diffuse),
                                                  +0.047 (residual-spiky),
       vs Δ_S6 = +0.007 on both (S6's vector slot-2 gap is near zero in
       this anchored diagnostic).
     The DIAG-04b balance metric measured u-balance AT the oracle but never
     measured where the score's actual argmax now sits.
  3. Fixing slot-2 alone is insufficient when the slot-1 anchor is wrong.
     `--force-oracle-v2` (chosen_v2 ← oracle_v2_proj at every block) yields
     capture ≈1.00/2.00 on 6/7 §6 matrices; only `--force-oracle-frame`
     (BOTH oracle anchors) recovers ~1.95/2.00.
  4. Therefore: the slot-2 problem is NOT primarily about balance. The
     tested E2/per-direction balance route is killed; further scalar or
     per-vector weight work should only continue if it first passes the
     oracle-vs-winner gap screens below.

Controlling principle (replaces "calibrate weights for oracle balance"):

   The question is no longer "does the oracle have the right balance?"
   It is "what information makes the oracle frame identifiable?"

   Notation: throughout the overview, `B_union` means the RIGHT row-space
   subspace available to the audit,
     B_union := rowspan([B; A_cur; A_fut])
              = range([B; A_cur; A_fut]^T) ⊂ R^d,
   not the column-space/range of the raw vertically stacked matrix.

   A score is "oracle-identifying" iff its argmax (on the relevant
   manifold — sphere for rank-1, Stiefel(d, r) for rank-r) coincides
   with the oracle target appropriate to that test: slotwise vector,
   oriented frame, or unordered subspace. Those targets are related but
   not interchangeable; span-level claims should use principal angles,
   while oriented-frame claims need a score that breaks right-rotation
   symmetry. All claims are additionally capped by the cos² ceiling
   imposed by B_union. Oracle-balance, oracle-symmetry, and similar audit
   properties measure features OF the oracle but do not measure
   identification.

Canonical screens (mandatory for any new score-design proposal):

  S-1  oracle-vs-winner gap (vector level):
         Δ_score(winner − oracle_v2_proj) ≤ 0 (or near-zero within tol)
       on every §6 matrix at b31. If S6 satisfies this and the new
       variant does not, the variant has moved the argmax off the
       oracle and must be rejected regardless of any audit metric.
       Implemented in `probe_e2_landscape.py`.

  S-2  oracle-vs-winner gap (frame level, FAM-01-DIAG):
       Two-part rule (revised 2026-04-28). Z is rank-r; score is a
       frame-level extension of u_X (e.g. u_X(Z) = ||A_X Z||_F² /
       ||A_X||_F², HM over X). Run high-budget joint Stiefel(d, r)
       ascent on Score(Z) → Z_winner. Define
         Δ_score   = Score(Z_winner) − Score(Z_oracle)
         pa_cos²[k] = sorted descending principal-angle cos² between
                     span(Z_winner) and span(V_exact[:, :r])
       and apply both:
         (a) ALIGNMENT bar (operational): min_k pa_cos²[k] ≥ τ_align,
             with τ_align = 0.5 for slot-r ship and τ_align ≥ 0.9 for
             slot-1 ship. This is what the streaming bench's
             final_exact_cos terminally measures, so it is the
             load-bearing acceptance bar.
         (b) MISRANKING bar (diagnostic): Δ_score ≤ 0 (within tol)
             means the score's argmax IS the oracle frame. Δ_score > 0
             with high pa_cos² is HARMLESS OVERSHOOT — the score's
             argmax is a different but nearly-aligned frame; ship is
             still possible. Δ_score > 0 with low pa_cos² is REAL
             MISRANKING — the score genuinely prefers a non-oracle
             subspace; evidence augmentation needed.
       Verdict labels (canonical):
         PASS-aligned         min pa_cos² ≥ τ_align AND Δ_score ≤ 0
         PASS-with-overshoot  min pa_cos² ≥ τ_align AND Δ_score > 0
         FAIL-slot-r-only     pa_cos²[0] ≥ 0.9 AND pa_cos²[r-1] < τ_align
                              (slot-1 captured; lower slots collapse —
                              this is the §6 slot-2 pathology, not a
                              new kill; T3 streaming bench is still
                              warranted to size the cos0² lift)
         FAIL-subspace        pa_cos²[0] < 0.9 (slot-1 also lost;
                              evidence model is genuinely misranking
                              the dominant direction; structural
                              redirect to evidence augmentation)
       Acceptance: a rank-r variant ships only if it produces
       PASS-aligned or PASS-with-overshoot on every §6 matrix.
       FAIL-slot-r-only matrices are conditional candidates (T3-
       streaming-bench gated on cos0² gain). FAIL-subspace matrices
       require new evidence axes.

       Tests whether the evidence model is sufficient to identify the
       oracle FRAME, not just a single direction. The vector-level
       test (S-1) is necessary but the frame-level test (S-2) is the
       definitive one for rank-r ship variants.

  S-3  row-cheat dominance:
         Score(Z_oracle) ≥ Score(Z_rowcheat)
       where Z_rowcheat is the top-r SVD of M_gain. Already implemented
       (per-block diagnostic includes frame_S6_oracle and frame_S6).
       The score must rank the oracle ABOVE the row-of-window cheat;
       otherwise oracle is not even in the level set the optimizer
       reaches.

  S-4  force-oracle ceiling probes:
         (a) `--force-oracle-v2` (chosen_v2 ← oracle_v2_proj):
             cos² recovery measures whether the regression is
             reachability (recovers if so) vs streaming-state
             divergence (does not recover if V_default[:,0] is bad).
         (b) `--force-oracle-frame` (V_selected ← rank-2 SVD frame
             of [oracle_v1_proj, oracle_v2_proj]):
             cos² ceiling under the current pipeline. Bounds the
             best any score variant can achieve at the chosen
             half_win.

  S-5  anchor sensitivity (greedy vs joint):
         Compare the score-max in (i) B_def(slot-1 winner) vs (ii)
         B_def(oracle_v1_proj). If (ii) recovers the oracle while (i)
         doesn't, the failure is greedy-anchoring; FAM-01 rank-r joint
         lift is indicated. If both produce the same non-oracle argmax,
         the failure is in the score itself (evidence model is
         insufficient) and joint optimization will not save it.

Hypothesis triage (every new proposal must declare which it addresses):

  H1  argmax shift: per-direction reweighting moves the score's argmax
      off the oracle. Confirmed for E2 (gap +0.041 / +0.047).
      PRIMARY MECHANISM. Any new weight scheme inherits this risk.
  H2  landscape discontinuity (Voronoi step changes): small (~1e-3
      score jumps under E2), not load-bearing.
  H3  slot-1 anchor collapse from removing F-norm prior: REFUTED in
      this narrow sense by probe D — E2 gradient near sketch_v1 is
      30–100× LARGER than S6, back-pull is positive, so E2 did not
      weaken the local sketch_v1 attraction. This is not a global
      "slot-1 solved" claim: the force-oracle-v2/frame evidence above
      shows terminal recovery still depends critically on whether the
      streaming V_default[:,0] anchor is the oracle anchor.

Decision metrics that are EXPLICITLY INVALID under this principle:
  - "oracle has good u-balance"          (S6_E2 has it, fails bench)
  - "oracle looks more symmetric"
  - DIAG-04b-style balance ratios as a STANDALONE acceptance metric
  - any audit-stage metric that does not include S-1 / S-2 above
DIAG-04b numbers remain useful as a DESCRIPTIVE measurement of
oracle balance but cannot be used to decide ship/kill.

Open question (replaces "what weights balance the oracle?"):
   What minimal evidence makes the oracle frame Z_oracle the unique
   maximizer of a value-only frame-level score over Stiefel(d, r)?
   Possible signals (each must be tested, not assumed):
     - absolute energy scale (||A_X Z||_F vs sum/Frobenius)
     - cross-window response alignment (not just per-window magnitude)
     - carry compatibility vs carry entrapment (V_state alignment with
       a structural penalty for trivially matching the carry)
     - frame-level interactions (non-separable across columns of Z)
   FAM-01 (rank-r lift) is reframed as a DIAGNOSTIC of evidence
   sufficiency, not as the solution. If even joint Stiefel-r
   optimization with the current evidence model fails to make
   Z_oracle the maximizer, the evidence model itself is incomplete.

FAM-01-DIAG result (2026-04-28; reread under the two-part S-2 rule).
The canonical frame-level screen was implemented in
`probe_frame_oracle_gap.py` and run at b31 on the §6 7-matrix suite
with 100 random restarts. T1 gradient checking for
Score(Z)=HM(u_sk,u_cur,u_fut), u_X=||A_X Z||_F²/||A_X||_F², passed
28/28 cells across b1/b2/b12/b31 (worst score rel=5.84e-08; worst
trace sanity rel=1.20e-08). Δ_score is positive on all seven matrices
(every Z_winner outscores Z_oracle), but the operational verdict is
governed by alignment, not Δ_score alone. With pa_cos² between
span(Z_winner) and V_exact[:, :2]:

  matrix                  Δ_score   pa_cos²[0]  pa_cos²[1]  S-2 verdict
  residual-spiky-shocks   +0.0050   0.967       0.867       PASS-with-overshoot
  etf-basket-basis        +0.0335   0.996       0.154       FAIL-slot-2-only
  mixed-tail-sharp        +0.0130   0.971       0.484       FAIL-slot-2-only
  mixed-tail-balanced     +0.0121   0.988       0.530       FAIL-slot-2-only
  mixed-tail-soft         +0.0158   0.896       0.474       FAIL-slot-2-only*
  diffuse-diffuse         +0.0094   0.507       0.057       FAIL-subspace
  static-cex              +0.0348   0.331       0.301       FAIL-subspace

  * pa_cos²[0]=0.896 is just under the 0.9 slot-1 bar; treat as FAIL-
    slot-2-only with a flag for subspace-recheck if cleaner restarts
    or higher budget tighten slot-1.

The required ablations (HM × total energy, HM × cross-window Gram/
right-space agreement) also have Δ_score > 0 on all seven matrices in
the free-frame screen, but their alignment behavior must be reread
under the same rule before they are treated as kills.

Reread conclusions:
  - The hypothesis "magnitude-only HM frame evidence is unconditionally
    not oracle-identifying" is too strong. The score's argmax IS
    aligned with the oracle subspace on residual-spiky-shocks (~0.92
    average pa_cos²) — overshoot there is harmless and a streaming
    ship is plausible.
  - The §6 SLOT-2 pathology survives at rank-r: on 4 matrices (etf-
    basket-basis, mixed-tail-sharp, mixed-tail-balanced, mixed-tail-
    soft) joint Stiefel optimization captures slot-1 cleanly (pa_cos²[0]

1quater. Oracle u-imbalance: HM3 with miscalibrated weights leaks score (SUPERSEDED — see §1quinquies)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-28.) HM3's "smallest-link enforcer" property only fires
correctly if the oracle direction itself has roughly balanced
(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2). The current S6 weights — sk_F2_low (rank-r truncated
F²), cur_F2 (full F²), fut_F2 (full F²) — were chosen as a "unit-fixer"

against an oracle-balance criterion. Empirically the oracle is far from
balanced on the matrices where S6 fails.

INFRA-07 (component_time_series_probe.py) measures (u_sk, u_g1, u_g2)

ratio u_sk / u_g1 at oracle_v1_proj — what we would WANT to be ~1 if
HM3 were calibrated — is dramatically matrix-dependent:

  matrix                     b6 sk/g1   b31 sk/g1   S6 cos1²
  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  etf-basket-basis           1.36x      1.74x       0.652  (works)

The matrix where S6 works partially (etf-basket-basis, 0.652 cos1²) is

Mechanism (M4): HM3 of the oracle on static-cex b31 is
  HM3(0.48, 0.0012, 0.0012) ≈ 3 / (2.08 + 1666 + 1666) ≈ 0.0018

A non-oracle direction with mediocre but balanced response — e.g.
(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) = (0.05, 0.05, 0.05) in some span(V_state)-aligned
direction — scores HM3 ≈ 0.05, which is 28× higher than oracle's HM3.
The optimizer picks the non-oracle "balanced fake" because the score
does not have its peak at the oracle.

This is distinct from but interacts with §1bis's M2 (carry pins slot-r
to span(V_state)):
  M2 says the carry CARRIES correct signal but pins v to span(V_state)
       at the expense of v_oracle.
  M4 says the score's PEAK isn't at the oracle in the first place,
       even before optimization-landscape concerns. The miscalibrated
       weights mean that when oracle has high u_sk and low u_g1/u_g2
       (which it intrinsically does because the prefix carry accumulates
       across blocks while a half-window only sees half_win rows), HM3
       under-rewards oracle and over-rewards mediocre balanced
       directions. M2 says "the optimizer can't find oracle"; M4 says
       "even if it could, oracle isn't the highest-score point under
       this score."

Distinct from §3's plateau drift:
  §3 is an empirical high-score/non-oracle phenomenon: INFRA-05 and the
      b31 landscape probes find directions with nearly maximal S6 score
      but poor oracle alignment. This does not by itself prove a
      codimension theorem or a global-max characterization.
  M4 says the oracle isn't even at the score's max — it's BELOW the
      max because the max is at a non-oracle balanced direction.

These are not redundant: a score could be M4-fine (peak at oracle) and
plateau-bad (level set wide), or M4-broken (peak misplaced) and
plateau-fine (narrow basin around the wrong peak). The static-cex

The conceptual fix for M4: choose w_sk, w_g1, w_g2 such that for

w_X / raw_X(v*) match across X. This makes HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) at the
oracle ≈ the common u value (not dominated by the smallest term), and
non-oracle directions whose u's are smaller in any window get correctly
penalized by HM3's smallest-link.

The challenge: w_X must be value-only (no V_exact).

1ter. The high-sample-entropy bias and SVD complementarity (the real "why phi")
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-28.) Assumption (A2) in
reports/approximation/new_approx_combined.txt — "h_2 normalized by
log-row-count is invariant from C_w to N_w to A" — is the algebraic
scaffolding that produced phi's form. It is NOT a property we expect
to actually hold on the matrices we run. Operationally, the score
family — phi in combined, HM3 in S6, relH1 in Q3 — is just deliberately
biased to reward high-sample-entropy directions and refuse to reward
low-entropy ones. The score does not estimate, predict, or extrapolate
to population entropy of A v; it is an inductive bias on the carried
basis, nothing more.

Why this is the right bias direction (the SVD-complementarity plan):

  - iSVD / streaming-SVD does well on LOW-sample-entropy directions —
    a few rows dominating the response is exactly what an SVD update
    naturally latches onto. iSVD is the low-entropy specialist.
  - The score-design family is the COMPLEMENT — by refusing to reward
    low-entropy responses, it pushes the carried right basis toward
    directions whose response is spread across many rows: the HIGH-
    sample-entropy directions. The score family is the high-entropy
    specialist.
  - The eventual policy is a hybrid in which iSVD covers the low-
    entropy directions and the score family covers the high-entropy
    ones, and together they cover the matrix's right space. Under this
    plan the score family is NOT supposed to beat iSVD on residual-
    spiky-shocks / risk-residual-panel — those are SVD's territory by
    design.

Where the plan currently breaks: the score family must FIRST be
competent on its own home turf — the high-sample-entropy regime — and
right now it is not. diffuse-diffuse (S6 cos1² = 0.005) and
mixed-tail-sharp (S6 = 0.013) are high-entropy regimes where iSVD
itself is weak (0.489 / 0.013) and the family is meant to dominate.
Until the family is competent there, the SVD-complementarity story is
forward-looking, not validated. The first job is to fix the high-
entropy regime; the spiky-residual / low-entropy regime is downstream
and is not the family's responsibility under the complementarity plan.

How §2's four design moves operationalize the same bias:

  - phi (combined) is a WITHIN-WINDOW high-entropy reward computed on
    C_w = [R; A_w].
  - HM3 (S6, after move (d) removed phi) is a BETWEEN-WINDOW high-
    entropy reward — concentrating energy on a single half-window
    drives the corresponding u_X to zero and HM3 → 0.
  - F1 / relH1 (Q3, deferred) restores the within-window term as a
    multiplicative factor on top of HM3, doubling down on the bias
    along a different sample axis.

None of these are population-entropy estimators. They differ in WHICH
sample axis the bias is enforced over (within-window, between-window,
or both).

How §1bis's mechanisms re-read under this framing:

  - M1 is the bias firing CORRECTLY on mixed-tail-sharp at b1, not a
    bug in phi. Oracle slot-2 there has a genuinely concentrated
    response (low sample entropy by construction of the matrix); the
    family is designed not to pick it. iSVD is supposed to handle that
    direction, except iSVD also fails (cos1² = 0.013). This is the
    BOUNDARY regime where neither family wins — partial concession to
    the structure of the matrix.
  - M3 is the family failing on its OWN home turf. combined's bias
    samples only [R; A_w], so on diffuse-diffuse — where the population
    high-entropy direction needs A_fut to be visible — combined's bias
    is too small-sample-noisy to identify it. HM3 / future_hmean is
    the sample-broadening fix; the bias direction is unchanged. That
    fix works at the score-ranking level (T2) but is undone by the
    plateau-drift in §3 at the streaming level (T3).
  - The plateau-drift on mixed-tail-sharp at b31 (toolkit INFRA-05)
    is bias-direction-correct-but-optimization-fails in the empirical
    sense: the implemented rank-1 score admits high-score points with
    low oracle alignment, and the optimizer lands there. FAM-01 tests
    whether a frame-level, subspace-target score and joint search remove
    that failure; it is not assumed from level-set dimension alone.

Operational consequence for §7's priority: high-sample-entropy regime
work is P0 and structural; low-entropy / spiky-residual regime is
conceded to SVD/iSVD by the complementarity plan and is downstream.
FAM-01 (rank-r lift) targets high-entropy plateau-drift and is the
top-priority structural fix. FAM-02 (row-concentration guard) is
reframed: its primary value is as an additional within-window high-
entropy bias term that may help on the HIGH-entropy regime; beating
iSVD on spiky-residual is a bonus and not the ship-criterion.

What we ACTUALLY want: high-sample-entropy ORACLES, not just any
high-entropy direction
---------------------------------------------------------------

The bias above is "reward high sample entropy" — but the *unstated
premise* is that the top right singular vectors of A (the oracles) are
the directions whose responses are spread across many rows. The score
takes "spread on the currently visible windows" as a PROXY for "spread
on the population", and the proxy IS the bias. The bias is only useful
to the extent that this proxy holds.

The proxy can be fooled. The pathology to keep on the radar:

  TAIL CONSPIRACY. On heavy-tailed matrices a NON-oracle direction v
  can score high sample entropy on the current visible windows because
  a few rare large-norm rows in the visible sample align with v
  constructively — the "spread" is supplied by a small number of tail-
  row conspirators rather than by uniform contribution from many rows.
  The next window draws different tail rows, the conspiracy doesn't
  replicate, and v's response collapses on later windows. The score
  rewards v at the current block as if it were a high-entropy oracle
  direction; it is not, and the carried basis decays as the conspiracy
  unwinds. This is currently a failure mode the family has not solved.

  Two distinct detection signatures, each catching a different shape
  of conspiracy:

    (i)  BETWEEN-window: u_g1 ≫ u_g2 (or vice versa). The high entropy
         doesn't replicate from A_cur to A_fut. HM3 already catches
         this — the smallest-link form drives HM3 → 0 when one window
         is starved. This is the partial fix combined did not have.

    (ii) WITHIN-window: low relH1 (a single row supplies most of
         ‖A_X v‖²). The "spread" looks high in the h_2 metric only
         because a few medium-large entries appear among many small
         ones; in extreme form, the row-of-A_fut cheat
         (hm_triplet_raw_best, §5 of the toolkit) is one row supplying
         the entire response. Q3 / FAM-02 / F1 — relH1 multiplicative
         on top of HM3 — is the within-window guard.

  The two signatures are different cuts of the same pathology and are
  not redundant: (i) catches conspiracies that A_cur and A_fut do not
  share; (ii) catches conspiracies that LOOK spread across rows but
  aren't. On heavy-tailed matrices both A_cur and A_fut may by chance
  contain tail rows that align with v — HM3 alone is not enough, and
  the within-window check does additional work. Under §1ter's framing
  this is the REAL reason F1 is on the design list, not the spiky-
  residual / row-concentration narrative it was historically marketed
  as.

  The row-of-A_fut cheat (hm_triplet_raw_best) is the EXTREME form of
  tail conspiracy — a single row dominates v's response. The T2 STOP
  rule (oracle ≥ row-cheat on every block) is the simplest tail-
  conspiracy regression test we have.

Implication for the bias proxy: the design target is not "reward any
high-sample-entropy v" but "reward v's whose high-sample-entropy
property is STABLE — across rows within a window, and across windows
across time". HM3 enforces between-window stability; relH1 enforces
within-window stability; in combination they aim at stable-spread
oracles. The score family has so far enforced only between-window
stability (HM3 in S6); the within-window axis is the open lever and
is what the failure mode above wants closed.

combined-baseline M1/M2/M3 numerics after recent code churn and (ii)
test whether the score-favoured directions at those failures are

cross-window Pearson replicability of |A_cur v| vs |A_fut v|. Closure

Only if that note verifies separation at all three failure points should

Theory status: combined is derived; the new scores are heuristic
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Important status distinction (2026-04-28): the original combined score
has a real derivation under assumptions. In particular, (A2) gives a
lower-bound / surrogate story for the phi factor. We now believe A2 is
not reliable as an operational assumption on the counterexample suite,
but the combined score still has a tight algebraic origin: if the
assumptions hold, the score has a theorem-shaped justification.

The S1..S6 / relH1 family does NOT have an equally strong population-
recovery theory. TH-01 (summary/theory_hm3_surrogate.md, 2026-04-28)
resolves Q8 as ITERATE: HM3 is defensible as the smooth proxy for a
visible max-min stability objective over carry/current/future normalized
responses, but it is not a proven lower bound on a population objective,
a consistent estimator of future energy, or a derived approximation to
subspace recovery. It is still a deliberately chosen inductive bias for
final SVD recovery:

  - normalize the carry/current/future responses into comparable energy
    fractions;
  - use HM to make cross-window balance a hard requirement;
  - later multiply by relH1 if we want within-window stability as well.

That story is coherent and empirically motivated, and now has a limited
surrogate interpretation: maximize HM3 is a smooth reciprocal-barrier
relaxation of maximizing min(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2). The required assumptions
are different from A2: comparable Frobenius-normalized responses, split-
sample relevance of A_cur/A_fut, rank-r carry relevance, and positive
signal in all visible sources. Without those assumptions, and without
TH-02/TH-03, S6 stays labeled as a heuristic high-entropy stability bias.

The new-score story should therefore be read as:

  combined:  algebraically derived surrogate, empirically diagnosed as
             failing when its assumptions / sampling view break;
  S6 family: visible split-sample stability surrogate, empirically useful
             in some regimes, still heuristic for final subspace recovery.


2. Design intuitions for S6 (F-weighted HM3)
--------------------------------------------

  score(v) = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2)        sketch present
  score(v) = HM2(u_g1, u_g2)              block-1 fall-through

  u_sk = ‖A_sketch v‖² / sk_F2_low       sk_F2_low = ‖A_sketch‖_F² (rank-r CARRY)
  u_g1 = ‖A_cur v‖²    / cur_F2          cur_F2    = ‖A_cur‖_F²
  u_g2 = ‖A_fut v‖²    / fut_F2          fut_F2    = ‖A_fut‖_F²

Four design moves, each addressing a specific pathology:

(a) Harmonic mean as the BALANCE ENFORCER.
    HM3 is dominated by its smallest argument. To score high under HM3, v
    must keep ALL THREE u's bounded away from zero. This is exactly why
    combined fails: combined's v's have raw_g2 ≈ 1e-6 (essentially
    orthogonal to A_fut), so u_g2 ≈ 4e-8 and HM3 ≈ 1e-7. HM3 turns
    "balance" from a soft preference into a hard constraint.

(b) Frobenius weighting as the UNIT-FIXER.
    Raw responses ‖A_X v‖² live on incompatible scales (carry small,
    cur/fut ~26 in our setup, full-prefix sketches grow with block).
    Dividing by ‖A_X‖_F² puts every term on a [0, ~1] scale tied to the
    *fraction of available energy* v captures. Now HM3's three arguments
    are directly comparable.

(c) Rank-r CARRY (sk_F2_low), not full-prefix Frobenius (sk_F2_full).
    sk_F2_full grows linearly with block_id, so u_sk would shrink linearly
    — score becomes block-id sensitive in a perverse way. sk_F2_low is
    roughly stationary across blocks because the carry singular values
    track the matrix's true top-r. This makes u_sk ∈ [0, ~0.5] tightly,
    which is critical for rank-r generalization (the scaling stays sane
    as r grows).

(d) Smooth on the sphere wherever u's > 0 — no gate, no relH1.
    S5's hard gate (raw_sk > T → score, else 0) created a discontinuity
    that excluded the oracle from the reachable set on some blocks (P4 in
    the prior context). S6 removes the gate to make the oracle reachable
    everywhere it has positive responses. Cost: no protection against
    spiky responses (the row-of-A_fut cheat) — diagnostic confirms S6
    picks low-relH1 v's on mixed-tail-sharp. This is parked as F1, not
    fixed.

-------------------------------------------------

3. Plateau drift is an empirical failure mode
----------------------------------------------

The plateau claim used here is diagnostic, not a theorem. What we have
measured is that S6's high-score region in the available union rowspace is
not concentrated around the projected oracle: the optimizer routinely finds
higher-score, non-oracle points, and random high-score samples can have poor
principal-angle alignment. The informal "plateau" label refers to that
observed high-score/non-oracle set. Do not infer from codimension-1 level
sets alone that every scalar score must drift, or that rank-r must fix it.

Empirics (T2 on mixed-tail-sharp, b6, b12, b31): the S6 optimizer's vector
scores higher than oracle_v2_proj on score_S6 (Δ ≈ +1e-3 to +2e-2), but
its align_v2_proj is 0.04–0.25 — i.e., the optimizer found a different
high-score point.

This is a major reason S6 underperforms on the streaming bench, but the
advisor audit narrows the claim: small greedy-vs-joint gaps and plateau
samples show implemented routines consistently find non-oracle frames that
beat the oracle; they do not certify the global maximum of the nonconvex
Stiefel landscape. The next step is therefore a frame-level oracle-vs-winner
screen with high-budget restarts (FAM-01-DIAG), not a blanket claim that
rank-r lifting alone fixes the score. If the frame screen still finds
Z_winner > Z_oracle, the evidence model is incomplete and the fix is in the
features, not merely in the optimizer.


4. The block-1 fall-through and why it's natural
-------------------------------------------------

At block 1 there's no sketch yet, so we can't compute u_sk. The natural
fall-through is HM2(u_g1, u_g2). Up to a per-block constant (cur_F2 ≈
fut_F2), this is identical to c-weighted HM-evi at block 1, which is why
c_evi_v1 was the top of the rank table at block 1 in the diagnostic.
This is a feature, not a bug — at b1 the only signal is split-sample
balance between A_cur and A_fut, and HM2 is the right form for that.

Explicit equivalence (why oracle_v_proj LOSES to c_evi_v1 at b1):
  - At b1, c_sk = 0 → c-weighted HM-evi reduces to a 2-arg form over
    (raw_g1, raw_g2) with weights (c_g1², c_g2²).
  - c_g1 = half_win/cur_F2 ≈ c_g2 ≈ 1.21, so weighted HM ≈ unweighted
    HM(raw_g1, raw_g2) up to a multiplicative constant.
  - S6 at b1 reduces to HM(raw_g1/cur_F2, raw_g2/fut_F2). Since
    cur_F2 ≈ fut_F2 ≈ 26.4 (rows are unit-energy on every matrix here),
    that's the same objective as c-weighted HM-evi up to a per-block
    rescale.
  - c_evi_v1 is by construction the OPTIMIZER of that objective; the
    projected oracle is the top SV of the FULL matrix A restricted to
    rowspan([A_cur; A_fut]) = range([A_cur; A_fut]^T) ⊂ R^d,
    which is balanced (raw_g1 ≈ raw_g2) but not
    energy-maximal locally. So c_evi_v1 ≥ oracle on score_S6 at b1.
  - Empirically (mixed-tail-sharp b1):
        c_evi_v1: u_g1=0.01612, u_g2=0.01664 → HM2 = 0.01638
        oracle_v2_proj: u_g1=0.01578, u_g2=0.01583 → HM2 = 0.01580
    The (u_g1+u_g2)/2 gap is ~4%, which is HM's gap exactly because
    both candidates are already nearly balanced.


5. Generalization to rank-r (the immediate next step)
------------------------------------------------------

The score generalizes naturally:
  u_sk(V) = ‖A_sketch V‖_F² / ‖A_sketch‖_F²   for V ∈ St(d, r)
  u_g1(V) = ‖A_cur V‖_F²    / ‖A_cur‖_F²
  u_g2(V) = ‖A_fut V‖_F²    / ‖A_fut‖_F²

  score(V) = HM3(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2)

Formally, 0 <= u_X(V) <= 1: an r-frame can capture at most the total
Frobenius energy in A_X. The r/rank(A_X) scale is only an
equal-spectrum/random-subspace heuristic for typical captured energy,
not a bound. Score is invariant under right multiplication by O ∈ O(r),
i.e. it is a function of span(V), not of a particular oriented frame.
Frame labels such as Z_oracle are still useful for running Stiefel
optimizers, but success/failure claims for this Grassmann-invariant
objective must be stated through span(V_r) and principal angles.

The hard parts of the lift:
  (i)   Replace sphere-ascent with Stiefel-ascent. Tangent projection,
        retraction (QR or polar), line search.
  (ii)  Re-derive the gradient. Each column gets the same shape of grad
        we already have, but now combined into a matrix-valued grad with
        the Stiefel projection applied. FD check has to be lifted too.
  (iii) Plateau detector becomes a SUBSPACE metric: principal angles
        between span(V_opt) and span(V_oracle), not pairwise cos. The
        "score(opt) > score(oracle) but cos² ≈ 0" detector → "score(opt)
        > score(oracle) but max principal angle ≈ π/2".

Why the rank-r lift is still the right next structural test: the failure
we care about is frame/subspace recovery, while the current score commits
one deflated vector at a time behind a possibly bad slot-1 anchor. A
Grassmann-invariant frame score evaluates the candidate subspace directly,
uses principal angles as the native success metric, and lets S-5 separate
"bad greedy anchor" from "insufficient evidence model." It may reduce the
empirical high-score/non-oracle drift seen by INFRA-05, but that is a
measurement to verify with FAM-01-DIAG and rank-r plateau probes, not a
generic dimension-shrinkage guarantee.

  benefit.)


6b. Reliability-aware evidence: what is done vs missing
--------------------------------------------------------

(Added 2026-04-28.) The score family should now be read as an early-stage
heuristic for aggregating evidence across windows. The main limitation is
not only the HM-style aggregator; it is that the evidence variables

  u_X(v) = ||A_X v||^2 / ||A_X||_F^2

are magnitude-only proxies. They say that a direction captures normalized
energy in a window, but they do not by themselves say whether that energy is
reliable. A complete evidence model should track:

  - energy: signal strength in each visible source;
  - support: how many rows contribute the response;
  - consistency: whether the same structure appears across windows;
  - robustness: whether outlier rows dominate the statistic;
  - stability: whether the evidence persists under row perturbation /
    resampling.

Current status by direction:

  1. Rank-r / subspace-level scoring.
     Done: principal-angle diagnostics (INFRA-01), rank-r row-cheat frame
     baseline (INFRA-03), plateau-width baseline (INFRA-05), and Stiefel FD
     gradient-check infrastructure (INFRA-02).
     Missing: the actual FAM-01 frame-level score implementation and T2/T3
     validation. The score must be Grassmann-invariant and use frame-level
     evidence such as ||A_X Z||_F^2, not independent per-column scoring.

  2. Within-window evidence quality / influence control.
     Done: relH1 and effective-support diagnostics exist; DIAG-01 measured
     oracle/candidate support across the suite. D0 also integrated one
     concrete score form, S6 * relH1(A_cur v), and killed it.
     Missing: a non-D0 reliability-aware score that modifies the evidence
     itself, for example u_X -> u_X * relH1(A_X v) on each window, a
     leverage-weighted u_X, or an influence-capped row aggregation. D0's
     failure refutes only the parameter-free current-window multiplier, not
     the broader reliability-control idea.

  3. Cross-window consistency / correlation.
     Done: HM3 enforces magnitude balance across carry/current/future.
     Missing: a diagnostic or score term that checks directional agreement
     of the response patterns across windows. A high u_cur and high u_fut can
     still be misleading if they come from different row structures.

  4. Robust evidence.
     Done: row-cheat and entropy diagnostics expose outlier-dominated
     candidates.
     Missing: robust u_X definitions such as capped, trimmed, Huberized, or
     influence-reweighted row-energy sums with differentiable gradients.

  5. Stability-based evidence.
     Done: split-window HM3 is a crude stability proxy, and TH-02 describes
     when visible-window entropy should or should not generalize.
     Missing: resampling / perturbation diagnostics and a score factor that
     rewards directions whose row-energy evidence has low subsample variance.

Guiding principle for the next phase: evidence should reflect not just
magnitude, but reliability. The next structural step remains rank-r scoring;
the next evidence upgrades should be phrased as reliability-aware u_X
definitions, not as more scalar aggregators over the same magnitude-only u_X.
