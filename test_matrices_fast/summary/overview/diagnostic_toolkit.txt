Diagnostic toolkit for streaming-SVD score design
==================================================

Date: 2026-04-28
Companion to: summary/overview/score_design_overview.txt

This is the catalog of debugging / diagnostic tools we use when iterating
on the slot-2 (slot-r) score. Each tool is listed with WHAT it answers,
WHEN to reach for it, and the typical output location.


1. Per-block diagnostics (snapshots at b1, b2, b6, b12, b31)
-------------------------------------------------------------

These run the streaming algorithm forward to the chosen blocks and dump
all candidates × all variants × all metrics for those blocks. They are
the SINGLE most useful family of tools when designing a new score.

  r_sk_g_score.py [--no-oracle-warmstart]
    What: Full table of S1..S6 scores + raw norms + relH1 + alignment
          + state_align for every candidate at the chosen block.
          Also runs the rank-2 sequential optimizer for each variant.
    When: Always run this first when adding a new variant. Confirms
          oracle ranks well in T2 before committing to a streaming bench.
    Out:  summary/r_sk_g_*.csv|json|<matrix>.txt
    Key columns: r_sk, raw_g1, raw_g2, hm3, score_S?, sat_S?,
                 align_v?_proj, pa1_o2, state_align, relH1.
    Acceptance row at the end of each block:
      "Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw): S?=±..." — does the score outrank
      the row-of-A_fut cheat?
    Per-block subspace alignment table:
      "subspace alignment (cos² principal angles vs oracle 2-frame)" —
      one row per rank-2 candidate frame (combined, sketch, mgain_svd,
      r_sk_g_S4/5/6/6_GM, r_sk_g_S5_sketch_init) reporting the two
      principal-angle cos² of [v1, v2] vs [oracle_v1_proj,
      oracle_v2_proj], plus their mean. Slot-assignment-invariant view
      of "how much of the oracle 2-plane does this frame recover?",
      complementing the per-slot align_v?_proj columns.
      Backed by `principal_angles(V_opt, V_oracle)` in
      test_matrices_fast/subspace_metrics.py (INFRA-01 — see
      score_family_workflow.txt §5). Wired in two places: (i) per-row
      `pa1_o2` column = cos² of v vs the oracle 2-plane (the single
      principal angle for the 1D-vs-2D case; equals
      align_v1_proj + align_v2_proj for orthonormal oracle frame), and
      (ii) the per-block frame table above. For r=1 the metric reduces
      to the existing pairwise cos²; for rank-r frames it is the
      foundation that FAM-01 / FAM-03 / INFRA-02 / INFRA-04 will build
      on. Smoke run: summary/infra_subspace_alignment/smoke.txt.

  frob_hm3_score_diagnostic.py
    What: Focused F-HM3 ranking in the user's "F2/raw_X" display form.
    When: Designing a new F-weighted variant, want a clean ranked table.
    Out:  summary/frob_hm3_score_*.txt

  frob_norm_diagnostic.py
    What: Raw-norm dump (no scoring). Shows ‖A_X v‖² per candidate.
    When: Sanity check on whether a variant's denominator is well-scaled.

  compare_combined_vs_s6_b1b2.py
    What: Two-block focused view of the F2/raw quantities + relH1 for
          combined_v1/v2, oracle_v1/v2_proj, S6_v1_full / S6_v2_deflate.
    When: Block-level component breakdown for a small set of candidates.

  compare_oracle_alignment_b1b2.py
    What: 2x2 cos² table: {combined_v1/v2, S6_v1/v2} vs {oracle_v1/v2_proj}.
    When: Is the optimizer slot-aligning with the oracle pair, or is it
          finding off-axis high-score points (the plateau pathology)?

  combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py
    What: Per-block decomposition for the ORIGINAL combined score
          (score_variant="combined" in cex_restricted_space_probe.py):
          gain_sketch = ‖A_sketch v‖², gain_block = ‖A_cur v‖²,
          sketch_frac, phi, state_align, score_total, plus the 4-cell
          cross-alignment {combined_v1, combined_v2} × {oracle_v1_proj,
          oracle_v2_proj}. Symmetric Δscore decomposition into via_gain
          and via_phi terms (§6c-style time-series across blocks).
          Also runs basis_selection="joint" on the same snapshots as a
          companion candidate panel (joint_v1, joint_v2) so greedy-vs-
          joint can be A/B'd at fixed score.
    When: Whenever the question is "why does the original combined
          baseline fail slot-2?" or "would joint optimization at the
          same r help?" Use as the canonical reference probe BEFORE
          designing a new score variant — establishes the failure
          mechanism the new score must address.
    Out:  summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_<matrix>.{csv,txt}
          summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_<matrix>_landscape.txt
          summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_synthesis.txt
    Verified findings on mixed-tail-sharp at b1/b2/b6/b12/b31:
      - phi at b1 is the entire slot-2 score discriminator (M1).
      - state_align(combined_v_2) → 0.989 at b31 (M2).
      - joint Stiefel-2 ≡ greedy at b31 (R1, REFUTES "joint fixes it").


2. Gradient sanity
------------------

  r_sk_g_score.py --gradient-check
    What: Finite-difference vs analytic gradient at random v + at a
          carry-aligned v (for variants with gates that clip random v's).
    When: Always before T3. Catches sign errors and scale errors before
          burning a streaming bench.
    Acceptance: rel_err < 1e-7 at float64. S6 is smooth on the sphere,
                so a random v probe is sufficient. S5 needs a carry-
                aligned probe because random v's lie in the score=0 zone.

  stiefel_grad_check.py
    What: Column-wise finite-difference vs analytic gradient on St(d,r).
          Each FD direction is seeded in one frame column, projected into
          the Stiefel tangent space by P_V(G)=G - V sym(V^T G), then evaluated
          with a symmetric polar-retracted central difference. Includes the
          known-correct trace-form sanity f(V)=trace(V^T M V), grad=2MV,
          plus reusable rank-r S6 / S6_GM value-and-grad functions.
    When: Before FAM-01 / FAM-03 rank-r Stiefel ascent work. This is the
          rank-r replacement for the single-v r_sk_g_score.py gradient check.
    Out:  summary/infra_stiefel_fd_gradient_check/gauntlet.txt
          summary/infra_stiefel_fd_gradient_check/epsilon_sweep.txt
          summary/infra_stiefel_fd_gradient_check/synthesis.md
    Acceptance: rel_err < 1e-7 at float64 for the trace sanity and score
                probes. Smoke run on mixed-tail-sharp b2/b12, r=2:
                worst score rel_err 2.29e-09; worst trace sanity rel_err
                1.11e-09. Verdict ship; unblocks FAM-01 / FAM-03 gradient
                correctness infrastructure.


3. Streaming bench (the ground truth)
--------------------------------------

  half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py --policies ... --rsk-variant ...
    What: Runs the actual streaming algorithm to block 31 and reports
          final cos²(V_carry, V_exact) for the top-r right SVs, plus
          tail_mass = 1 - sum_k cos²[k] across rank.
    When: After T1 (gradient) and T2 (per-block ranking) pass.
    Out:  summary/bench_matrix_sweep_*/M_win64.{json,csv,txt,log}
    Policies of interest (--policies):
      isvd                    pure streaming SVD of M_gain (latency baseline)
      combined                combined-greedy v1 + opt2
      hybrid                  combined-greedy v1 + deflated-SVD v2
      future_hmean_online     greedy v1 + HM-online scored v2. Default
                              candidate pool is VALUE-ONLY (opt2,
                              mgain_deflated_svd, block_complement,
                              prev_opt2). Operational baseline numbers
                              live at
                              summary/bench_matrix_sweep_value_only_online/
                              (INFRA-10, 2026-04-28). The oracle-aware
                              pool (adds `q2_vs_q1oracle` =
                              V_exact[:,1] projected into the row span)
                              is reachable via --online-include-oracle
                              and is a CEILING reference only —
                              numbers in summary/bench_matrix_sweep/
                              synthesis.txt should not be used as a
                              ship target.
      future_hmean_r_sk_g     score-design family (S1..S6 via --rsk-variant)
    Two acceptance bars to keep in mind:
      (i)  Strict 3-matrix bar (per spec §4): cos[1]² ≥ 0.6 on static-cex
           AND mixed-tail-sharp; ≥ 0.7 on diffuse-diffuse; tail_mass ≤ 0.5
           on at least 2 of 3 matrices. This is the MINIMUM bar.
      (ii) Operational bar — match-or-beat future_hmean_online with the
           VALUE-ONLY pool (INFRA-10) on the §6 table of
           score_design_overview.txt. Run the full 17-matrix sweep
           when a variant clears (i), to size the gap matrix-by-matrix.
           Acceptance criteria reference online_VO only; the oracle-
           aware ceiling is informative but never a ship target.


4. Optimizer-landscape probes
------------------------------

  future_hmean_local_max_landscape.py
    What: Score vs distance from oracle along radial slices.
    When: Debugging "is the oracle a local max?"

  future_hmean_local_optimum_probe.py
    What: Random-restart climb from many sphere starts; logs landing points.
    When: Debugging "does the optimizer reliably find the same basin?"

  plateau_width_probe.py    (INFRA-05; new 2026-04-28)
    What: Sample N=200 random Stiefel frames V on St(d, r) inside
          {V : score(V) ≥ τ · score(V_opt)} (default τ=0.95) and report
          principal-angles to the projected oracle frame. Per-matrix CSV
          + cross-matrix boxplot.
    When: Quantify whether the score's empirical high-score region is a
          narrow basin around oracle (cos² near 1, tight) or a wide-but-
          multimodal shell displaced from oracle (cos² spread / near 0).
          Direct measurement of the §3 plateau-drift phenomenon from
          score_design_overview.txt — the answer to Q2.
    How:  Default samples within B_union =
          rowspan([B; A_cur; A_fut]) = range([B; A_cur; A_fut]^T) ⊂ R^d
          for tractable acceptance; rejection sampling with MCMC fallback
          if rate <1%.
    Out:  summary/infra_plateau_width/{matrix}_b{block}_{variant}_r{rank}.csv
          summary/infra_plateau_width/summary_plot.png
          summary/infra_plateau_width/synthesis.md
          summary/infra_plateau_width/summary.json
    Verified findings (S6, r=1, N=200, τ=0.95):
      - All three canonical matrices have a "wide-but-multimodal-and-
        displaced" basin: in-sublevel sample min_cos² clusters at 0–0.1,
        far from oracle.
      - mixed-tail-sharp at b31: opt min_cos² = 0.000 (the catastrophic
        plateau-drift signature).
      - Acceptance rates 2.84–6.74% — pure rejection sufficed.
    Pre-req for FAM-01's empirical landscape check: re-run with --rank>1
    after the lift to verify whether high-score non-oracle samples become
    less prevalent.

  future_hmean_optimizer_diagnostic.py
    What: The underlying sphere-ascent optimizer. Used as the engine by
          all the variants via __globals__["future_hmean_value_grad"]
          monkey-patching. Reading this is sometimes necessary when an
          optimizer call returns None.

  --- TIER-A CANONICAL SCREENS (mandatory; codified 2026-04-28 from
  score_design_overview.txt §1quinquies controlling principle) ---

  S-1 vector-level oracle-vs-winner gap     → probe_e2_landscape.py
  S-2 frame-level oracle-vs-winner gap      → probe_frame_oracle_gap.py
                                              (FAM-01-DIAG canonical done)
  S-3 row-cheat dominance                   → per-block diagnostic's
                                              frame_S6_oracle vs frame_S6
  S-4 force-oracle ceiling probes           → half_window_sliding_*
                                              --force-oracle-v2 / -frame
  S-5 anchor sensitivity                    → probe_e2_high_budget.py

  Any new score-design proposal must pass S-1, S-3, S-4 before T3
  bench commitment, and S-2 before rank-r ship. "Oracle balance" /
  DIAG-04b ratios are descriptive-only; they cannot stand in for these
  screens.

  probe_e2_landscape.py    (AB-03 phase 1 closure; new 2026-04-28)
    What: Per-matrix at b31 (or any block via --block):
          (A) score under S6 and S6_E2 at oracle_v{1,2}_proj,
              sketch_v{1,2}, S6 winner, S6_E2 winner — the
              oracle-vs-winner gap test (S-1).
          (B) geodesic landscape: sweep unit v on the great circle
              between oracle_v1_proj and oracle_v2_proj, log score
              under both variants.
          (C) along the geodesic, log argmax(k_sk, k_g1, k_g2) for the
              per-direction weight schemes — flags Voronoi-cell
              transitions and measures score jumps at boundaries.
          (D) slot-1 attractor strength: gradient projected onto
              sketch_v1 under small perturbations.
    When: Whenever a new audit-stage weight scheme (DIAG-04b style)
          claims oracle balance, run this to check whether the new
          score's argmax actually sits AT the oracle. Required screen
          for FAM-01 / FAM-04 / future per-vector reweightings before
          T3 commitment.
    How:  Self-contained driver; uses combined-score stream_to_block
          to reach the snapshot, then evaluates landscape with both
          score variants. ~30 s/matrix.
    Out:  summary/score_family_aggregator_ablation/
            landscape_<matrix>_b<block>.{txt,json}
            landscape_summary_b<block>.txt
    Companion: probe_e2_landscape_summary.py aggregates the
          force-oracle bench output (see below) into a single closure
          table.

  half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py --force-oracle-v2 / --force-oracle-frame
    What: Bench overrides for closure / ceiling probes:
          --force-oracle-v2: at every block, set chosen_v2 ← the
            slotwise target v_2, defined as V_exact[:,1] projected into
            B_union = rowspan([A_sketch; A_h1; A_h2]). V_default[:,0]
            still comes from streaming.
          --force-oracle-frame: at every block, set V_selected ←
            rank2_svd_frame(oracle_v1_proj, oracle_v2_proj, M_gain).
            Replaces BOTH the streaming carry slot-1 anchor and the
            optimizer's slot-2 pick.
    When: To isolate "is the regression a reachability failure?"
          (force-oracle-v2 should recover cos1² if so) vs "is the
          ceiling itself low?" (force-oracle-frame establishes the
          ceiling). Used in AB-03 phase 1 closure (see
          why_E2_breaks_landscape.md).
    Findings (2026-04-28, S6_E2 stream): force-oracle-v2 does NOT
          recover cos1² on 6/7 §6 matrices because V_default[:,0] is
          itself a poor anchor on the regression matrices; only
          force-oracle-frame recovers ~1.95/2.00 oracle-mass capture.

  probe_e2_high_budget.py     (S-5 anchor sensitivity; new 2026-04-28)
    What: At b31, runs slot-2 sphere ascent under three slot-1 anchors:
          (1) high-budget S6 / S6_E2 winner, (2) oracle_v1_proj,
          (3) sketch_v1 (V_state[:,0]). High budget = 200 random
          starts, maxit=2000, tol=1e-12. Reports score_S6 and
          score_S6_E2 of the slot-2 winner under each anchor and
          compares to score(oracle_v2_proj).
    When: To distinguish "greedy-anchoring failure" (different anchor
          recovers oracle) from "evidence-model failure" (no anchor
          recovers oracle). Required Tier-A screen S-5 for any new
          score variant.
    Out:  summary/score_family_aggregator_ablation/
            highbudget_<matrix>_b<block>.txt
            highbudget_summary_b<block>.txt
    Findings (2026-04-28, diffuse-diffuse b31): under S6_E2 with
          oracle_v1_proj-anchored deflation and 200 starts, slot-2
          winner scores 0.339 vs oracle_v2 score 0.216 (Δ=+0.123).
          Even with the right anchor and high budget, S6_E2's argmax
          in B_def(oracle_v1_proj) is not the oracle. Under S6 the
          gap is +0.0001 (oracle is the argmax). Confirms H1 robustly.

  FAM-01-DIAG: frame-level oracle-vs-winner gap (S-2 screen; CANONICAL DONE)
    What: Joint Stiefel(d, 2) optimization of a frame-level score
          Score(Z) = HM(u_sk(Z), u_cur(Z), u_fut(Z)) where
          u_X(Z) = ||A_X Z||_F² / ||A_X||_F². Reports Δ_score =
          Score(Z_winner) − Score(Z_oracle) AND pa_cos² principal
          angles between span(Z_winner) and V_exact[:, :r].
    When: Definitive screen for any rank-r ship variant. Tests
          whether the evidence model is sufficient to identify the
          oracle FRAME (not just a single direction). Closure rule
          is two-part: alignment governs ship/kill, Δ_score is
          diagnostic context only — see score_design_overview.txt
          §1quinquies for verdict labels (PASS-aligned,
          PASS-with-overshoot, FAIL-slot-r-only, FAIL-subspace).
    How:  `python probe_frame_oracle_gap.py --gradient-check` runs T1
          gradient checking for the canonical HM frame score using the
          INFRA-02 Stiefel FD harness. `python probe_frame_oracle_gap.py`
          runs the S-2 oracle-vs-winner gap screen.
    Out:  summary/infra_frame_oracle_gap/T1_gradient_check.{txt,md}
          summary/infra_frame_oracle_gap/frame_gap_b31.{txt,json}
          summary/infra_frame_oracle_gap/synthesis.md
          summary/infra_frame_oracle_gap_ablations/frame_gap_b31.{txt,json}
          summary/infra_frame_oracle_gap_ablations/synthesis.md
    Status: T1 PASS (2026-04-28): 7-matrix §6 suite at b1/b2/b12/b31,
          r=2; 28/28 cells passed with worst score rel=5.84e-08 and
          worst trace sanity rel=1.20e-08. Canonical S-2 (two-part
          rule, reread 2026-04-28 evening): Δ_score positive on all
          seven §6 matrices at b31, but alignment splits the verdict
          (1 PASS-with-overshoot, 4 FAIL-slot-2-only, 2 FAIL-subspace —
          see workflow [FAM-01-DIAG] for the per-matrix table). HM ×
          total energy and HM × Gram/right-space agreement ablations

          matrices remain live T3 candidates (slot-1 captured ≥0.9 or
          full subspace overshoot harmless); diffuse-diffuse and
          static-cex are FAIL-subspace and redirect to evidence
          augmentation (FAM-07/FAM-08/FAM-09).

  direction_alignment_probe.py    (new 2026-05-04)
    What: Take any direction v (or rank-r frame V) at (matrix, block)
          and report:
          (1) cos² (top principal angle) and mean cos² vs. a fixed
              REFERENCE PANEL of "kinds of directions":
                - oracle (TWO flavors — see "Oracle proj vs exact" below):
                    oracle_v?_exact     V_exact[:, k] directly
                                        (population truth — does NOT
                                        live in B_union in general)
                    oracle_v?_proj      V_exact[:, k] projected into
                                        B_union and re-normalized
                                        (slotwise reachable target;
                                        matches §5 "ABSOLUTE references"
                                        and oracle_v?_proj as printed
                                        by r_sk_g_score.py)
                    oracle_frame_exact / oracle_frame_proj  rank-r frames.
                - FD/carry: V_state_v? per column, V_state_frame
                - per-block top right SVs of A_cur, A_fut, M_gain
                  (≡ iSVD candidate), [A_cur; A_fut] (visible window),
                  B_top — both per-slot vectors and frame.
                - row cheats: top-r rows of A_cur and A_fut
                  (orthonormalized), per-slot and frame.
              Oracle reachability per slot, ||P_{B_union} V_exact[:, k]||²,
              is printed in the pretty header — it is the upper bound on
              cos²(v, oracle_v?_exact) for any v in B_union and equals
              cos²(v, oracle_v?_exact) / cos²(v, oracle_v?_proj) (modulo
              a ratio of squared-norms; see derivation below).
          (2) Row-response bias of v itself per window
              {B, A_cur, A_fut, visible}: relH1, effective support,
              effective-support fraction, top-1 row share, top-3 row
              indices. Reuses entropy_stats from oracle_entropy_audit
              (DIAG-01) verbatim.
          The state_align_check column equals ‖V_state^T v‖² (the §6
          per-row invariant) so cross-tool consistency is one-line
          verifiable against r_sk_g_score.py output.

    Oracle proj vs exact — the distinction:
          - oracle_v?_exact = V_exact[:, k] is the population right-SV
            of the FULL matrix A. Comparing v to this measures convergence
            to the population truth.
          - oracle_v?_proj = the orthonormalized projection of
            V_exact[:, k] into B_union = rowspan([B; A_cur; A_fut]) — the
            visible row span at the current block. This is the "BEST v_k
            in B_union pointed at the true singular vector" per §5
            ABSOLUTE references.
          - For any unit v that lives in B_union (which sketch_v?,
            combined_v?, S6_v?, mgain_topSV_v? all do by construction):
                cos²(v, oracle_v?_exact) = ||proj||² · cos²(v, oracle_v?_proj)
            where ||proj||² = ||P_{B_union} V_exact[:, k]||² is the
            "oracle reachability" reported per block in the pretty
            output. The gap "exact vs proj" is a property of the matrix
            and block (how much of the population direction lives in
            the visible window), NOT of the input v.
          - Use oracle_v?_proj when judging "did the optimizer find the
            best in-window target?" (the sharper question for value-only
            scores). Use oracle_v?_exact when judging "did streaming
            converge to the population truth?" — typically only
            informative once the visible window has accumulated enough
            history (oracle_reach approaches 1).
          The toolkit's other diagnostics use oracle_v?_proj as the
          reference because it is the streaming-achievable target;
          direction_alignment_probe is the one tool that surfaces both
          flavors so the projection gap is quantifiable per block.
    When: Whenever you want to ask "is direction X biased toward the
          per-block top SV / a particular row / the carry / the
          oracle?" without editing the candidate panel of the per-block
          probes. Useful for ablation winners, hand-crafted test
          vectors, or comparing FD/carry vs score-optimized vectors
          on the same reference set.
    How:  Two-tier API — `build_reference_pack(args, matrix, block)`
          streams once and builds the reference set; `analyze_direction(
          v, pack, label=...)` is cheap and can be called on as many
          input directions as needed. CLI default smoke panel is
          {sketch_v1, S6_v1, oracle_v1} on mixed-tail-sharp b31.
    Out:  summary/infra_direction_alignment/{matrix}_b{block}_alignment.csv
          summary/infra_direction_alignment/{matrix}_b{block}_rowbias.csv
          summary/infra_direction_alignment/{matrix}_b{block}.txt
    Determinism note: stream_to_block calls
          cex_restricted_space_probe.entropy_iter_basis_forget which uses
          the GLOBAL numpy RNG (np.random.standard_normal) for restart
          seeds and feasibility noise. Without an explicit
          np.random.seed(...) call, two consecutive runs on the same
          seed/args produce different V_state — observed swing on
          static-cex b31: cos²(sketch_v2, oracle_v2_proj) varied from
          0.000 to 0.932 across runs. direction_alignment_probe seeds
          numpy's global RNG to (args.seed + 7919·block) at the start
          of build_reference_pack. Other probes built on stream_to_block
          (oracle_entropy_audit, component_time_series_probe,
          plateau_width_probe, ...) inherit the same hazard and should
          add the same seed call before reporting numbers as
          ship-quality.

    Smoke (mixed-tail-sharp b31): sketch_v1 vs V_state_v1 = 1.0000
          (tautology); sketch_v1 vs Acur_topSV_v1 = 0.0000 (carry has
          drifted off the per-block top SV — M2 signature); sketch_v1
          vs oracle_v1_proj = 0.0086 (carry pinned away from oracle);
          S6_v1 vs oracle_v2_proj = 0.9746 (slot-allocation pathology
          — slot-1 winner lands on oracle_v2 rather than oracle_v1);
          oracle_v1 visible relH1 = 1.0, eff_frac = 1.0 (perfectly
          diffuse) vs sketch_v1 visible relH1 ≈ 0.85 (carry is
          mildly row-concentrated).
    Closes §8(a) (subspace-level alignment metric on arbitrary inputs)
          when used at rank>=2 — principal_angles is invoked uniformly
          for both vector-vs-frame and frame-vs-frame inputs.

  Deflation-complement landscape probe
    What: At a chosen block, fix the slot-1 winner v_1, then evaluate
          the score along a panel of unit directions in the deflation
          complement (orthogonal to v_1): the optimizer's slot-2 pick,
          oracle_v_j_proj_⊥ for each j, retained singular vectors_⊥,
          and a few random in-span directions. Decompose into gain/phi.
    When: To distinguish "phi explicitly avoids oracle" (M1 mechanism)
          from "rank-r carry pins slot-2" (M2 mechanism) and from
          "score-max is at the oracle but optimizer drifted off"
          (a plateau-drift mechanism, see §3 of overview).
    How:  Currently implemented inline in
          combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py::slot2_landscape_block1.
          For other blocks / other score variants, copy and adapt — the
          template is small (~50 lines).
    Generalizes to rank r: replace "deflation complement of v_1" with
          "deflation complement of {v_1,…,v_{k-1}}" and probe slot k
          for each k = 2..r. Especially useful for slot r since that's
          where M2 bites hardest (smallest residual signal).

  probe_m1m2m3_unification.py     (DIAG-05; DONE, added 2026-04-28)
    What: One-pass reproduction and unification probe for the original
          combined-score failure mechanisms in overview §1bis:
          M1 phi penalty, M2 carry pinning, M3 A_fut blindness.

5. Reference candidates ALWAYS produced per-block
--------------------------------------------------

The per-block diagnostic computes these for every block; they form the
reference frame for ANY new score:

  combined_v1, combined_v2          V_default[:, 0..1] from combined-step
  sketch_v1, sketch_v2              state.V columns (the carry directions)
  mgain_svd_v1, mgain_svd_v2        top-2 right SVs of [B_top; A_cur]
  hm_triplet_raw_best               row-of-A_fut cheat baseline (slot-2)
  rowcheat_v1, rowcheat_v2          rank-r row-cheat columns (INFRA-03)
  oracle_v1_proj, oracle_v2_proj    slotwise vector targets v_i:
                                    V_exact[:, 0..1] projected into
                                    B_union = rowspan([B; A_cur; A_fut])
  c_evi_v1, c_evi_v2                c-weighted HM-evi optimizer
  r_sk_g_S?_best                    each variant's optimizer
  r_sk_g_S?_v1_full, _v2_deflate    each variant's sequential rank-2

The two ABSOLUTE references:
  oracle_v{i}_proj  is the slotwise vector target: the BEST v_i in
                    B_union pointed at the true singular vector. No
                    value-only score can score-rank it above
                    hm_triplet_raw if A_fut has a single dominant row.
  hm_triplet_raw_best  is the worst-case "row cheat" baseline. If the
                       new score puts hm_triplet above oracle_v_proj on
                       any block, the score is exploitable.

Rank-r row-cheat baseline (INFRA-03; built per-block at any r > 1):
  rowcheat_v1, rowcheat_v2  Per-vector columns of the orthonormalized
                            top-r-rows-of-A_fut frame, exposed in the
                            candidate panel so the per-block table shows
                            their per-direction shares.
  V_rowcheat_frame          top_r_rows_frame(A_fut, r) from
                            row_cheat_baseline.py: pick the r rows of A_fut
                            with the largest squared norm, stack as columns,
                            QR-orthonormalize. At r=1 reduces to the
                            direction of the row with maximum norm.
  Z_oracle                  oracle_frame_proj(V_exact, B_union, r): top-r
                            oracle right singular vectors projected into
                            B_union column-wise, then QR'd. Use this
                            label for oriented-frame score tests.
  span(V_oracle_frame_proj) Subspace target associated with Z_oracle.
                            Use principal angles for Grassmann-invariant
                            rank-r recovery claims.
  Frame-level scorer        frame_score_S6(...) and frame_score_S6_GM(...)
                            (HM3 / GM3 over u_X_F = ||A_X V||_F²/||A_X||_F²).
  Acceptance line printed by write_text:
    Δ(oracle_frame − rowcheat_frame) [rank_r=...,oracle_r=...]:
        frame_S6=±...  frame_S6_GM=±...
  T2 STOP rule (rank-r generalization of the slot-2 row-cheat invariant):
    score(Z_oracle) ≥ score(Z_rowcheat) on every probed block.
  When this fails on any block, the rank-r-lifted score is row-exploitable
  and must be killed before T3.


6. Three diagnostic invariants per row
---------------------------------------

  align_v?_proj   cos²(v, oracle_v?_proj) — does v point at the oracle?
  state_align     ‖V_state^T v‖² — how much of v lies in the carry direction?
  Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw) per variant — exploitability check.

state_align is particularly useful: combined typically has it 0.5–1.0 on
mid-late blocks (combined sits in span(state.V)); oracle has it lower
because v_exact is partly in the part NOT in the carry.


6b. Per-candidate component breakdown (the FIRST thing to look at)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

When a candidate ranks unexpectedly low/high under a new score, decompose
the score into its u-components per candidate before doing anything else:

  cand            u_sk (or F2/raw_sk)   u_g1 (F2/raw_g1)   u_g2 (F2/raw_g2)   score
  oracle_v2_proj  0.282                 0.0072             0.0072             0.0106
  c_evi_v1        0.170                 0.0107             0.0108             0.0156
  combined_v2     0.440                 0.0034             6.7e-7             2.0e-6

Reading: oracle_v2 has the second-highest u_sk but its u_g1/u_g2 are
half of c_evi's → HM3 gets dragged down by the smallest term → c_evi
beats oracle on score despite worse sketch alignment. This is the
"smallest-link" diagnosis.

Display convention: the user-facing form is F2/raw_X (= 1/u_X), large
when v captures little energy in matrix X. The two views are dual; pick
the one that highlights the dragging term most clearly.

Sort-by-score table convention: print candidates in DESCENDING score
order, with rank in the leftmost column. Where the oracle / c_evi land
in the order is the first thing to read.

frob_hm3_score_diagnostic.py emits exactly this layout for S6.

Tail-conspiracy detection (added 2026-04-28; see §1ter of
score_design_overview.txt). When a non-oracle v ranks above oracle on
score, the next thing to look for is whether v's high-sample-entropy
property is STABLE or a tail-row conspiracy that won't replicate. Two
signatures, both readable from this same component table:

  - BETWEEN-window: large gap between u_g1 and u_g2 for v (e.g. u_g1
    ≈ 0.05, u_g2 ≈ 0.005). The "spread" exists in A_cur but not A_fut;
    HM3 should catch this and drive the score low — if v still ranks
    above oracle, the bias may be over-rewarding the larger u and the
    HM aggregator's smallest-link enforcement is not biting hard
    enough on this matrix.
  - WITHIN-window: low relH1 column for v in the same row of the
    table. relH1 ≈ 1/(active rows of A_X v) — when one or two rows
    supply most of the energy, relH1 is small. Even when u_g1 and
    u_g2 are both moderate, low relH1 on either side flags that the
    "high entropy" is supplied by a small number of conspirators in
    that window. Currently NOT in the score (S6 dropped phi via move
    (d)); this is what Q3 / FAM-02 / F1 is about.

Tail conspiracies that look balanced in (u_g1, u_g2) but are low-relH1
in BOTH windows are the hardest case — neither HM3 nor a single-window
relH1 threshold catches them; only a multiplicative relH1 factor (Q3)
or a leverage-aware u_X (Q4) does. Heavy-tailed matrices are where
this manifests; it is a candidate failure mode for mixed-tail-* T3
plateau drift in addition to the empirical high-score/non-oracle
landscape seen by INFRA-05.

Oracle u-imbalance signature (added 2026-04-28; see §1quater of
score_design_overview.txt). The third reading on the same component
table — orthogonal to tail-conspiracy detection — is whether the
ORACLE row itself is balanced or imbalanced. Look at the
(u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) row for oracle_v_proj. If u_sk is much larger than
u_g1 / u_g2 (or vice versa), HM3 of oracle is dominated by oracle's
own smallest u, and oracle's HM3 score is suppressed below what
mediocre-but-balanced non-oracle directions can achieve. This is the
M4 mechanism — DIFFERENT from M2 (carry pinning) and DIFFERENT from
§3 plateau drift, although they can co-occur. The fix is in WEIGHTS,
not in the aggregator: choose w_X such that raw_X(oracle)/w_X is
similar across X. DIAG-04 quantifies this; AB-03 searches for a
value-only weight scheme.

Two distinct regimes for the score-design line (reframed 2026-04-28
under §1ter of score_design_overview.txt — the score family is
deliberately biased toward high-sample-entropy directions, designed to
COMPLEMENT iSVD, which handles low-sample-entropy directions; (A2) is
not assumed to hold and the score is an inductive bias, not a
population-entropy estimator):

  - HIGH-SAMPLE-ENTROPY (the score family's home turf): mixed-tail-*,
    static-cex, etf-basket-basis, diffuse-diffuse. The score family is
    by design pushed toward directions whose response is spread across
    rows — these matrices are where the bias is supposed to pay off.
    Currently it does not: S6 cos1² is 0.005 on diffuse-diffuse and
    0.013 on mixed-tail-sharp, well below where the family must win.
    OPERATIONAL TARGET — every variant must demonstrate competence
    here BEFORE the SVD-complementarity story is validated.
  - BOUNDARY: residual-spiky-shocks. DIAG-01 measured oracle slot-1 as
    low-support but oracle slot-2 as moderate-support (median visible
    eff_frac 0.474, min 0.403), so the old LOW label was too coarse.
    Use it as a mixed-case audit point, not as a primary ship criterion.
  - LOW-SAMPLE-ENTROPY (iSVD/SVD's territory by design): risk-residual-
    panel. iSVD wins here because concentrated single-row-dominated
    responses are exactly what an SVD update naturally latches onto.
    Under the complementarity plan, the score family is NOT expected to
    beat iSVD here; closing this gap is a STRETCH GOAL, not a ship-
    criterion. F1 (relH1 / row-concentration guard) is reframed: not
    specifically a fix for this regime, but a stronger within-window
    high-entropy bias that may also help on the high-entropy regime —
    see Q3 in score_design_overview.txt §7.

  TH-04 closure (2026-04-30) gives the theoretical mechanism behind this
  split. iSVD's finite-window update maximizes observed stacked norm, so a
  transient leverage spike can beat a diffuse stable direction whenever
  its one-window gain exceeds the diffuse accumulated gain. The empirical
  audit in summary/theory_isvd_entropy_regime.md shows this pattern on
  static-cex and mixed-tail-*; it also records the caveats that
  diffuse-diffuse is partly recovered by iSVD, etf-basket-basis is more
  rank/slot allocation than pure entropy failure, and residual-spiky /
  risk-residual remain iSVD territory. Use this as a complementarity
  argument, not a blanket replacement claim.

  TH-05 closure (2026-04-30) hardens TH-04 against the adversarial-
  ordering objection. Under iid rows (each row has diffuse v-response a,
  each row independently has u-spike L with probability p), the
  separation event occurs at non-negligible probability: at the
  canonical m=32, q=96, a=1, L=8, p=1/128 setting, the joint event
  (iSVD picks transient u, current-plus-validation picks v, HM2 picks v)
  fires on 9.3 % of trials, matching the closed-form
  m p (1-p)^(m+q-1). HM2 dominates current-plus-validation which
  dominates iSVD on per-trial v-selection across the iid grid. See
  summary/theory_isvd_random_finite_sample/theory_isvd_random_finite_sample.md
  before invoking the iSVD shortcoming in any report — the point is
  finite-sample leverage variance, not adversarial placement.

9. Recommended workflow for a new score variant
------------------------------------------------

  Step 1: Implement value-and-grad in r_sk_g_score.py:r_sk_g_value_grad.
          Add to gradient_check loop.
  Step 2: Run --gradient-check on 2 matrices × 4 blocks, float64.
          Acceptance: rel < 1e-7 everywhere.
  Step 3: Wire into analyze_block (per-block diagnostic). Run T2 on
          static-cex / mixed-tail-sharp / diffuse-diffuse, blocks
          1/2/6/12/31, with --no-oracle-warmstart.
  Step 4: Inspect: Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw), align_v?_proj of the
          optimizer's best, oracle's rank under score_new in each block.
          STOP if oracle is below hm_triplet on any block.
  Step 5: Wire into half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py streaming
          policy (the future_hmean_r_sk_g branch). Add the new variant
          to --rsk-variant choices.
  Step 6: Run T3 streaming bench on the 7-matrix suite, half_win=32.
          Compare cos1², tail_mass against combined / iSVD baselines.
  Step 7: Write a synthesis.txt mirroring
          summary/bench_matrix_sweep_r_sk_g_S5_sketchinit/synthesis.txt.
          Decide: ship, iterate, or kill.

Roughly 30 minutes wall-clock for steps 1–6 once the score and grad are
written down (most of it is parallelized streaming bench).
