Diagnostic toolkit for streaming-SVD score design
==================================================

Date: 2026-04-28
Companion to: summary/overview/score_design_overview.txt

This is the catalog of debugging / diagnostic tools we use when iterating
on the slot-2 (slot-r) score. Each tool is listed with WHAT it answers,
WHEN to reach for it, and the typical output location.


1. Per-block diagnostics (snapshots at b1, b2, b6, b12, b31)
-------------------------------------------------------------

These run the streaming algorithm forward to the chosen blocks and dump
all candidates × all variants × all metrics for those blocks. They are
the SINGLE most useful family of tools when designing a new score.

  r_sk_g_score.py [--no-oracle-warmstart]
    What: Full table of S1..S6 scores + raw norms + relH1 + alignment
          + state_align for every candidate at the chosen block.
          Also runs the rank-2 sequential optimizer for each variant.
    When: Always run this first when adding a new variant. Confirms
          oracle ranks well in T2 before committing to a streaming bench.
    Out:  summary/r_sk_g_*.csv|json|<matrix>.txt
    Key columns: r_sk, raw_g1, raw_g2, hm3, score_S?, sat_S?,
                 align_v?_proj, pa1_o2, state_align, relH1.
    Acceptance row at the end of each block:
      "Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw): S?=±..." — does the score outrank
      the row-of-A_fut cheat?
    Per-block subspace alignment table:
      "subspace alignment (cos² principal angles vs oracle 2-frame)" —
      one row per rank-2 candidate frame (combined, sketch, mgain_svd,
      r_sk_g_S4/5/6/6_GM, r_sk_g_S5_sketch_init) reporting the two
      principal-angle cos² of [v1, v2] vs [oracle_v1_proj,
      oracle_v2_proj], plus their mean. Slot-assignment-invariant view
      of "how much of the oracle 2-plane does this frame recover?",
      complementing the per-slot align_v?_proj columns.
      Backed by `principal_angles(V_opt, V_oracle)` in
      test_matrices_fast/subspace_metrics.py (INFRA-01 — see
      score_family_workflow.txt §5). Wired in two places: (i) per-row
      `pa1_o2` column = cos² of v vs the oracle 2-plane (the single
      principal angle for the 1D-vs-2D case; equals
      align_v1_proj + align_v2_proj for orthonormal oracle frame), and
      (ii) the per-block frame table above. For r=1 the metric reduces
      to the existing pairwise cos²; for rank-r frames it is the
      foundation that FAM-01 / FAM-03 / INFRA-02 / INFRA-04 will build
      on. Smoke run: summary/infra_subspace_alignment/smoke.txt.

  frob_hm3_score_diagnostic.py
    What: Focused F-HM3 ranking in the user's "F2/raw_X" display form.
    When: Designing a new F-weighted variant, want a clean ranked table.
    Out:  summary/frob_hm3_score_*.txt

  frob_norm_diagnostic.py
    What: Raw-norm dump (no scoring). Shows ‖A_X v‖² per candidate.
    When: Sanity check on whether a variant's denominator is well-scaled.

  compare_combined_vs_s6_b1b2.py
    What: Two-block focused view of the F2/raw quantities + relH1 for
          combined_v1/v2, oracle_v1/v2_proj, S6_v1_full / S6_v2_deflate.
    When: Block-level component breakdown for a small set of candidates.

  compare_oracle_alignment_b1b2.py
    What: 2x2 cos² table: {combined_v1/v2, S6_v1/v2} vs {oracle_v1/v2_proj}.
    When: Is the optimizer slot-aligning with the oracle pair, or is it
          finding off-axis high-score points (the plateau pathology)?

  combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py
    What: Per-block decomposition for the ORIGINAL combined score
          (score_variant="combined" in cex_restricted_space_probe.py):
          gain_sketch = ‖A_sketch v‖², gain_block = ‖A_cur v‖²,
          sketch_frac, phi, state_align, score_total, plus the 4-cell
          cross-alignment {combined_v1, combined_v2} × {oracle_v1_proj,
          oracle_v2_proj}. Symmetric Δscore decomposition into via_gain
          and via_phi terms (§6c-style time-series across blocks).
          Also runs basis_selection="joint" on the same snapshots as a
          companion candidate panel (joint_v1, joint_v2) so greedy-vs-
          joint can be A/B'd at fixed score.
    When: Whenever the question is "why does the original combined
          baseline fail slot-2?" or "would joint optimization at the
          same r help?" Use as the canonical reference probe BEFORE
          designing a new score variant — establishes the failure
          mechanism the new score must address.
    Out:  summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_<matrix>.{csv,txt}
          summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_<matrix>_landscape.txt
          summary/combined_obj_sketch_bias_synthesis.txt
    Verified findings on mixed-tail-sharp at b1/b2/b6/b12/b31:
      - phi at b1 is the entire slot-2 score discriminator (M1).
      - state_align(combined_v_2) → 0.989 at b31 (M2).
      - joint Stiefel-2 ≡ greedy at b31 (R1, REFUTES "joint fixes it").


2. Gradient sanity
------------------

  r_sk_g_score.py --gradient-check
    What: Finite-difference vs analytic gradient at random v + at a
          carry-aligned v (for variants with gates that clip random v's).
    When: Always before T3. Catches sign errors and scale errors before
          burning a streaming bench.
    Acceptance: rel_err < 1e-7 at float64. S6 is smooth on the sphere,
                so a random v probe is sufficient. S5 needs a carry-
                aligned probe because random v's lie in the score=0 zone.


3. Streaming bench (the ground truth)
--------------------------------------

  half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py --policies ... --rsk-variant ...
    What: Runs the actual streaming algorithm to block 31 and reports
          final cos²(V_carry, V_exact) for the top-r right SVs, plus
          tail_mass = 1 - sum_k cos²[k] across rank.
    When: After T1 (gradient) and T2 (per-block ranking) pass.
    Out:  summary/bench_matrix_sweep_*/M_win64.{json,csv,txt,log}
    Policies of interest (--policies):
      isvd                    pure streaming SVD of M_gain (latency baseline)
      combined                combined-greedy v1 + opt2
      hybrid                  combined-greedy v1 + deflated-SVD v2
      future_hmean_online     greedy v1 + HM-online scored v2 (PRODUCTION
                              baseline — strongest on tail-dominant
                              matrices; see summary/bench_matrix_sweep/
                              synthesis.txt for the 17-matrix sweep)
      future_hmean_r_sk_g     score-design family (S1..S6 via --rsk-variant)
    Two acceptance bars to keep in mind:
      (i)  Strict 3-matrix bar (per spec §4): cos[1]² ≥ 0.6 on static-cex
           AND mixed-tail-sharp; ≥ 0.7 on diffuse-diffuse; tail_mass ≤ 0.5
           on at least 2 of 3 matrices. This is the MINIMUM bar.
      (ii) Operational bar — match-or-beat future_hmean_online on the
           §6 table of score_design_overview.txt. The strict 3-matrix bar
           is comfortably cleared by online on (i)'s static-cex and
           mixed-tail-sharp slot-2 (0.819 / 0.850), so a new variant that
           clears (i) but still trails online by 0.3+ has not closed the
           real gap. Run the full 17-matrix sweep (the same harness as
           bench_matrix_sweep) when a variant clears (i), to size the
           gap to online matrix-by-matrix.


4. Optimizer-landscape probes
------------------------------

  future_hmean_local_max_landscape.py
    What: Score vs distance from oracle along radial slices.
    When: Debugging "is the oracle a local max?"

  future_hmean_local_optimum_probe.py
    What: Random-restart climb from many sphere starts; logs landing points.
    When: Debugging "does the optimizer reliably find the same basin?"

  plateau_width_probe.py    (INFRA-05; new 2026-04-28)
    What: Sample N=200 random Stiefel frames V on St(d, r) inside
          {V : score(V) ≥ τ · score(V_opt)} (default τ=0.95) and report
          principal-angles to the projected oracle frame. Per-matrix CSV
          + cross-matrix boxplot.
    When: Quantify whether the score's high-score level set is a narrow
          basin around oracle (cos² near 1, tight) or a wide-but-multi-
          modal shell displaced from oracle (cos² spread / near 0).
          Direct measurement of the §3 plateau hypothesis from
          score_design_overview.txt — the answer to Q2.
    How:  Default samples within union rowspan B_union for tractable
          acceptance; rejection sampling with MCMC fallback if rate <1%.
    Out:  summary/infra_plateau_width/{matrix}_b{block}_{variant}_r{rank}.csv
          summary/infra_plateau_width/summary_plot.png
          summary/infra_plateau_width/synthesis.md
          summary/infra_plateau_width/summary.json
    Verified findings (S6, r=1, N=200, τ=0.95):
      - All three canonical matrices have a "wide-but-multimodal-and-
        displaced" basin: in-sublevel sample min_cos² clusters at 0–0.1,
        far from oracle.
      - mixed-tail-sharp at b31: opt min_cos² = 0.000 (the catastrophic
        plateau-drift signature).
      - Acceptance rates 2.84–6.74% — pure rejection sufficed.
    Pre-req for FAM-01 plateau-shrinkage hypothesis: re-run with --rank>1
    after the lift to verify the high-score level set narrows.

  future_hmean_optimizer_diagnostic.py
    What: The underlying sphere-ascent optimizer. Used as the engine by
          all the variants via __globals__["future_hmean_value_grad"]
          monkey-patching. Reading this is sometimes necessary when an
          optimizer call returns None.

  Deflation-complement landscape probe
    What: At a chosen block, fix the slot-1 winner v_1, then evaluate
          the score along a panel of unit directions in the deflation
          complement (orthogonal to v_1): the optimizer's slot-2 pick,
          oracle_v_j_proj_⊥ for each j, retained singular vectors_⊥,
          and a few random in-span directions. Decompose into gain/phi.
    When: To distinguish "phi explicitly avoids oracle" (M1 mechanism)
          from "rank-r carry pins slot-2" (M2 mechanism) and from
          "score-max is at the oracle but optimizer drifted off"
          (a plateau-drift mechanism, see §3 of overview).
    How:  Currently implemented inline in
          combined_objective_sketch_bias_probe.py::slot2_landscape_block1.
          For other blocks / other score variants, copy and adapt — the
          template is small (~50 lines).
    Generalizes to rank r: replace "deflation complement of v_1" with
          "deflation complement of {v_1,…,v_{k-1}}" and probe slot k
          for each k = 2..r. Especially useful for slot r since that's
          where M2 bites hardest (smallest residual signal).


5. Reference candidates ALWAYS produced per-block
--------------------------------------------------

The per-block diagnostic computes these for every block; they form the
reference frame for ANY new score:

  combined_v1, combined_v2          V_default[:, 0..1] from combined-step
  sketch_v1, sketch_v2              state.V columns (the carry directions)
  mgain_svd_v1, mgain_svd_v2        top-2 right SVs of [B_top; A_cur]
  hm_triplet_raw_best               row-of-A_fut cheat baseline
  oracle_v1_proj, oracle_v2_proj    V_exact[:, 0..1] projected into B_union
  c_evi_v1, c_evi_v2                c-weighted HM-evi optimizer
  r_sk_g_S?_best                    each variant's optimizer
  r_sk_g_S?_v1_full, _v2_deflate    each variant's sequential rank-2

The two ABSOLUTE references:
  oracle_v_proj  is the BEST v in B_union pointed at the true SV. No
                 value-only score can score-rank it above hm_triplet_raw
                 if A_fut has a single dominant row.
  hm_triplet_raw_best  is the worst-case "row cheat" baseline. If the
                       new score puts hm_triplet above oracle_v_proj on
                       any block, the score is exploitable.


6. Three diagnostic invariants per row
---------------------------------------

  align_v?_proj   cos²(v, oracle_v?_proj) — does v point at the oracle?
  state_align     ‖V_state^T v‖² — how much of v lies in the carry direction?
  Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw) per variant — exploitability check.

state_align is particularly useful: combined typically has it 0.5–1.0 on
mid-late blocks (combined sits in span(state.V)); oracle has it lower
because v_exact is partly in the part NOT in the carry.


6b. Per-candidate component breakdown (the FIRST thing to look at)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

When a candidate ranks unexpectedly low/high under a new score, decompose
the score into its u-components per candidate before doing anything else:

  cand            u_sk (or F2/raw_sk)   u_g1 (F2/raw_g1)   u_g2 (F2/raw_g2)   score
  oracle_v2_proj  0.282                 0.0072             0.0072             0.0106
  c_evi_v1        0.170                 0.0107             0.0108             0.0156
  combined_v2     0.440                 0.0034             6.7e-7             2.0e-6

Reading: oracle_v2 has the second-highest u_sk but its u_g1/u_g2 are
half of c_evi's → HM3 gets dragged down by the smallest term → c_evi
beats oracle on score despite worse sketch alignment. This is the
"smallest-link" diagnosis.

Display convention: the user-facing form is F2/raw_X (= 1/u_X), large
when v captures little energy in matrix X. The two views are dual; pick
the one that highlights the dragging term most clearly.

Sort-by-score table convention: print candidates in DESCENDING score
order, with rank in the leftmost column. Where the oracle / c_evi land
in the order is the first thing to read.

frob_hm3_score_diagnostic.py emits exactly this layout for S6.


6c. Cross-candidate component table (when designing a new u_sk)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Pin the candidate panel (sketch_v1, mgain_svd_v1, oracle_v_proj,
c_evi_v1, combined_v1) and walk the BLOCK axis. For each fixed candidate,
plot how (u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) evolves over blocks 1, 2, 6, 12, 31. This
reveals when each component "kicks in" and how matrix hardness modulates
its growth — exactly what carry-confidence variants (Family B in the
score-ideas spec) want to encode.

Not yet wrapped in a script; produce ad-hoc from frob_norm_diagnostic.py
output.


7. Counterexample matrix suite
-------------------------------

Each matrix stresses a different failure mode. A new variant has to be
tested across the suite, not just on one matrix. Numbers below are
final cos1² (slot-2) at half_win=32, sliding mode, block 31, from
summary/bench_matrix_sweep/synthesis.txt (online, isvd, hybrid,
combined) and summary/bench_matrix_sweep_r_sk_g_S6/synthesis.txt (S6).
"online" = future_hmean_online = current production baseline.

  matrix                  combined  hybrid  isvd   online  S6     gap-to-beat
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  static-cex              0.913     —       0.044  0.819   0.025  online
  mixed-tail-sharp        0.027     0.030   0.013  0.850   0.013  online
  mixed-tail-balanced     0.044     —       0.060  0.827   0.0004 online
  mixed-tail-soft         —         0.123   0.003  0.019   0.022  combined/hybrid
  diffuse-diffuse         —         —       0.489  0.528   0.005  online ≈ iSVD
  etf-basket-basis        0.000     —       0.205  0.962   0.652  online
  residual-spiky-shocks   —         0.570   0.637  0.246   0.266  iSVD/hybrid
  risk-residual-panel     —         0.085   0.471  0.064   —      iSVD/hybrid

Per-matrix failure modes and what each tests:

  static-cex                The structured-counterexample matrix.
                            Combined nails it (cos1²=0.913). iSVD fails
                            (carry latches onto the wrong direction).
                            Online matches combined (0.819). S6 fails
                            (0.025 — finds a high-S6 v that is *not* the
                            second SVD direction; P4 plateau drift).
                            Tests "does the new score preserve combined's
                            counterexample handling?"

  mixed-tail-sharp          Combined fails slot-2 (0.027) due to
                            mechanisms M1 (phi de-rewards concentrated
                            oracle responses) and M2 (rank-r carry pins
                            slot-2 to span(V_state)) — see §1bis of
                            score_design_overview.txt. iSVD ignores
                            slot-2 entirely. Online clears it (0.850).
                            S6 still fails (0.013) — narrow basin.
                            Tests "does the new score escape the wide
                            HM3 plateau and beat online's slot-2?"

  diffuse-diffuse           iSVD wins on slot-2 (0.489). Combined fails
                            entirely (cos0² = 0.085) because it is
                            *blind to A_fut*: combined maximizes
                            ‖Bv‖² + ‖A_cur v‖² so its slot-1 is top SV
                            of A_cur, which is a LOCAL noise direction
                            on diffuse rowspace; the population top SV
                            (oracle) is the v that is balanced across
                            A_cur AND A_fut. This is mechanism M3 —
                            the original motivation for adding A_fut to
                            the future_hmean / r_sk_g family. iSVD wins
                            because its carry update integrates across
                            blocks. Online ties iSVD (0.528 vs 0.489).
                            S6 fails (0.005) — same plateau drift.
                            Tests "does the new score recover the iSVD
                            regime when the rowspace is uniform?"

  residual-spiky-shocks     iSVD/hybrid win (0.637/0.570). Online
                            underperforms (0.246) because HM-online
                            misreads the spike as low-entropy noise.
                            S6 slightly above online (0.266) but still
                            far from iSVD. Tests spiky-row sensitivity;
                            this is where a row-concentration guard
                            (relH1 / F1) is the natural fix per the
                            multi-matrix synthesis.

  risk-residual-panel       Same regime as residual-spiky-shocks. iSVD
                            wins (0.471). Online fails (0.064).

  etf-basket-basis          Online wins decisively (0.962). S6 second
                            (0.652 — the strong score-design signal).
                            Combined fails on slot-2 (0.000); tests
                            "where does the F-weighted balance pay off
                            relative to combined's local-A_cur fit?"

  mixed-tail-soft           Hybrid edges out (0.123). Tests soft-tail
                            behavior; less stress than -sharp.

  mixed-tail-balanced       Online wins (0.827). Tests balanced-tail
                            regime; combined's slot-2 fails as in
                            -sharp.

Two distinct regimes for the score-design line:
  - Tail-dominant (mixed-tail-*, static-cex, etf-basket-basis,
    diffuse-diffuse): online is the SOTA baseline. S6 must close the
    gap. The 4 tail-dominant matrices in the §6 table of
    score_design_overview.txt are the operational target.
  - Spiky-residual (residual-spiky-shocks, risk-residual-panel):
    iSVD/hybrid still win; online itself underperforms here. S6 is
    *competitive* with online but not iSVD. F1 (relH1 / row-
    concentration guard) is the open lever.

8. What the toolkit DOESN'T have yet
-------------------------------------

The gaps to fill, especially relevant when generalizing to rank-r:

(a) Subspace-level alignment metric. Currently we have pairwise cos² with
    each oracle column. For rank-r we need principal angles between
    span(V_opt) and span(V_oracle).

(b) Optimizer trajectory tracker. We log final positions, not paths.
    For Stiefel ascent on a flat-plateau score we'll want score and
    principal-angles every iteration to see drift.

(c) Plateau-width quantifier. CLOSED 2026-04-28 (INFRA-05) by
    plateau_width_probe.py — see §4 entry above and
    summary/infra_plateau_width/synthesis.md. Verdict: basin on the
    canonical 3-matrix probe set is "wide-but-multimodal-and-displaced"
    at r=1; mixed-tail-sharp opt min_cos² = 0.000 at b31. The probe
    becomes the FAM-01 baseline: re-run with --rank>1 after the lift
    to verify plateau shrinks.

(d) Stiefel FD gradient check. The current --gradient-check is single-v
    only. Rank-r will need column-wise FD with tangent-space projection.

(e) Rank-r row-cheat baseline. hm_triplet_raw_best is slot-2 only. The
    rank-r row-cheat ("top-r rows of A_fut") doesn't exist as a candidate
    yet — needed to keep the exploitability check meaningful at r > 2.

(f) Hyperparameter sweep harness. We hand-pick alpha/beta/gamma per
    variant; no automated grid search across the matrix suite.

(g) Carry-trajectory probe. For each matrix in the suite, dump per-block
    s_top, sk_F2_low, spectral_concentration (s_1²/Σs_i²), and carry
    drift (‖V_t V_t^T − V_{t−1} V_{t−1}^T‖_F). Directly informs
    Family-B confidence multipliers (Q5 in score_design_overview.txt).
    Empirics so far (mixed-tail-sharp): s_top 0.91→0.99, sk_F2_low 1.66→
    1.93 (bounded — rank-r truncation absorbs only top-r structure).
    Diffuse vs counterexample matrices should show different growth
    profiles; quantify before picking the multiplier shape.

    c_evi_v1, sketch_v1) plot (u_sk, u_g1, u_g2) per block per matrix.
    Reveals when each component crosses the noise floor and how the
    crossing time depends on matrix hardness. Should show: u_sk for
    sketch_v1 climbs fastest on low-rank-effective matrices, slowest
    on diffuse-diffuse.

(i) Subspace-level row-cheat baseline. Currently hm_triplet_raw_best
    only knows about a single dominant row. The rank-r analogue needs
    "top-r rows of A_fut" as a frame baseline so the exploitability
    invariant (Δ(oracle − cheat) > 0) survives the rank-r lift.

(j) Slot-r-specific stress. M1 (phi penalty on concentrated responses)
    and M2 (rank-r carry pins slot to span(V_state)) both predict the
    failure scales with slot index k under greedy deflation: slot 1
    wins on dominant signal; slot r has the most-deflated complement
    AND the most-concentrated remaining oracle, so phi penalty is
    maximal AND state-align constraint is tightest. The deflation-
    complement landscape probe should be run at slot k = 2..r and
    plotted as "phi drop vs k" — predicted shape: monotonically widening
    gap between phi(combined_v_k) and phi(oracle_v_k_proj_⊥). If this
    plot shows the gap is flat in k, M1 is not the dominant mechanism
    at higher rank and the priority ordering in §7 of the overview
    needs revisiting.


9. Recommended workflow for a new score variant
------------------------------------------------

  Step 1: Implement value-and-grad in r_sk_g_score.py:r_sk_g_value_grad.
          Add to gradient_check loop.
  Step 2: Run --gradient-check on 2 matrices × 4 blocks, float64.
          Acceptance: rel < 1e-7 everywhere.
  Step 3: Wire into analyze_block (per-block diagnostic). Run T2 on
          static-cex / mixed-tail-sharp / diffuse-diffuse, blocks
          1/2/6/12/31, with --no-oracle-warmstart.
  Step 4: Inspect: Δ(oracle_v2 − hm_triplet_raw), align_v?_proj of the
          optimizer's best, oracle's rank under score_new in each block.
          STOP if oracle is below hm_triplet on any block.
  Step 5: Wire into half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py streaming
          policy (the future_hmean_r_sk_g branch). Add the new variant
          to --rsk-variant choices.
  Step 6: Run T3 streaming bench on the 7-matrix suite, half_win=32.
          Compare cos1², tail_mass against combined / iSVD baselines.
  Step 7: Write a synthesis.txt mirroring
          summary/bench_matrix_sweep_r_sk_g_S5_sketchinit/synthesis.txt.
          Decide: ship, iterate, or kill.

Roughly 30 minutes wall-clock for steps 1–6 once the score and grad are
written down (most of it is parallelized streaming bench).
