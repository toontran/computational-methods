future_hmean_online v2 — candidate-pool + rank-2 SVD patch synthesis
====================================================================

Date: 2026-04-23


Recommendations executed
------------------------
From `summary/joint_win32_vanishing_analysis.txt` and
`summary/future_hmean_online_synthesis.txt`:

  A. Align the online candidate pool to the benchmark's five-candidate set.
     Before: {opt2, q2_vs_q1oracle, q2_raw_projected, mgain_deflated_svd,
              opt2_outside}
     After:  {opt2, q2_vs_q1oracle, mgain_deflated_svd,
              block_complement(A_half1), prev_opt2}

  B. Replace the Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization of (v_1, chosen_v2) with
     a rank-2 SVD step inside span{v_1, chosen_v2}:
         C = [v_1, chosen_v2]
         Qc, _ = qr(C)
         _, _, Vt = svd(M_gain @ Qc)
         V_selected = Qc @ Vt.T
     This preserves the 2D span but reorders the frame by local singular
     dominance on M_gain, avoiding the step-27 cancellation.

Both changes applied only to the `future_hmean_online` (and
`future_hmean_online_joint`) policies. `combined`, `half_hmean`,
`half_hmean_guard` retain their previous behaviour.


Patched file
------------
`half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py`:
  - `build_candidates` now also emits `block_complement` (block_svd_complement
    on A_half1) and `prev_opt2` (orthogonalised against v_1). The full
    candidate dictionary still carries `half2_complement`, `q2_raw_projected`,
    `opt2_outside` so `combined` / `half_hmean*` policies are unchanged.
  - Online policies filter to `ONLINE_POOL =
    ('opt2', 'q2_vs_q1oracle', 'mgain_deflated_svd',
     'block_complement', 'prev_opt2')`.
  - New helper `rank2_svd_frame(v1, chosen_v2, M_gain, rank)` returns the
    rank-2 SVD-ordered frame; online policies use it in place of
    `probe.orthonormalize_columns`. Falls back to Gram-Schmidt if the 2D
    span is rank-deficient.
  - Every row in the CSV/JSON now carries `svd_frame_used` (0/1).

Step-27 crash: resolved
-----------------------
The pre-patch diagnostic at half_win=32 showed V_selected[:,1] losing
V_exact[:,0] at step 27 (ex2 crashed from 0.399 to 0.015 and stayed there
through step 31). Post-patch, steps 24..31 look like:

  step  selected         svd_used   ex1     ex2     oracle_mass
   24   opt2             1          0.877   0.582   0.999
   25   opt2             1          0.893   0.609   0.999
   26   opt2             1          0.909   0.635   0.999
   27   opt2             1          0.922   0.660   0.999
   28   opt2             1          0.937   0.684   0.999
   29   opt2             1          0.951   0.707   0.999
   30   opt2             1          0.966   0.728   0.999
   31   opt2             1          0.979   0.749   1.000

Two mechanisms contribute: (1) the Gram-Schmidt cancellation is replaced by
a rank-2 SVD step that reorders the span by singular dominance — the
V_exact[:,0] component cannot be wiped because it is a strong singular
direction of M_gain inside the span; (2) `q2_raw_projected`, the candidate
that was causing the co-linear pick at step 27, is no longer in the online
pool, so the greedy never chooses it. Either change alone likely suffices,
but they were applied together per the recommendations.


Summary metrics
---------------
- half_win=32 sliding: mean_align 0.186 → 0.749 (+0.563), exact_cos2 0.178 →
  0.351 (+0.173), mean_oracle_mass 0.898 → 0.934 (+0.036), relerr 0.041 →
  0.0051 (~8x better).
- half_win=64 sliding: mean_align 0.425 → 0.690 (+0.265), exact_cos2 0.219
  → 0.332 (+0.113), mean_oracle_mass 0.975 → 0.898 (-0.077), relerr 0.042
  → 0.0072 (~6x better).

The subspace-quality metrics (mean_align, exact_cos2, relerr_sval) improve
at both sizes. The mean_winner_oracle_mass moves in opposite directions:
closer to benchmark at half_win=32 (+0.036), but away from benchmark at
half_win=64 (-0.077). At half_win=64 the label distribution shifts toward
`opt2` (9/15 vs 3/15 pre-patch) — removing `q2_raw_projected` and
`opt2_outside` from the pool reshapes the max-gain normaliser and lets
`opt2` win more often even when a q2-like pick would be better.

This residual gap is the exact mechanism the parent synthesis flagged
("...when `opt2_outside` has high current-block gain it can inflate
max_gain1, shrinking shares for the oracle candidate and occasionally
letting `opt2` win the HM."). Aligning the pool removed the
`opt2_outside` inflator, which explains why opt2 wins *more* at
half_win=64, not less. Sample sizes at half_win=64 are small (15
decisions), so this effect is noisy.


Remaining gap and possible next steps
-------------------------------------
1. `mean_winner_oracle_mass` at half_win=32 is 0.934 vs benchmark 0.970 —
   a 3.6-point residual. The pre-patch residual was 7.2 points; the patch
   has closed half of it.

2. Candidate pool is now aligned with the benchmark on counts and
   identity, but the score numerator/denominator (max-normalisation of
   gain1, gain2) still uses the online pool's current-half shares, not
   future-block gain. Swapping `relH1` for a future-block entropy proxy
   (e.g. using the carried-state singular vector gain instead of A_half1
   entropy) could close more of the gap while staying online.

3. At half_win=64 the patch improves subspace quality but worsens
   `mean_winner_oracle_mass` because opt2 crowds out q2 wins. Worth
   checking whether adding a mild q2-preference tiebreaker (or applying
   the max-normalisation over a more stable candidate set) recovers the
   pre-patch half_win=64 oracle_mass while keeping the half_win=32 gains.

4. The rank-2 SVD reframe could in principle be worth applying to the
   `combined` / `half_hmean*` policies too. Not tested here; would need a
   separate ablation to confirm it doesn't disturb cases where the
   Gram-Schmidt behaviour is currently benign.


Bottom line
-----------
- The step-27 V_exact[:,0] collapse at half_win=32 sliding is fully
  resolved. mean_align jumps 0.186 → 0.749.
- mean_winner_oracle_mass closes about half the gap to the benchmark at
  half_win=32 (0.898 → 0.934 vs target 0.970).
- At half_win=64 the patch improves subspace alignment but shifts label
  counts toward opt2, lowering oracle_mass on this small-sample test.
- Root cause of residual gap: candidate pool is aligned, but the
  max-normalisation denominator in the HM score still rewards the online
  pool's opt2 too strongly.
