Matrix benchmark and objective-development summary
==================================================

Purpose
-------
This note summarizes the recent benchmark recheck and connects it to the
existing oracle-projection, joint-Stiefel, and row-regularization notes. The
goal is to give a compact starting point for understanding the non-CEX matrix
families and deciding whether to change the restricted algorithm or its score
objective.


Current implementation state
----------------------------
The restricted optimizer still defaults to the original backward-compatible
greedy path:

  --basis-selection greedy

The work-in-progress options now include:

  --basis-selection joint
  --joint-warm-start-greedy
  --joint-warm-start-oracle
  --row-concentration-lambda L

The oracle warm start uses:

  Q_oracle = orth(P_row(M_gain) V_exact[:, :rank])

and adds this as a Stiefel restart seed labeled:

  oracle-projected

This is diagnostic/oracle-assisted, not a deployable online method, because it
uses V_exact.

The row-concentration regularizer optimizes:

  score_reg(v) = score(v) - lambda * sum_i p_i^2
  p_i = (A_block v)_i^2 / sum_j (A_block v)_j^2

The raw score outputs remain backward compatible. Regularized scores are
available in:

  diag["regularized_score"]
  diag["regularized_score_sum"]


Benchmark context
-----------------
Original benchmark file:

  benchmarks.txt

Old baseline of interest:

  n=128, win=32, rank=2, preset=small, seed=0
  restricted = low-budget simple greedy restricted algorithm

The new recheck used a medium-budget joint restricted algorithm:

  q0=8
  qmax=48
  num_restarts=8
  maxit=120
  expansion_maxit=8
  post_expansion_maxit=80
  dtype=float32
  reduced_optimizer=cex

Runner:

  python run_joint_warmstart_benchmarks.py

The recheck tested two methods:

  joint-greedywarm-medium
  joint-oraclewarm-medium


High-level benchmark result
---------------------------
The medium joint algorithm improves the old greedy restricted result most
clearly on static-cex:

  method                    align       relerr
  ------------------------------------------------
  old restricted             0.344369    0.00252601
  joint-greedywarm-medium    0.201402    0.00010514
  joint-oraclewarm-medium    0.171806    0.00071261

This supports the idea that the low-budget greedy restricted algorithm
understated the potential of the restricted approach. A future implementation
could plausibly include joint optimization at much lower marginal cost.

For most documented quant families, medium joint gives small improvements or
near-identical results relative to old restricted:

  alternative-data-signals
  futures-term-structure
  crowded-strategy
  rates-cross-currency
  etf-basket-basis
  execution-cost-slippage
  stat-arb-spreads

The improvements are usually modest, which means the old greedy restricted
result was already close to the target on these matrices.

Two regressions are important:

  risk-residual-panel
    old restricted             align 0.345120, relerr 0.02982747
    joint-greedywarm-medium    align 0.672373, relerr 0.05191749
    joint-oraclewarm-medium    align 0.566750, relerr 0.04779328

  intraday-liquidity-shape
    old restricted             align 0.004775, relerr 0.00173719
    joint-greedywarm-medium    align 0.009190, relerr 0.00146502
    joint-oraclewarm-medium    align 0.009190, relerr 0.00146502

On intraday-liquidity-shape, the joint methods improve singular-value error
but worsen vector alignment. On risk-residual-panel, both metrics worsen.


Matrix interpretation from matrices.txt
---------------------------------------
static-cex:
  Dense, flat leading Hadamard-like signal directions compete with many
  localized close-magnitude tail directions. The big improvement from joint
  optimization is consistent with the idea that the greedy low-budget path was
  selecting columns myopically and missing a better rank-2 carried subspace.

Alternative-data-signals:
  Persistent search/spend factors compete with delayed and stale-source
  artifacts. Joint optimization gives a small improvement, suggesting the
  original greedy path mostly kept the persistent factor.

Futures-term-structure:
  Near-tied level/slope curve modes compete with roll-pressure shocks and
  maturity-local jumps. Joint and oracle-warm results are identical here,
  suggesting the objective landscape is benign once the right row space is
  available.

Crowded-strategy:
  Persistent crowded trade and staggered unwind modes compete with sparse
  crowded-name bursts. Joint gives a small improvement. This is a likely case
  where a continuity/carry-aware objective might help more than pure joint
  optimization.

Rates-cross-currency:
  Funding and carry modes compete with crisis/fixing windows and localized
  basis jumps. Medium joint barely changes results, so the low-budget greedy
  algorithm was already adequate.

Options-vol-surface:
  Smooth surface level/term/skew modes compete with localized volatility
  events and quote microstructure. Joint is essentially neutral/slightly worse.
  The objective may already be near the same optimum, or alignment may be
  harsh under near-tied smooth factors.

Risk-residual-panel:
  Persistent crowding/liquidity residual books compete with sector-local
  idiosyncratic pulses, event volatility scaling, and heavy-tailed residual
  noise. The joint score objective appears to chase a rank-2 score optimum
  induced by transient/localized residual structure. This is the strongest
  case for changing the objective.

Macro-factor-panel:
  Inflation/growth/rates/dollar modes compete with macro-release stripes and
  localized event shocks. Joint is nearly neutral. This looks like a stable
  factor-subspace case where objective changes are not urgent.

Realized-vol-corr:
  Market-vol and correlation-cluster modes compete with event windows and
  localized correlation bursts. Joint is nearly neutral and slightly worsens
  alignment while slightly improving scale.

ETF-basket-basis:
  Dense basket mode competes with sector/constituent dislocations. Joint gives
  a small improvement, consistent with static-cex-like dense signal plus
  localized tail pressure, but the old greedy path was already strong.

Execution-cost-slippage:
  Persistent liquidity/market-impact modes compete with venue, queue, and
  auction artifacts. Joint gives a tiny improvement. This may need temporal
  continuity rather than a stronger per-block score optimizer.

Stat-arb-spreads:
  Mean-reversion and crowded-residual modes compete with local spread-breakdown
  pulses. Joint gives a small improvement, but structured transient pressure
  remains relevant.

Intraday-liquidity-shape:
  Persistent U-shaped liquidity and auction-pressure directions compete with
  venue bursts, halt-like stripes, and other microstructure events. The joint
  methods improve scale but worsen alignment, suggesting the score objective
  may rotate toward transient liquidity shapes that help singular value
  recovery but hurt exact top-vector alignment.


Important lesson from oracle warm start
---------------------------------------
Oracle warm start is not an upper bound on algorithm quality. It initializes
from the projection of the exact top subspace into the current row space, but
the entropy score can still move away from that frame.

This was already visible in the oracle-gradient notes:

  - Q_oracle is geometrically close to the desired carried subspace.
  - Unrestricted entropy-score optimization can increase score by leaving
    Q_oracle.
  - The outward incentive is strongest in early blocks and becomes much
    smaller near the final block.

In the medium benchmark recheck, oracle warm start usually matches greedy warm
start. On static-cex it improves alignment but has worse singular-value error
than greedy warm start. That tradeoff is another sign that the score objective
and exact SVD recovery target are not identical.


Working hypotheses
------------------
1. The old greedy restricted benchmarks are a lower bound on what the
   restricted method can do. Joint optimization can substantially improve
   static-cex and mildly improve several quant families.

2. The main remaining failures are probably objective failures, not just
   optimizer failures. Risk-residual-panel is the strongest evidence: a more
   powerful joint optimizer makes the result worse.

3. The entropy score rewards some localized or transient row structure,
   especially in matrices with heteroskedastic residual pulses or
   microstructure bursts.

4. Oracle projection is a useful diagnostic target, but optimizing the current
   entropy score from that target can still move away from the exact SVD
   target.

5. Different matrix families may need different regularization:

   - Dense signal plus localized tail pressure:
       static-cex, etf-basket-basis
       likely benefits from joint optimization and row concentration control.

   - Persistent near-tied smooth factors plus event artifacts:
       futures-term-structure, options-vol-surface, macro-factor-panel,
       realized-vol-corr
       likely needs stable subspace/carry preservation more than stronger
       local optimization.

   - Heteroskedastic/local residual or microstructure bursts:
       risk-residual-panel, intraday-liquidity-shape,
       execution-cost-slippage, stat-arb-spreads
       likely needs row/leverage regularization and/or temporal continuity.


Candidate objective changes
---------------------------
1. Row-concentration penalty:

   score_reg(v) = score(v) - lambda * sum_i p_i^2

   This directly penalizes current-window response energy concentrated in a few
   rows. First candidates:

     lambda = 1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2

   Most relevant targets:

     risk-residual-panel
     intraday-liquidity-shape
     static-cex
     etf-basket-basis

2. Leverage-weighted row penalty:

   score_reg(v) = score(v) - lambda * sum_i leverage_i p_i

   Possible leverage definitions:

     row norm of A_block
     leverage in current block top singular span
     leverage in M_gain row structure
     historical high-frequency row influence

   Most relevant when a few known rows or event windows dominate the outward
   score gradient.

3. Carry/continuity penalty:

   Penalize large movement away from the previous carried subspace unless the
   score gain is large enough. This may fit matrices with persistent factors
   plus transient structured shocks.

   Most relevant targets:

     crowded-strategy
     execution-cost-slippage
     intraday-liquidity-shape
     stat-arb-spreads

4. Oracle-alignment diagnostic, not deployable objective:

   Continue using Q_oracle for debugging:

     found_vs_Q_oracle
     outside_norm at Q_oracle
     score difference between direct-Q_oracle and unrestricted optimization

   This identifies whether a regression is due to search failure or objective
   mismatch.


Recommended next experiments
----------------------------
1. Run medium joint with row-concentration regularization:

   python run_joint_warmstart_benchmarks.py \
     --row-concentration-lambda 0.001

2. Focus first on regression cases:

   python run_joint_warmstart_benchmarks.py \
     --matrices risk-residual-panel,intraday-liquidity-shape \
     --row-concentration-lambda 0.001

3. Sweep lambda for the most important cases:

   matrices:
     static-cex
     risk-residual-panel
     intraday-liquidity-shape
     etf-basket-basis

   lambdas:
     0, 1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2

   compare:
     final_align
     final_relerr
     score_sum
     regularized_score_sum
     found_vs_Q_oracle where available
     outside_norm at Q_oracle where available

4. Add row diagnostics for the regression cases:

   For each block and winning frame:

     top rows by |normalized row dot v|
     row concentration sum_i p_i^2
     block row norm leverage
     overlap with current block top singular vectors

   Goal:
     determine whether risk-residual-panel and intraday-liquidity-shape
     regress because of a small number of event rows or because the rank-2
     score objective is selecting a genuinely different smooth subspace.

5. Keep greedy baseline in every table.

   A stronger optimizer is not automatically better. The old greedy path can
   act as an implicit regularizer by not fully optimizing the transient-heavy
   score objective.
