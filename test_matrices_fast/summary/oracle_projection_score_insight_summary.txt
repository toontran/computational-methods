Best non-oracle configuration
-----------------------------
The best real, non-oracle configuration found so far is high-random joint
Stiefel with residual expansion:

  python cex_restricted_space_probe.py \
    --matrix static-cex --mode restricted --dtype float64 --carry left \
    --reduced-optimizer cex --expansion-direction residual \
    --n 128 --win 32 --rank 2 --preset cex-replicate \
    --q0 4 --qmax 32 --krylov-depth 2 --residual-tol 1e-4 \
    --expansion-maxit 8 --num-restarts 64 --maxit 1000 --tol 1e-10 \
    --post-expansion-maxit 500 --reuse-line-search-grad --expansion-warm-start \
    --basis-selection joint --joint-solver riemannian --verbose

Observed result:
  mean_align    0.999259
  rel_err_sval  0.00005185
  elapsed       about 53.5 seconds

This improvement is path-dependent: earlier carried states become much better,
not because the final-block optimizer alone finds a better basin from the old
greedy state.


Key geometric target
--------------------
Let

  M_gain = current window+sketch matrix
  V_exact[:, :2] = exact full-matrix top-2 right singular subspace
  Q_oracle = orth(P_row(M_gain) V_exact[:, :2])

where P_row(M_gain) is projection onto the current M_gain row space.

The found high-random rank-2 carried subspace is extremely close to Q_oracle:

  block    found_vs_Q_oracle principal cosines
  ------------------------------------------------
  1:32     0.999099, 0.806759
  33:64    0.999722, 0.990456
  65:96    0.999879, 0.998463
  97:128   0.999977, 0.999518

By contrast, closeness to the top-2 right singular subspace of M_gain itself is
much weaker in the second direction until the last block:

  block    found_vs_top2(M_gain)
  ------------------------------
  1:32     0.503144, 0.186707
  33:64    0.758955, 0.222245
  65:96    0.996579, 0.281224
  97:128   0.999978, 0.999792

Interpretation:
  The successful trajectory is not simply following top2(M_gain) early.
  It is tracking the projection of the global exact top-2 right singular
  subspace into the currently available row space.


Winning initialization geometry
-------------------------------
For the high-random run, the winning initial seeds were:

  block    initial_best       final_best
  ------------------------------------------
  1:32     random-17#18       expansion-warm#1
  33:64    random-33#35       expansion-warm#1
  65:96    prior-carried#1    expansion-warm#1
  97:128   random-40#42       expansion-warm#1

The "final_best = expansion-warm" label means that after subspace expansion,
the optimizer mostly continues from its previous expansion-stage solution.
The more meaningful origin of the basin is the initial_best label.

The initial winning seeds were often close to the local M_gain SVD structure
and row-like early in the stream. But the final found carried subspace after
optimization/expansion is much closer to Q_oracle than to top2(M_gain).

Objective mismatch test
-----------------------
Hypothesis tested:
  Maybe the global entropy-score maximizer lies inside Q_oracle, and the
  optimizer only needs to rotate within that subspace.

Test:
  Fix Q_oracle for each block, optimize only the 2D rotation inside Q_oracle,
  and compare the best in-subspace score against unrestricted joint
  optimization initialized from Q_oracle.

Results:

  block    best score inside Q_oracle   unrestricted score   unrestricted - oracle
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1:32     1.796559106                  1.801291644          +4.73e-03
  33:64    1.852910035                  1.855684956          +2.77e-03
  65:96    1.906110404                  1.906261965          +1.52e-04
  97:128   1.957572691                  1.957604883          +3.22e-05

Conclusion:
  The hypothesis is nearly true geometrically but false as a score
  maximization statement. The unrestricted score optimizer can increase the
  entropy score slightly by leaving Q_oracle. The outward incentive is largest
  in early blocks and becomes tiny by the final block.

What is special about rows 27, 32, 13, 25, 9?
---------------------------------------------
For static-cex, A is generated as

  A_unpermuted = U diag(s) V'
  A = A_unpermuted[p, :]

The first two left singular vectors are global Hadamard directions. Each tail
left singular vector has one large row coordinate. Tail singular values decay
slowly. Therefore rows corresponding to early tail coordinates are high-norm
and high-leverage when they appear in an early streaming window.

For the selected first-block rows:

  stream row   original row   row norm rank   outside-dir cos rank   block top2 leverage rank
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  27           1              1               1                      1
  32           9              2               2                      2
  13           11             3               3                      3
  25           15             5               4                      4
  9            29             6               5                      5

All have the same sign in the first two top-left Hadamard/global directions:

  Utop1 = +0.088388
  Utop2 = +0.088388

Interpretation:
  These are not arbitrary rows. They are high-energy, high-leverage tail rows
  that landed in the first window under the row permutation. The entropy score
  is reacting to this local row-energy structure and tilting away from the
  oracle projected top-2 subspace.


Current working interpretation
------------------------------
1. The target that explains successful streaming behavior is not top2(M_gain)
   itself, but Q_oracle = orth(P_row(M_gain) V_exact[:, :2]).

2. High-random joint succeeds because some random starts land in a basin that
   tracks Q_oracle through the stream.

3. The entropy objective is close to aligned with this oracle-projection target
   but is not exactly aligned. It rewards small outward deviations, especially
   in early blocks with high-leverage local tail rows.

4. Direct use of Q_oracle is perfect by construction as a diagnostic, but not
   available in production because it uses V_exact.

5. A better practical objective/optimizer should probably:
     - preserve the oracle-projection-like subspace when found,
     - reduce early-block attraction to local high-leverage tail rows,
     - or regularize the entropy score against local row/leverage artifacts.


Recommended next experiments
----------------------------
1. Gradient decomposition at Q_oracle:
     Compute entropy-score gradient at Q_oracle and project it onto:
       - Q_oracle tangent rotations,
       - orthogonal complement of Q_oracle,
       - projected exact v3/v4,
       - block right singular vectors,
       - top-row span.
     Goal: confirm that the outward deviation is already a first-order ascent
     direction and identify its source.

2. Score tradeoff table:
     Compare score and alignment for:
       - direct Q_oracle,
       - best rotation inside Q_oracle,
       - unrestricted joint from Q_oracle,
       - unrestricted high-random joint,
       - old greedy.
     Do this block by block on the same carried state.

3. Local-row regularization:
     Try modifying the score objective to penalize concentration on a few rows
     or to discount high-leverage first-window tail rows.

4. Projected-target proxy:
     Seek a production proxy for Q_oracle. Candidate ideas:
       - use carried state plus residual expansion to approximate row-space
         projection of a stable global subspace,
       - stabilize across blocks using previous V rather than pure local M_gain
         top singular vectors,
       - add a selection rule that prefers candidates whose carried subspace is
         stable under row-space projection across adjacent blocks.

5. Formalize diagnostics:
     Add command-line flags for:
       - oracle projected-exact initialization,
       - fixed-Q_oracle rotation-only optimization,
       - score-gradient decomposition at Q_oracle,
       - per-block row-leverage/outside-direction reporting.
