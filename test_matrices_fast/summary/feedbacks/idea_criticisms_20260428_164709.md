# Idea criticisms — current_report.txt

## Summary verdict

The report's diagnosis that the combined score's argmax differs from $V_r(A)$ is correct and well-instrumented, but the framing is conceptually muddled in two load-bearing places. (i) M1, M2, M3 are not three independent failure mechanisms; they are three projections of one underlying fact — *no pointwise function of window-local energies/tails can identify a global eigendirection of $A^\top A$* — which makes the V1–V4 sweep a confirmation exercise rather than a discriminating test. (ii) The shift from "lower bound on $\|Av\|^2$" to "argmax-recover $V_r$" silently changes the success criterion in a way that throws away the only theorem the framework actually has, then declares the residual gap "structural" without ever measuring tightness of the original bound. The V4 oracle-injection diagnostic is close to tautological as written, and the "evidence sufficiency" notion is not coherent enough to falsify a candidate feature.

## Robust claims

- **The greedy-vs-joint audit ($|j-g|\le 0.0004$) is genuinely informative.** Appendix B.1 establishes that on V1/V2/V3 the optimisation gap is below $4\times 10^{-4}$ everywhere, so the residual $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ values cannot be blamed on per-direction decomposition. This rules out a real alternative hypothesis and is the strongest piece of the report.
- **M2 is real and mechanically explained.** The energy term reducing to $\|Bv\|^2$ once $\rho_{\mathrm{past}}\gtrsim 5$, with $\mathrm{state\_align}$ rising from $0.167\to 0.988$ across $b\in\{6,12,31\}$ on `mixed-tail-sharp` (A.2), is unambiguous. This is a genuine consequence of the carry construction and would survive any reframing.
- **The $E_2$ landscape-break finding ($\Delta\ge +0.122$ even with oracle anchor on `diffuse-diffuse`) is solid.** The high-budget joint sweep with three different $\hat v_1$ choices in A.4 makes the "balance-tuning relocates the peak" conclusion hard to escape.
- **The force-oracle ceiling table establishes reachability.** $1.95/2.00$ ceilings under `force_frame` show $B_{\mathrm{union}}$ contains both oracle directions; the regression is not a search-domain problem.

## Weakly supported claims

- **"Three failure mechanisms" framing.** M1, M2, M3 are presented as separable but they share a common root: the score is a pointwise function of window-local norms ($\ell_2, \ell_4$ on $Bv, A_w v, R v$). Any such function is invariant under operations that preserve those norms but rotate global geometry. M1 is "tail-of-$C_w v$ wins"; M2 is "tail-of-$Bv$ wins"; M3 is "tail-of-single-half-window wins". These are the same shape with different dominant slot. The report acknowledges this in the "common thread" placeholder paragraph but never operationalises the unification, and the V1–V4 sweep is designed as if M1/M2/M3 were independent.
- **"Evidence sufficiency" as a coherent test.** The report defines sufficiency as "$Z_{\mathrm{oracle}} = \arg\max_{Z\subset B_{\mathrm{union}}} S_{\mathcal E}(Z)$". But this is *exact* argmax-recovery. It is essentially never satisfied by any finite-dimensional pointwise feature on a generic matrix — there is always a perturbation direction inside $B_{\mathrm{union}}$ that nudges the score up. So V1, V2, V3 were guaranteed to "fail" before any experiment; the test has no positive instances. A coherent sufficiency notion would need a tolerance $\tau$ and a rationale for it (e.g. "$\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}<\tau$ implies streaming bench within $\epsilon$"); none is given.
- **HM_3 motivation.** HM_3 is introduced as the "smallest-link enforcer" but the report itself observes (¶ "balancing HM_3, and why it hit a wall") that the oracle is *not* balanced across slots — slot ratios run from $0.6\times$ to $412\times$ in A.4. So HM_3's whole virtue (penalising imbalance) actively works against the oracle on most of the test matrices. HM_3 is a fix-of-a-fix: it fixed the $A_w$-only failure mode of M3 but introduced a balance assumption that is empirically false.
- **V1 and V2 choice rationale.** The two H3-style additions feel arbitrary. V1 (multiply by $A_{\mathrm{fut}}^\top A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ Rayleigh) and V2 (current–future cosine) are each plausibly motivated in one sentence, but there is no sense in which they *exhaust* the natural extensions to $\{u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}}\}$. Why not $\langle Bv, A_w v\rangle$? Why not $u_{\mathrm{sk}}\cdot u_{\mathrm{fut}}$ alone? The sweep tests two specific points; failure of two points cannot license the conclusion "feature additions cannot close the gap" reached in §3.

## Probably wrong / overclaimed

- **"M1 is not a bug in the inequality, it is a consequence of using a lower bound for argmax."** This sentence is correct but used to dismiss something it shouldn't. The report never measures *how loose* the lower bound is at $V_r$ vs at $\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_2$. If the bound is tight at the oracle and loose elsewhere, the original framework was already healthy and one only needs to tighten the inequality. By switching success criteria the report has discarded the only quantity that has a theorem behind it. *This is the single biggest framing concession in the report and it is not defended.*
- **A.4 force-oracle table reading.** The text says "Forcing only $\hat v^{(S_6)}_2 \mapsto v^\star_2$ recovers $\approx 1.00$ of the $1.95$ ceiling on the six regression-prone matrices … the leading direction is also broken." But $1.00$ out of a $1.95$ ceiling for a *frame* score that sums two $\cos^2$ values means roughly that one direction is captured at $\approx 1.0$ and the other at $\approx 0.0$. If the second direction is forced to oracle, then the captured slot is $v^\star_2$ and $\hat v^{(S_6)}_1 \perp v^\star_1$. That is a *much* stronger statement than "the leading direction is broken" — it suggests $\hat v^{(S_6)}_1$ is approximately orthogonal to $v^\star_1$, not merely a poor approximation. The report does not interrogate this.
- **"$Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ may itself fail to lie in $B_{\mathrm{union}}$, in which case 'oracle recovery' refers to the projection of $V_r$ into $B_{\mathrm{union}}$."** This caveat is dropped in but never measured. The force-frame ceiling at $1.95$–$2.00$ suggests $V_r \subset B_{\mathrm{union}}$ in practice on these matrices, but there is no direct check (e.g. $\|(I-P_{B_{\mathrm{union}}})V_r\|_F$). On smaller half-windows or other matrices this may fail and silently shift the goalposts.
- **V4 as a diagnostic.** $S_4(Z) = S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z) + (\lambda/2)\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2$ is close to tautological: as $\lambda\to\infty$, the maximiser on $\mathrm{St}(d,2)\cap B_{\mathrm{union}}$ trivially approaches $V_r$ projected into $B_{\mathrm{union}}$. The "$\lambda$ at which the gap closes" therefore measures the *ratio* between HM gradient and oracle-projection gradient at the oracle, which is a feature of the score normalisation, not a meaningful sufficiency threshold. The fact that `diffuse-diffuse` needs $\lambda=0.5$ vs `mixed-tail-soft` $\lambda=0.1$ is not given a mechanistic interpretation, but the most natural one is that the score's gradient at $V_r$ is simply larger on `diffuse-diffuse`.
- **n=2 (St(d,2)) restriction.** Every probe runs at rank-2. M2 in particular is rank-sensitive — the carry $B$ stores rank-$r$ information, so if the experiments ran at $r=4$ or $r=8$, $\mathrm{state\_align}$ to $V_r(A)$ might recover, since $\mathrm{span}(V_{\mathrm{state}})$ would be a better match for $\mathrm{span}(V_r(A))$. The report implicitly treats St(d,2) findings as universal but never checks. If failures attenuate with $r$, the entire problem is "rank-2 is a starvation regime", which is a different fix.

## Reframings worth considering

1. **Revert to the lower-bound criterion and measure tightness.** Compute $\mathrm{score}_w(v) / \|Av\|_2^2$ at $v = v^\star_i$ and at $v = \hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i$ across blocks. If the bound is tight at oracle and loose elsewhere, the entire M1–M3 narrative is wrong-headed: the issue is bound-looseness, fixable by tightening (A1)/(A2) constants, not a design failure.
2. **Unified statement: pointwise-window-norm impossibility.** Conjecture: for any score $S(v)=f(\|Bv\|_2,\|Bv\|_4,\|A_w v\|_2,\|A_w v\|_4,\|Rv\|_2,\|Rv\|_4)$ with $f$ continuous, there exist matrices $A$ for which $\arg\max_v S \ne v^\star_1$ even when $V_r \subset B_{\mathrm{union}}$. If true (provable by a constructive counterexample using two matrices with identical row-norm distributions but different singular vectors), the V1–V4 sweep is testing the wrong question and *no* H3-style addition can succeed. This would reroute work to H2.
3. **Replace "evidence sufficiency" with "evidence-rate".** Instead of asking $\arg\max = V_r$, ask: how does $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ scale with half-window size $|A_{\mathrm{fut}}|$? If $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}} = O(1/\sqrt{m})$ the score is consistent and the right move is to enlarge the window. If $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is $O(1)$ in $m$, the score has an irreducible bias and only H2 fixes it.
4. **Strengthen V4: separate "feature is informative" from "feature is sufficient".** Add a $\lambda$-derivative diagnostic: $\partial_\lambda \|V_r^\top \hat Z(\lambda)\|_F^2$ at $\lambda=0$. This measures how much oracle-information the *existing* HM features already encode. A near-zero derivative means HM is locally orthogonal to the oracle direction, which is a much sharper failure mode than "argmax differs".

## Suggested next experiments

1. **Tightness audit of (\*).** For each test matrix and each block, log $\mathrm{score}_w(v^\star_i) / \|Av^\star_i\|_2^2$ and $\mathrm{score}_w(\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i)/\|A\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i\|_2^2$. If the oracle ratio is closer to 1 than the combined-argmax ratio, the inequality is *not* the problem and the report's pivot to argmax-recovery is unjustified.
2. **Rank sweep of M2.** Re-run A.2 at $r\in\{2,4,8,16\}$. If $\mathrm{state\_align}$ at the oracle recovers monotonically with $r$, M2 is a rank-2 starvation effect, not a structural defect.
3. **Constructive impossibility witness.** Build a matrix pair $(A, A')$ with identical row $\ell_2/\ell_4$ distributions per block but rotated $V_r$. If both produce identical $\mathrm{score}_w$ landscapes but different oracles, the pointwise-norm-feature class is provably insufficient and the V1/V2/V3 results follow from a one-line theorem. This kills H3 cleanly.
4. **Measure $V_r \cap B_{\mathrm{union}}$ directly.** Log $\sin^2\theta_{\max}(V_r, B_{\mathrm{union}})$ each block. If nonzero on any matrix, the oracle-projection caveat is load-bearing and §3's "ceiling 1.95" reading needs revisiting.
5. **Replace HM_3 with a balance-agnostic aggregator.** Geometric mean or just $u_{\mathrm{sk}}\cdot u_{\mathrm{cur}}\cdot u_{\mathrm{fut}}$ ($\log$-additive). If this restores the streaming bench on the three regression matrices, "HM_3 is a fix-of-a-fix" is confirmed and the whole $E_2$ episode was a wild goose chase.
6. **Power-iteration feature, properly tested.** §3's open-work item (b) gestures at this. Concretely: $u_{\mathrm{pow}}(Z) := \|A_w^\top A_w Z + A_{\mathrm{fut}}^\top A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z\|_F^2 / \mathrm{trace}(\cdot)$ approximates the leading invariant subspace of $A^\top A$ from streaming data. This is the *minimal* H2-style addition; it should be run through the V3-style sweep before any other work continues, since if it fails the entire H3 program is dead and the report's "next steps" need re-prioritising.

## Severity / confidence / scope ranking

Sorted by severity, then by confidence within each tier.

### Fatal

- **Fatal (Likely, Core).** Pivot from value-lower-bound to argmax-recovery throws away the only theorem the framework has, with no measurement of bound tightness at $V_r$ vs at $\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}$. (§"Probably wrong" item 1.) If tightness is fine at the oracle, M1–M3 dissolve into a non-issue. Demoted to Major if the tightness audit (next-step 1) confirms the bound is loose at the oracle.
- **Fatal (Speculative, Core).** "Evidence sufficiency" as a binary $\arg\max = V_r$ test has no positive instances on generic matrices, so V1/V2/V3 were guaranteed to "fail" before any data was collected. Without a tolerance $\tau$ tied to streaming-bench impact, the sweep cannot discriminate features. (Weakly-supported item 2.)

### Major

- **Major (Likely, Core).** M1/M2/M3 are not orthogonal failure mechanisms — they are three projections of "no pointwise function of window-local norms identifies a global eigendirection." The V1–V4 design treats them as independent and so cannot rule out the unified explanation. (Weakly-supported item 1; Reframing 2.)
- **Major (Likely, Method).** $\mathrm{HM}_3$ penalises imbalance, but the oracle is empirically imbalanced (slot ratios $0.6\times$ to $412\times$ in A.4). $\mathrm{HM}_3$'s smallest-link virtue actively works against the oracle. (Weakly-supported item 3.)
- **Major (Likely, Method).** A.4 force-oracle reading understates how broken $\hat v_1^{(S_6)}$ is: a frame capture of $\approx 1.00/1.95$ when slot 2 is forced to oracle implies $\hat v_1^{(S_6)}$ is nearly *orthogonal* to $v_1^\star$, not merely a poor approximation. (Probably-wrong item 2.)
- **Major (Speculative, Assumption).** The rank-2 restriction may be doing load-bearing work. M2 in particular is rank-sensitive; at $r\ge 4$ the carry's $\mathrm{state\_align}$ to $V_r(A)$ may recover. If so, the "structural" diagnosis is really "rank-2 starvation." (Probably-wrong item 4; next-step 2.)

### Moderate

- **Moderate (Likely, Method).** V1, V2 chosen without a principled exhaustion argument — failure of two specific ratio-style additions does not license "no $\Delta\mathcal E$ closes the gap." A pre-registered family of additions (or a parametrised sweep) is needed. (Weakly-supported item 4.)
- **Moderate (Certain, Method).** V4 is close to tautological as a sufficiency diagnostic: $\lambda\to\infty$ trivially recovers $V_r$, so "$\lambda$ at closure" measures gradient-norm ratios at $V_r$, not feature-set sufficiency. Replace with the $\partial_\lambda$-at-zero diagnostic. (Probably-wrong item 3; Reframing 4.)
- **Moderate (Likely, Presentation).** §3's three open items are mis-prioritised. Item 3 (window-local approximation of $A^\top A$) is the only real falsification test for H3 itself and should be item 1.

### Minor

- **Minor (Speculative, Assumption).** $V_r \subset B_{\mathrm{union}}$ is asserted via the force-frame ceiling but never directly measured ($\sin^2\theta_{\max}(V_r, B_{\mathrm{union}})$). On smaller windows or other matrices the caveat may bite. (Probably-wrong item 3; next-step 4.)
- **Minor (Speculative, Method).** V4's per-matrix $\lambda$ at closure ($0.1$ for two matrices, $0.5$ for `diffuse-diffuse`) given no mechanistic interpretation. Likely a normalisation artefact, not a depth-of-failure signal.

### Nitpick

- **Nitpick (Likely, Presentation).** "Common thread" placeholder paragraph names the unification but never operationalises it. Either delete or commit to the unified statement (Reframing 2).

## Bottom line on next-steps section

§3's three open items are mistargeted. (1) "Reconcile a numerical discrepancy" is bookkeeping. (2) "Frame-level row-cheat diagnostic" is a new screen for a problem that has not been shown to exist. (3) "Investigate window-local approximations of $A^\top A$" is the only real item and it is buried last. It should be the only item, framed as the falsification test for H3 itself: if the cheapest H2-leaning feature ($A_w^\top A_w + A_{\mathrm{fut}}^\top A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ power-iteration) doesn't pass the V3 sweep, then evidence-sufficiency at rank 2 with window-local features is provably impossible and the project must move to H2.
