Second-slot tail-bias verification
==================================

Date: 2026-04-21

Question
--------
Verify the theory:

  The second rank slot is captured early by a tail/high-entropy competitor.
  The carried sketch then gives that tail-ish direction old-energy credit, so
  later windows are biased toward keeping the tail slot and away from the
  missing second oracle direction.

This report extends `summary/consecutive_tail_conspiracy_diagnostic.txt`.


New Diagnostic Driver
---------------------
Added:

  second_slot_tail_bias_diagnostic.py

Raw outputs:

  summary/second_slot_interventions_all_raw.txt
  summary/second_slot_scores_failures_raw.txt

The driver reuses `cex_restricted_space_probe.py`'s combined-score optimizer and
logs:

  - normal trajectory;
  - force block 1 to projected QR oracle;
  - force blocks 1 and 2 to projected QR oracle;
  - force only the second block-1 slot toward the oracle residual;
  - previous second-slot survival in the next selected frame;
  - second-slot candidate scores under actual sketch B, zero sketch, and an
    oracle-forced sketch context.

Important caveat:

  This is a diagnostic harness, not the benchmark runner. Use comparisons
  within this harness as intervention evidence. The earlier raw dump remains
  the benchmark-aligned trajectory record.

Mixed-tail-sharp readout:

  The actual sketch B is the reason opt2 is competitive. Under zero_B or
  oracle_B, opt2 usually collapses, while oracle-like second-slot candidates
  become competitive or win. However, the exact winner depends on the
  orthogonalization context:

    - q2_vs_v1opt can be weak if the optimizer's first direction blocks it.
    - q2_vs_q1oracle is often much stronger under oracle_B.
    - later blocks can still show actual_B favoring a high-gain/tail opt2 even
      when q2_vs_q1oracle would win under oracle_B.

  This verifies both sketch contamination and frame/orthogonalization effects.

Conclusions
-----------
1. The previous winning second-slot tail direction usually survives into the
   next window.

   The survival metrics become very high after the first few windows. The
   previous second slot is not discarded and rediscovered from scratch; it is
   carried forward as a high-scoring candidate.

2. The sketch B is causally responsible for much of the second-slot tail bias.

   In the second-slot score decomposition, opt2 often wins under actual_B but
   loses badly under zero_B and oracle_B. This is exactly the expected signature
   if the carried sketch is biasing the second slot toward the tail.

3. Diffuse-diffuse is mostly an early sketch-capture problem.

   Forcing block 1 or blocks 1-2 into the oracle span makes the second oracle
   direction converge. The normal trajectory retains a tail-biased second slot
   because early B gives the wrong second direction old-energy credit.

4. Mixed-tail-sharp has an additional frame/orthogonalization issue.

   Forcing blocks 1-2 improves top-1 recovery, and oracle_B often makes the
   oracle-like second slot competitive. But the second oracle cosine can still
   be lost later. The likely reason is that the optimizer's first direction and
   the QR/orthogonalized oracle frame can make the second oracle residual score
   poorly, so a high-gain/tail complement can still occupy the second slot.

5. The binary "same tail direction" vs "fresh tail mixtures" is too narrow.

   The evidence supports a path-dependent second-slot mechanism:

     early weak oracle-2 retention
     -> tail-biased second slot stored in B
     -> previous opt2 remains high-scoring in the next block
     -> opt2 beats oracle-2 under actual_B
     -> zero_B/oracle_B removes or reverses the advantage

   The tail direction is not necessarily identical forever, but the winning
   second-slot tail region survives strongly enough to bias the next window.


Next Check
----------
The remaining open question is whether keeping an internal rank larger than 2
would prevent early deletion of oracle-2. The next experiment should run
internal ranks 4, 8, and 16, then evaluate the top-2 exact subspace projection
after final compression. If internal rank fixes the failures, the method needs
oversampled candidate retention rather than a sharper rank-2 objective.
