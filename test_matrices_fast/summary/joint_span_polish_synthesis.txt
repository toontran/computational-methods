Joint-span polish on top of future_hmean_online — synthesis
============================================================

Date: 2026-04-23


Question
--------
Use the greedy second-slot pick (`future_hmean_online`) as an initial rank-2
frame V_selected, then polish it by joint ascent on

    J_span(W) = HM( ||A_current W||_F^2 , ||A_future W||_F^2 ) * relH_cur(W)

where relH_cur(W) is the row-energy entropy of A_current W (row-wise squared
norms). Check whether this subspace-level objective, rotation-invariant over
W's columns, fixes the slot-swap failure of the per-slot objective on
`mixed-tail-sharp`.


Patch
-----
`half_window_sliding_hmean_experiment.py`:

- `span_objective(W, A_cur, A_fut)` computes J_span.
- `joint_span_polish(W_init, A_cur, A_fut, candidates, ...)` does projected
  Riemannian ascent on J_span, but **in the span of the candidate pool**, not
  in the full n-dim Stiefel:

    1. Build B = QR([V_init | candidate vectors]) — orthonormal basis of
       span({v_1, v_2_greedy} ∪ candidate pool), n × d, d ≤ 6.
    2. Parametrize W = B @ C, C ∈ R^{d × r} Stiefel.
    3. Ascend J_span over C with projected-tangent update + QR retraction +
       backtracking line search.
    4. Return W* = B @ C*.

- New policy `future_hmean_online_joint` = greedy `future_hmean_online`
  + joint polish.

Reason for the span constraint: a first, unconstrained polish (full n-dim
Stiefel) found spurious maxima far from signal — it boosted J_span by ~3
orders of magnitude but drove W into row-dense tail directions with
`mean_align` dropping to 0.05 and `oracle_proj_norm[0]` stuck near 0.28.
Restricting search to the 6-dim candidate span eliminates those spurious
directions.

Observations
------------
1. The polish DOES polish.
   dJ_med is strictly positive at every size (1e-7 to 4e-6). Span-constrained
   ascent finds a higher J_span than the greedy init. So the optimization is
   working.

2. The effect on subspace alignment is non-monotone.

   - half_win=16 sliding: polish is a big win. mean_align 0.017 -> 0.699, the
     greedy was stuck in a one-direction basin (cos1=0.99, cos2=0.64); polish
     rotated it into a two-direction basin (cos1=0.99, cos2=0.83) and
     recovered V_exact[:,0] capture (0.02 -> 0.70).
   - half_win=64 sliding: polish is a moderate win. mean_align 0.492 -> 0.679.
     Similar story: greedy missed V_exact[:,0]; polish recovered it.
   - half_win=32 sliding: polish HURTS. mean_align 0.739 -> 0.076. The
     greedy already captured both directions; polish rotated to a basin that
     drops V_exact[:,0], even though it still has non-trivial oracle_mass and
     high cos1 = 1.000.

3. Split-probe mode shows consistent-ish improvement at win=16 and win=64, no
   change at win=32. Pattern matches sliding.

4. The magnitude of improvement in J_span is tiny (dJ_med ~ 1e-6 against
   J_span ~ 0.5–1.0). That flat landscape is what lets the polish land far
   from the greedy basin in subspace terms even when moving only a tiny
   amount in J_span. J_span is not discriminative enough on its own in this
   regime.


Why the win=32 sliding regression
---------------------------------
At half_win=32 sliding the greedy end-state already captures both oracle
directions (final_exact_cos = [0.977, 0.739]). The polish then finds a
nearby higher-J_span direction which happens to trade subspace alignment for
HM energy. J_span does not penalize losing V_exact[:,0] specifically; it
only rewards total HM energy + row diffuseness. So when the local basin has
multiple nearly-flat maxima, the polish can choose the one with slightly
higher HM energy at the cost of V_exact[:,0] capture.

This is the same mechanism that plagued the unconstrained polish, just
happening at a smaller scale because of the span constraint.


Bottom line
-----------
- Span-constrained polish on J_span(W) = HM(gain_cur, gain_fut) * relH is a
  correct rank-2 ascent: it strictly improves J_span at every size.

- It gives a large boost to subspace recovery when the greedy initialization
  misses V_exact[:,0] (half_win=16 and half_win=64 sliding on
  `mixed-tail-sharp`), moving mean_align from 0.017 to 0.699 and from 0.492
  to 0.679 respectively.

- It can hurt when the greedy initialization already captures both oracle
  directions and sits near a J_span local-max (half_win=32 sliding here),
  because J_span has other nearby basins that trade signal fidelity for
  energy balance.

- J_span's flatness in the candidate span (dJ ~ 1e-6) lets small changes in
  the objective correspond to large swings in signal alignment. That is the
  central limitation: the rank-2 HM objective is not sharp enough on
  `mixed-tail-sharp` to pin down the correct subspace on its own.


Implications for the objective
------------------------------
To make the polish robustly useful on this matrix, the objective needs to be
sharpened so that signal fidelity is rewarded more strongly than
row-response balance. Candidate modifications:

- Normalize HM terms by candidate-pool maxima (candidate-share HM in 2D),
  so J_span aligns with the greedy scoring and does not reward raw
  tail-energy.
- Add a guard factor that rewards low `top_k` row concentration on BOTH
  halves simultaneously (pair-entropy), not just current-half relH.
- Add a soft anchor term `+ lambda * ||V_selected^T W||_F^2` that prefers
  subspaces close to the greedy init; the polish then only rotates when the
  J_span gain is large enough to overcome the anchor.
- Replace J_span with a rank-2 version of the benchmark's
  `obj_future_complement_penalty` (which was the best offline conservative
  objective): explicitly reject the projected block_svd complement at the
  subspace level.

At fixed J_span, a span-constrained polish is the right shape of move, but
the ingredient J_span itself is the weak link.
