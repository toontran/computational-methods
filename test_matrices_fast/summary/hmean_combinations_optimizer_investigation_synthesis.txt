HM-combination optimizer investigation synthesis
================================================

Date: 2026-04-25

Question
--------
Repeat the future-HM optimizer test for the harmonic-mean combination
policies:

- `future_hmean_triplet_online`
- `future_hmean_nested_online`
- `future_hmean_pairwise_online`
- `future_hmean_weighted_online`

The test asks whether there are better points inside rowspan([A_w; A_{w+1}])
than the streaming optimizer's v2, and whether those points are actually
preferred by the optimizer's combined forgetting objective.

New diagnostic
--------------
Added `hmean_combinations_optimizer_diagnostic.py`.

For each block and policy it:
1. Replays the normal streaming combined-score optimizer on M_gain =
   [sketch; A_w].
2. Fixes optimizer v1.
3. Builds B = rowspan([A_w; A_{w+1}]) orthogonalized against v1.
4. Optimizes the policy's smooth HM-combination objective inside B.
5. Compares that in-union point against optimizer v2 under:
   - the HM-combination policy objective, and
   - the optimizer's actual combined forgetting score.

Policy normalizers are fixed from the online candidate pool, matching
`reports/approximation/new_approx_hmean_combinations_online.txt`.

Artifacts
---------
- `hmean_combinations_optimizer_diagnostic.py`
- `summary/hmean_combinations_optimizer_diagnostic.csv`
- `summary/hmean_combinations_optimizer_diagnostic.json`
- `summary/hmean_combinations_optimizer_diagnostic.txt`

Run
---
Eight matrix families, n=1024, half_win=32, rank=2, first 4 overlapping
half-window pairs. The local in-union polish used `--union-maxit 25` and
`--union-random-starts 2` to keep the 4-policy diagnostic tractable.

Aggregate result
----------------
Across the 24 post-first-block checks per policy:

  policy                           better in-union policy score   lower combined score
  future_hmean_nested_online       24/24                          24/24
  future_hmean_triplet_online      24/24                          24/24
  future_hmean_weighted_online     18/24                          24/24
  future_hmean_pairwise_online      3/24                          24/24

Interpretation by policy
------------------------
`nested`:
Strongest and cleanest analogue of the future-HM result. Every tested block
has a better in-union point under the nested HM objective, but that point is
worse under combined forgetting score. Mean policy ratios are typically 1.2x
to 4.7x, with static-cex inflated by a few very large early ratios.

`triplet`:
Same qualitative result as nested. Every tested block has a better in-union
triplet-HM point, and every one loses under combined score. The best in-union
point usually has very high M_gain rowspace mass (~0.99), so this is mostly
objective mismatch rather than search-space invisibility.

`weighted`:
Mixed on policy improvement: 18/24 post-first blocks improve inside the
two-half union. All 24 still lose under combined score. This is expected:
once prior rows receive large weight, the weighted objective moves closer to
the sketch-heavy combined objective, so the optimizer v2 is often already
near a weighted-HM optimum.

`pairwise`:
This is different. Only 3/24 post-first blocks found a better in-union point
than optimizer v2 under the pairwise objective. The reason is structural:
pairwise uses

    HM(||[sketch; A_w]v||^2, ||[sketch; A_{w+1}]v||^2) * H1

so it rewards the carried sketch in both HM arguments. The optimizer v2 often
has substantial mass outside rowspan([A_w; A_{w+1}]) but inside the sketch
space, and that can be genuinely good for pairwise. In other words, pairwise
is not mainly failing to find better two-half union points; it is designed to
prefer retained sketch directions.

Main conclusion
---------------
For triplet and nested, the story matches the future-HM investigation:
better two-half union points exist, but the streaming optimizer is not
optimizing that objective. It optimizes combined forgetting score, and those
HM-combination-optimal points are consistently lower under that score.

For weighted, the prior-sketch weighting partly collapses the distinction
between the HM-combination objective and combined score.

For pairwise, the objective itself is sketch-retentive. The default optimizer
v2 can be better than any in-union point because pairwise puts the sketch in
both harmonic-mean arguments.

Implication
-----------
If the goal is to move toward the more optimal directions inside the
`A_w/A_{w+1}` union, pairwise is the wrong combination. Nested or triplet are
better targets for a post-v1 second-slot polish, but such a polish must be
accepted with an explicit combined-score floor if we want to avoid breaking
the retained-state update.
