Objective-design repeated block-only support synthesis
======================================================

Date: 2026-04-23

Purpose
-------
The corrected block-only recheck showed that fresh-row support is real, but it
also showed that raw block gain can promote SVD-complement directions instead
of oracle q2. This pass tests what "repeated block-only support" should mean if
we want it to be useful for objective design.

Ideas tested
------------
1. Direct q2-vs-SVD current-block support

For each block, compare candidates under corrected block-only gain:

  gain2(v) = ||A_block v||^2

Candidates:

  opt2
  q2_vs_q1oracle
  svd_complement_mgain
  block_svd_complement
  prev_opt2

where `block_svd_complement` is the best current-block right singular direction
orthogonal to selected v1.

2. Repeated support index

For each candidate:

  gain_share_t(v) = gain2_t(v) / max_candidate gain2_t
  repeated_support_index(v) = mean_t gain_share_t(v)^2

This measures recurring fresh-row energy, but does not know whether that energy
is oracle-consistent.

3. Oracle-repeat index

For each candidate:

  oracle_mass_t(v) = ||Q_oracle,t^T v||^2
  oracle_repeat_index(v) = mean_t gain_share_t(v) * oracle_mass_t(v)

This is the important correction. It says fresh-row support only counts as
oracle support when the direction also lies in the projected oracle frame.

4. Stability and anti-spike checks

Also logged:

  mean_stability = mean_t |v_t^T v_{t-1}|
  stable_support_index = mean_t gain_share_t(v) * |v_t^T v_{t-1}|
  current_relH = entropy(A_block v) / log(block_rows)
  anti_spike_support_index = mean_t gain_share_t(v) * current_relH_t(v)
  max_frac, top4_frac = largest response-energy concentration in A_block v

These are guards against two bad cases:

  - current-block SVD complement chasing;
  - one-row or few-row spikes masquerading as support.


n=1024 results
--------------
Best raw block-gain winner:

  matrix                  opt2   q2   svd_mgain   block_svd   prev_opt2
  static-cex              0/32   0/32 7/32        25/32       0/32
  diffuse-diffuse         0/32   0/32 1/32        31/32       0/32
  mixed-tail-sharp        0/32   0/32 13/32       19/32       0/32
  residual-spiky-shocks   0/32   0/32 1/32        31/32       0/32

This is the main negative result:

  "maximize repeated block-only gain" is not enough.

The best raw current-block direction is almost always the block-local SVD
complement. That direction is exactly what the earlier correction warned about:
it has fresh-row support but is usually not oracle support.

Index summary:

  matrix                  q2 repeat   q2 oracle-repeat   block_svd repeat   block_svd oracle-repeat
  static-cex              0.069281    0.163941           0.999843           0.007003
  diffuse-diffuse         0.925603    0.961516           1.000000           0.160769
  mixed-tail-sharp        0.107410    0.240491           0.999582           0.040001
  residual-spiky-shocks   0.335836    0.561226           1.000000           0.197721

Readout:

  - block_svd has near-perfect repeated raw support by construction, but poor
    oracle-repeat support.
  - q2 has weaker raw support than block_svd, but much stronger oracle-repeat
    support in every matrix.
  - diffuse-diffuse is the cleanest q2 case: q2 gain_share is high and
    oracle_mass is essentially 1, while block_svd has low oracle mass.
  - mixed-tail-sharp remains hard: q2 is oracle-consistent when present, but
    its fresh-row gain_share is much weaker than block_svd/mgain complements.

Anti-spike readout:

  matrix                  q2 anti-spike support   block_svd anti-spike support
  static-cex              0.163264                0.009688
  diffuse-diffuse         0.955817                0.806583
  mixed-tail-sharp        0.215058                0.039636
  residual-spiky-shocks   0.441004                0.704133

The anti-spike check is useful but not sufficient. It rejects the block_svd
direction clearly in static-cex and mixed-tail-sharp, but not in
diffuse-diffuse or residual-spiky-shocks, where block_svd response is diffuse
across rows despite being geometrically non-oracle.

Stability readout:

  matrix                  q2 mean stability   block_svd mean stability
  static-cex              0.900040            0.000131
  diffuse-diffuse         0.037901            0.001429
  mixed-tail-sharp        0.815615            0.001336
  residual-spiky-shocks   0.464631            0.006743

Raw direction-to-direction stability is a good anti-SVD-complement check, but
it is not a universal oracle-support check. In diffuse-diffuse, q2 has very
high oracle-repeat support but low direct vector stability because the projected
row-space/oracle representative changes across blocks. So stability should be
used as a guard against block-local complements, not as a required q2 selector.


What worked
-----------
The useful repeated-support signal is:

  repeated fresh-row support
  AND oracle consistency
  AND not spike/concentration dominated
  AND not merely block-local SVD complement support

In scalar diagnostic form:

  oracle_repeat_index(v) = mean_t gain_share_t(v) * oracle_mass_t(v)

is the best compact statistic from this pass.

It cleanly separates q2 from block_svd in all four matrices:

  static-cex:            q2 0.163941 vs block_svd 0.007003
  diffuse-diffuse:       q2 0.961516 vs block_svd 0.160769
  mixed-tail-sharp:      q2 0.240491 vs block_svd 0.040001
  residual-spiky-shocks: q2 0.561226 vs block_svd 0.197721

This also explains why the corrected block-only gain result was not enough.
Block-only gain exposes fresh-row energy, but the fresh-row maximizer is often
not the oracle candidate.


What failed
-----------
1. Raw block-only gain winner

Failed as a selector. It picks block_svd in 19/32 to 31/32 blocks depending on
the matrix.

2. Repeated raw gain alone

Failed as a selector. block_svd has repeat_support_index almost exactly 1.0 in
all matrices because it is usually the strongest raw block direction.

3. Direct vector persistence alone

Mixed. It rejects block_svd, whose mean stability is near zero, but it would
also falsely demote diffuse-diffuse q2.

4. Anti-spike alone

Mixed. It rejects obvious concentrated complements in static-cex and
mixed-tail-sharp, but diffuse block_svd directions can have high response
entropy while still being non-oracle.


Objective-design implication
----------------------------
Do not change the objective to simply maximize block-only gain, repeated
block-only gain, or block-only score.

The diagnostic support stack should be:

  1. Fresh support:
       gain_share_t(v) from corrected block-only gain.

  2. Oracle consistency:
       oracle_mass_t(v), or an online proxy if oracle is unavailable.

  3. Complement rejection:
       explicit comparison against block_svd_complement / current-block SVD
       residual directions.

  4. Anti-spike/stability guards:
       current_relH, max_frac/top4_frac, and cross-block direction stability.

For objective design, the hard part is replacing oracle_mass with an online
proxy. The current diagnostics say that a viable proxy should reward directions
that keep recurring across independent row blocks while not matching the
current block's top orthogonal SVD complement. Old sketch score alone is not
that proxy, because it preserves stale tail/history opt2.


Next recommended diagnostic
---------------------------
Test oracle-free proxies for `oracle_mass_t(v)`:

  - held-out future-block gain consistency over the next K blocks;
  - agreement with a small reservoir of independent old raw rows, excluding the
    carried sketch rows;
  - recurrence across disjoint row-block SVD subspaces rather than recurrence
    in a single block's top complement;
  - penalize high alignment to the current block's orthogonal SVD complement
    unless that alignment recurs in independent blocks.

The target is not "q2 wins current block". The target is:

  a direction receives fresh-row support repeatedly across independent blocks
  and that support cannot be explained by the current block's local SVD
  complement or by stale carried sketch state.
