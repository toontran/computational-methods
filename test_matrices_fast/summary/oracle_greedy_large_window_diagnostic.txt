Oracle-greedy diagnostic on large-window failure cases
======================================================

Date: 2026-04-20

Context
-------
See:
  summary/combined_rownorm_allblocks_benchmark_handoff.txt
  summary/larger_matrix_smaller_window_handoff.txt

The larger-matrix sweep showed that the combined-score advantage on
static-cex collapses when win/n >= 1/8 (combined matches iSVD at ~0.999
top-1 align) and returns at full strength when win/n <= 1/16 (combined
~0.12-0.16 vs iSVD ~0.999). This diagnostic asks:

  At large win/n, is combined failing because the greedy optimizer is stuck,
  or because the combined OBJECTIVE itself prefers the bad subspace?


Method: greedy-oracle
---------------------
Added to run_joint_warmstart_benchmarks.py:

  "greedy-oracle": {
      "basis_selection": "greedy",
      "joint_warm_start_greedy": False,
      "joint_warm_start_oracle": False,
      "rownorm_seed_first_block": True,
      "rownorm_seed_all_blocks": True,
      "oracle_candidate_check": True,
  },

Also: make_args now passes through oracle_candidate_check from overrides.

Semantics (from cex_restricted_space_probe.py around lines 4176-4211):
After the greedy optimizer produces V_score on each block, build an oracle
candidate V from the projected true span of V_exact, score it under the
same combined objective, and replace V_score iff the oracle candidate's
combined-score sum is strictly higher. This is a direct oracle probe of
the greedy step under the combined objective.

The oracle candidate was NEVER accepted. Greedy already finds the subspace
with the highest combined-score sum available on every block.


Interpretation
--------------
Failure is at the objective level, not the optimizer level. The combined
objective's argmax at large win/n coincides with the iSVD-like subspace;
the oracle subspace does NOT achieve a higher combined-score sum, so the
optimizer has nothing to improve.

Why:
  - At small win/n, the within-block SVD term in the combined score is
    weakly identifying (too few rows to pin down rank-r). The entropy /
    old-row-memory term has real leverage and can pull the pick away from
    the local SVD choice toward the global subspace. This is where
    combined beats iSVD on static-cex.
  - At large win/n, the within-block SVD term dominates (many rows per
    block resolve rank-r locally). The combined score is effectively a
    perturbation of the block SVD score; its argmax coincides with the
    iSVD pick and the entropy term cannot move the optimum.

So combined is not a "better optimizer targeting the same thing as iSVD";
it is a different objective. It agrees with iSVD in the data-rich per-block
regime and differs in the data-poor per-block regime where retaining
old-row information actually matters.


Implications for next steps
---------------------------
1. In the large-win regime, no amount of greedy improvement or oracle
   warm-start will help combined beat iSVD. Improvements have to come
   from changing the objective: for example, up-weighting the entropy /
   old-row-memory term as win grows, or normalizing the within-block SVD
   term so it does not dominate as win grows.

2. Before chasing a fix, add the rank-r subspace metric to the benchmark.
   It is plausible that at large win/n both methods are producing correct
   rank-2 subspaces with only a leading-vector rotation, in which case the
   top-1 0.999 values are a metric artifact and there is nothing to fix.

3. Deployment guidance: evaluate combined at win/n <= 1/16. Benchmarks at
   win/n = 1/8 systematically understate its edge.
