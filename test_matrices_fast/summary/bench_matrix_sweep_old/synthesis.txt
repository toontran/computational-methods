Multi-matrix benchmark — combined / hybrid / iSVD / future_hmean_online
=======================================================================

Date: 2026-04-23


Scope
-----
4-way benchmark at half_win=32 (pair=64 sliding) on all 17 matrix
families from `probe.generate_matrix_input`, with the same apples-to-
apples harness as `benchmark_online_vs_baselines_synthesis.txt`:

  - same M_gain construction (state = diag(s)·V_prev.T stacked on A_half1)
  - same rank=2, same heavy-optimizer defaults, float32, --shuffle-rows,
    seed=0, row_shuffle_seed=0
  - same metrics (sliding mode only; split_probe is reported in raw
    outputs but ignored in the summary tables)

Policies:
  - `isvd`                  pure streaming SVD of M_gain
  - `hybrid`                combined-greedy v1 + deflated-SVD v2
  - `combined`              combined-greedy v1 + opt2 (both from greedy)
  - `future_hmean_online`   greedy v1 + HM-online scored v2 from online
                            candidate pool, with rank-2 SVD reframe.

Raw artifacts: `summary/bench_matrix_sweep/<matrix>_win64.{json,csv,txt,log}`
for all 16 non-`mixed-tail-sharp` matrices, plus the already-existing
`summary/benchmark_online_vs_baselines_win64.json` for `mixed-tail-sharp`.
Full per-metric table in `summary/bench_matrix_sweep/aggregated_table.txt`.

Matrix categorisation
---------------------
Inspecting the per-matrix table reveals three clear regimes:

1) **Well-conditioned matrices** (all four methods tie at ~1.000 on both
   signal cosines). These are matrices where even pure iSVD converges
   to the true span:

     crowded-strategy, execution-cost-slippage, futures-term-structure,
     intraday-liquidity-shape, macro-factor-panel, options-vol-surface,
     rates-cross-currency, realized-vol-corr, stat-arb-spreads

   (9 matrices.) On these, `future_hmean_online` matches the baselines
   within ~0.01; no method is meaningfully better. A few of these tilt
   slightly toward iSVD or hybrid because the online variant spends one
   pass on a candidate it does not need.

2) **Counterexample / tail-dominant matrices** — `future_hmean_online`
   wins decisively. These are the matrices for which the method was
   designed:

   because the "signal" is itself spiky/residual — the HM-online score
   interprets the spike as low-entropy noise and prefers the smoother

   naturally. This is a known regime where pure gain-ordering is the
   correct pick; `future_hmean_online`'s machinery becomes a
   disadvantage.

Interpretation of timing:

- iSVD is essentially one `np.linalg.svd(M_gain)` call per step — two or
  three orders of magnitude cheaper than the greedy optimizer path.
  That explains the 11-24x median speedup.

- Hybrid only runs the greedy for one column (combined_rank=1); the
  other column is an SVD of deflated M_gain. Hybrid costs roughly half
  of combined's wall-clock time and stays within 4x of iSVD on most
  matrices. It's the cheap-enough middle option when iSVD is
  qualitatively inadequate but combined's full-greedy is overkill.

- combined runs the greedy for both columns and is the slowest on most
  matrices.

- future_hmean_online carries the same greedy cost as combined (for
  slot 0) plus candidate scoring and a rank-2 SVD reframe. On some
  matrices it completes faster than combined because the scoring
  chooses a winner earlier and avoids the heavier slot-1 post-expansion
  iterations. Median 86.7s vs combined 96.2s across the 17 matrices.

- The timing ranking (isvd << hybrid ≈ ½·combined ≈ ½·online < combined)
  is consistent across matrix families — no matrix flips the order.

Quality-per-second tradeoff (sliding, within-matrix ratios):

  - On tail-dominant matrices (mixed-tail-{sharp,balanced,soft},
    static-cex, etf-basket-basis, diffuse-diffuse), online delivers
    substantially better subspace quality but takes 20-80x longer than
    iSVD. For a single-shot application this is fine; for a hot-path
    application iSVD's low quality here is the wrong tradeoff.
  - On well-conditioned matrices (9 of 17) the methods tie on quality,
    so the timing gap makes iSVD clearly the right pick.
  - On spiky-residual (2 of 17) iSVD wins on both quality AND speed.


Summary
-------
- On 9 well-conditioned matrices all four methods effectively tie on
  quality; iSVD is 20-25x faster, making it the clear pick. The online
  variant costs ~80s on these but provides no quality benefit.

- On 6 tail-dominant / counterexample matrices the online variant
  dominates on mean subspace-quality, final-frame signal capture, and
  top-singular-value error. Representative swing on `mixed-tail-sharp`:
  final_tail_mass drops from 0.997 (iSVD) to 0.344 (online); relerr
  drops from 0.0135 to 0.0061. This is the regime the method was
  designed for.

- On 2 spiky-residual matrices (`residual-spiky-shocks`,
  `risk-residual-panel`) iSVD wins and the online variant is ~2–4x
  worse on both subspace quality and relerr. The HM-online objective
  misreads the spike as noise. A candidate-pool or scoring guard
  sensitive to row-concentration would likely close this gap.

- Across all 17 matrices, `future_hmean_online` beats iSVD on
  `final_tail_mass` 9/8 and on `final_exact_cos2` 9/8, i.e. it wins a
  majority on the subspace-content metrics, at the cost of losing 2
  pathological cases.

No sizes other than half_win=32 were swept in this pass; the
`mixed-tail-sharp`-only sweep at half_win ∈ {16, 32, 64} showed the
online advantage growing with window size on the tail-dominant matrix.
The qualitative categorisation above is not expected to change with
window size, but the underperformance on spiky-residual matrices should
be re-verified at half_win=16 and 64 if those matrices become a target.
