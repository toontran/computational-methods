Restricted+cex span-selection insight
=====================================

Context
-------

For the static CEX matrix, restricted+cex can still miss the true top right
singular vector even though the projected true vectors score very well in the
current sketch+window row space.

The key issue is not simply whether the normalized projection of v1 has a high
single-vector score. The rank-2 optimizer is greedy and orthogonality
constrained:

  1. choose opt1 by maximizing the single-vector objective;
  2. choose opt2 by maximizing the same objective subject to opt2 orthogonal to
     opt1.

The invariant in streaming_projection_invariant.txt needs the selected rank-r
subspace to contain the projected target direction(s). A greedy sequence of
good single-vector choices can fail to retain the desired rank-r projected
span.

Observed n=128, win=32, rank=2, restricted+cex-replicate
-------------------------------------------------------

Direct score sums for normalized projections of the true top two right
singular vectors onto the current optimizer row space:

  block  score(Pv1)+score(Pv2)   score(opt1)+score(opt2)   winner
  1      1.805422044328          1.802032135741             projected pair
  2      1.852955711330          1.854252792009             optimizer pair
  3      1.904944667470          1.903758385447             projected pair
  4      1.955616908484          1.955674459365             optimizer pair

So in blocks 1 and 3, the projected true pair has a larger raw sum of
individual scores than the optimizer's first two selected directions.

However, the projected vectors are not necessarily orthogonal after projection
onto the current row space:

  block  |<Pv1, Pv2>|
  1      0.2400447871
  2      0.0680677683
  3      0.0665370020
  4      0.0031591586

After QR-orthonormalizing the projected pair and summing the scores of that
orthonormal basis:

  block  QRscore(projected span)   score(opt1)+score(opt2)   winner
  1      1.781308786610            1.802032135741             optimizer pair
  2      1.851895334204            1.854252792009             optimizer pair
  3      1.903832202831            1.903758385447             projected span
  4      1.955614921237            1.955674459365             optimizer pair

Interpretation
--------------

The optimizer is not maximizing the unconstrained sum

  score(Pv1) + score(Pv2).

It is maximizing a greedy orthogonal sequence. That can prefer a first vector
that is slightly better individually but tilts the selected two-dimensional
span away from span(Pv1, Pv2). Once that span is compressed into the streaming
state, the projection-preservation invariant no longer applies exactly.

The first block is the most damaging case: the projected true pair has the
larger raw score sum, but its two vectors have substantial overlap after
projection. The greedy orthogonalized comparison favors the optimizer pair,
which immediately loses some of the target projected span.

Likely algorithmic implication
------------------------------

Improving the single-vector optimizer is not enough. The issue is rank-r span
selection.

Potential fixes to test:

1. Add a joint rank-r/span-level candidate check before compression.
2. Compare candidate subspaces by a subspace objective rather than by greedy
   single-vector scores.
3. Include the projected target-span diagnostic in experiments:

     raw projected-pair score sum,
     orthonormalized projected-span score sum,
     optimizer greedy score sum,
     principal angles between selected span and projected true span.

4. For synthetic/debug runs where V is known, force span(Pv1, Pv2) as a control
   to verify that post-selection compression preserves the invariant.
