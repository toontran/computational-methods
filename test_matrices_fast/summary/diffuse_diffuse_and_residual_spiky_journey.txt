Journey: from residual-panel failure to the diffuse-entropy-degeneracy diagnosis
================================================================================

Date: 2026-04-21

Purpose
-------
Self-contained technical trace of how the investigation moved from "combined
objective fails on risk-residual-panel" to the refined diagnosis that the
combined score's entropy term is a sample-efficient-degenerate discriminator
in the "both signal and noise are row-diffuse" regime. Captures the hypothesis
chain, the two new test matrices that pinned the failure axis, the
benchmark-naming pitfall that temporarily inverted the conclusion, and the
final score-component evidence that reframed the failure mode.


Starting point
--------------
Two prior artifacts defined the state entering this investigation:

  summary/combined_rownorm_allblocks_benchmark_handoff.txt
  summary/oracle_greedy_large_window_diagnostic.txt
  larger_sweep_results/{n512_win32,n1024_win32,...,n1024_win128}.txt

The combined-score variant with greedy rank-by-rank selection, row-normalized
SVD init on every block (rownorm_seed_all_blocks=True), old_memory_size=win,
q0=8, qmax=48, restarts=8, maxit=120, dtype=float32 was the deployed method.

In the post-bugfix sweep at n=1024 the method produced:

  matrix                   win=32      win=64      win=128
  static-cex               ~0.978      ~0.984      ~0.990  (high = good)
  risk-residual-panel      ~0.24       ~0.20       ~0.28   (low = bad)
  others                   near 1 (good)

Under the old residual-error scoring convention (mean_align measured subspace
error, lower was better) combined crushed iSVD on static-cex and most of the quant
catalog, but lost to iSVD on risk-residual-panel at every window.


Initial hypothesis: "diffuse noise defeats entropy"
---------------------------------------------------
Score-component dumps on risk-residual-panel n=1024 w=128 (block 1) showed the
combined optimizer selecting a non-signal direction with simultaneously higher
gain2 AND higher rel_H than the oracle signal direction. Specifically:

  optimizer_v1:    gain2=1.057, phi=2.204, rel_H=0.760, y4/y2^2=0.025
  oracle_raw_v1:   gain2=0.958, phi=1.762, rel_H=0.545, y4/y2^2=0.071

Interpretation at the time:

  - risk-residual-panel's bulk heavy-tailed heteroskedastic noise creates a
    dense, row-diffuse competing direction.
  - combined's entropy term penalizes row-concentrated directions (signal) and
    rewards row-diffuse ones (noise bulk).
  - So "noise diffuseness" was named as the failure mechanism.

Contrast with static-cex (known combined win): the non-signal competitor
there is a spike-localized tail column (one large coordinate + small shared
mass on last r_sig rows). Low row-entropy. Entropy term penalizes it, signal
wins.


The 2x2 factorial probe
-----------------------
The catalog at that moment covered only three cells of a 2x2:

                          diffuse tail       localized / spiky tail
    diffuse signal        (missing)          static-cex and most catalog
    residualized signal   risk-residual-panel (missing)

If "noise diffuseness" was truly the failure axis, the two missing cells
should resolve which variable matters:

  (A) diffuse signal + diffuse tail with close sigma      -> diffuse-diffuse
  (B) residualized signal + spiky idiosyncratic noise     -> residual-spiky-shocks

If (A) breaks combined, entropy-term-degeneracy is the culprit independent of
residualization. If (B) recovers combined, it confirms that risk-residual-
panel's failure is specifically about noise diffuseness rather than the
residual-panel factor construction itself.

The two matrices were added to:

  matrix_concepts.txt   (dialog section framing the 2x2 and predictions)
  matrices.txt          (catalog entries #10 and #11 in existing format)
  cex_restricted_space_probe.py
                       generate_diffuse_diffuse_input(...)
                       generate_residual_spiky_shocks_input(...)
                       and added to the --matrix choices and to the
                       MATRICES list in run_joint_warmstart_benchmarks.py


Matrix constructions
--------------------

(A) diffuse-diffuse. Minimal perturbation of static-cex:

    U0[:, 0:r_sig] = hadamard(n)[:, 0:r_sig] / sqrt(n)    # keep Hadamard signal
    G              = randn(n, n - r_sig)
    G             -= U0[:, :r_sig] @ (U0[:, :r_sig]' @ G) # orthogonalize against signal
    U0[:, r_sig:]  = qr(G)                                 # diffuse tail basis
    U, _           = qr(U0)

Spectrum is identical to static-cex:

    svec = [sigma1 * j^(-alpha_sig), tail_scale * j^(-alpha_tail)]
    sigma1=0.991, alpha_sig=0.003, alpha_tail=0.0145, tail_scale=0.99

So sigma_signal ~ 0.991, sigma_tail starts at 0.99. Close-sigma regime preserved;
only the tail geometry is changed from "spike-localized" to "row-diffuse."

(B) residual-spiky-shocks. Risk-residual-panel's U, V, svec construction is
preserved verbatim (same crowding/liquidity residual modes, same sector-
grouped V, same close-sigma gap). Only the additive noise model is swapped:

    risk-residual-panel:
        A = vol[t] * (U * svec) V' + idio_scale * t5_noise(n, n)

    residual-spiky-shocks:
        A = (U * svec) V'
        pick n_spike_rows ~ 2% of rows, random column support of size n/64,
        shared random unit direction, per-row amplitude spike_amp:
            for row in spike_rows:
                A[row, spike_support] += spike_amp * spike_direction
        A += noise_floor * randn(n, n)
        A -= A.mean(axis=0)

This replaces bulk diffuse heavy-tailed heteroskedastic noise with a rank-1
row-localized perturbation (same unit direction across 20 random rows) of
amplitude spike_amp.


Spike amplitude tuning
----------------------
Initial spike_amp=0.14 with per-row Student-t(df=4) scaling produced a single
extreme row (|t|~2, row norm 1.14) whose rank-1 direction had sigma=1.42,
pushing it above the factor signal (sigma~1.0). SVD top-2 of A then became
[spike, signal_1] instead of [signal_1, signal_2], making the benchmark's
V_exact target incoherent. All methods reported similar mean_align ~0.925
under the projection-norm convention.

Moving to a shared unit spike direction across spike rows (no per-row
Student-t amplification) and sweeping amplitude showed a nonlinear mapping
amp -> sigma_spike due to alignment with the factor signal column space.
Empirically:

    amp=0.050  -> sigma_spike ~ 0.22 (buried in tail, no visible competitor)
    amp=0.100  -> sigma_spike ~ 1.05 (spike overtakes signal)
    amp=0.140  -> sigma_spike ~ 1.17 (worse)
    amp=0.220  -> sigma_spike ~ 1.42

Final setting: spike_amp = 0.10. Spike is the top SVD direction with sigma=1.05
(slightly above signal) but not by enough to destroy the factor subspace; the
methods can still encounter it as a near-top row-localized competitor.


Benchmark-direction confusion
-----------------------------
The first benchmark pass at n=1024, win=32/64/128 on both new matrices
produced:

    matrix                   win   combined  iSVD    fd
    diffuse-diffuse          32    0.973     0.626   0.990
    diffuse-diffuse          64    0.342     0.483   0.993
    diffuse-diffuse         128    0.127     0.707   0.985
    residual-spiky-shocks    32    0.898     0.269   0.926
    residual-spiky-shocks    64    0.849     0.282   0.896
    residual-spiky-shocks   128    0.824     0.404   0.828

An initial misread treated mean_align as alignment-quality (higher=better),
yielding the conclusion:

  - combined wins big on residual-spiky-shocks  -> spiky noise rescues it
  - combined loses on diffuse-diffuse at larger win  -> diffuse tail breaks it

which seemed to confirm "noise diffuseness = failure axis" exactly.

Current correction: mean_align is now an alignment-quality quantity; higher = better.
This flipped the reading to:

  - combined LOSES to iSVD on residual-spiky-shocks at every window
  - combined WINS vs iSVD on diffuse-diffuse at w=64 and w=128, loses at w=32

Revised implications:

  1. Replacing residual-panel's diffuse noise with a row-localized spike did
     NOT rescue combined. So "noise diffuseness" is not sufficient to explain
     the risk-residual-panel failure. The residual-panel factor construction
     itself has something combined mishandles: both U_signal and U_tail
     columns are already time-concentrated (event windows, narrow Gaussian
     pulses), giving signal and non-signal similar row-entropy signatures.

  2. On diffuse-diffuse, combined's failure is confined to small win; it
     IMPROVES with window size. That is not consistent with "entropy is
     misleading"; it is more consistent with an in-block sample-noise issue
     that averages out as w grows.


Score-component investigation on diffuse-diffuse
------------------------------------------------
Ran diag_score_components.py diffuse-diffuse 1024 32 (dump_score_components=
True, oracle_candidate_check=True, score_variant=combined).

Relevant extracts:

Block 1 (rows 1:32, first block, no old memory yet):

    oracle_raw_v1:   gain2=0.8306, phi=5.657, rel_H=1.000000
    oracle_raw_v2:   gain2=0.8308, phi=5.657, rel_H=1.000000
    optimizer_v1:    gain2=0.8295, phi=5.657, rel_H=0.999997
    optimizer_v2:    gain2=0.8283, phi=5.657, rel_H=0.999988

phi is numerically identical across all four candidates (sqrt(32) = 5.657).
rel_H differences are at the 10^-6 level. At block 1 combined is IDENTICAL to
argmax gain^2 for all practical purposes. The gain^2 gap between the signal
(0.8306) and the optimizer's chosen direction (0.8295) is 0.001 - well within
the sample noise of 32 rows. There are many tail directions tied with signal
on gain^2 in a single 32-row block; the iterative Krylov search settles on a
near-tied non-signal direction. This matches the intuition "entropies equal ->
only norms matter -> argmax norm," but argmax-norm over 32 noisy rows is a
lottery among near-tied directions.

Block 2 onward (rows 33:64, block + 32 old-memory rows pooled):

    oracle_raw_v1:   gain2=0.832,  phi=3.615, rel_H=0.927
    optimizer_v1:    gain2=0.832,  phi=4.000, rel_H=0.99998

This is where the theory breaks. rel_H is NO LONGER equal across candidates.
The oracle's pooled rel_H falls to 0.927 because the Hadamard signal is
uniform only over the original n=1024 rows; its projection onto a
64-row pool (32 current + 32 sketched old) is Gaussian-ish with rel_H < 1.

The optimizer, which has 1024-dim freedom against only ~64 "be uniform over
these pooled rows" constraints, can construct a direction in the span of tail
singular vectors that IS exactly uniform over the 64 pooled rows. Such a
direction gets rel_H ~ 1.0, phi ~ 4.0, and beats the oracle on total even
when gain^2 is matched.

pooled-entropy degeneracy, not noise-entropy signature, is the failure
mechanism in diffuse-diffuse.

Pre-factor:

    c = log(rows_seen / rows_ref) / (2 * log(rows_entropy))
    log_phi = c * log(y4 / y2^2)
    phi = exp(log_phi)
    score = gain^2 * phi

log(y4/y2^2) is 0 for fully concentrated, -log(rows_entropy) for fully
uniform. c is negative during warmup (rows_seen < rows_ref), so log_phi is
positive for diffuse directions and phi >> 1. The entropy term rewards
pooled-uniformity, and in diffuse-diffuse, pooled-uniformity is a HUGE
submanifold of non-signal directions.

Why combined improves as w grows on diffuse-diffuse: larger pooled sets give
gain^2 less sample noise, so the signal's advantage (even though small in
absolute terms) starts accumulating reliably. At the same time, the
pooled-uniform submanifold is constrained more by a larger number of rows,
though it never vanishes. Empirically combined beats iSVD at w=64 and w=128.


Score-component investigation on residual-spiky-shocks
------------------------------------------------------
Ran diag_score_components.py residual-spiky-shocks 1024 32 with the same
flags as the diffuse-diffuse dump.

Block 1 (rows 1:32, first block):

    oracle_raw_v1:   gain2=0.664, phi=1.402, rel_H=0.195   total=0.931
    oracle_raw_v2:   gain2=0.654, phi=2.928, rel_H=0.620   total=1.914
    optimizer_v1:    gain2=0.649, phi=5.655, rel_H=0.9998  total=3.671
    optimizer_v2:    gain2=0.648, phi=5.655, rel_H=0.9999  total=3.665

Oracle sum = 2.845. Optimizer sum = 7.336. Optimizer beats oracle by ~2.6x.
principal_cosines(V_opt, Q_oracle) = 0.40, 0.29.

Two concrete observations that reframe the residual-panel story:

  (a) V_exact[:, 0] IS the spike. oracle_v1 has rel_H = 0.195, which is only
      achievable by a direction with support on ~20 of 1024 rows - exactly the
      rank-1 spike I injected (20 spike rows, shared column direction).

  (b) V_exact[:, 1] is a factor signal (oracle_v2 rel_H = 0.620). The
      crowding_event pulse lands on ~half of rows in a shuffled 32-row block,
      giving moderate row-concentration.

Combined's phi values then land exactly where the mechanism predicts:

  - spike direction: phi = 1.40 (heavy penalty - 4x below maximum 5.66)
  - signal direction: phi = 2.93 (2x below maximum)
  - optimizer's uniform-mix: phi = 5.655 (maximum)

gain^2 is basically tied across all four (0.648-0.664), so phi dominates the
total. Combined picks maximally-uniform tail-mix directions over BOTH spike
and signal. It is not just missing the target - it is actively maximally far
from V_exact because the entropy term points the wrong way on both oracle
columns simultaneously.

This is qualitatively different from static-cex:

  static-cex            residual-spiky-shocks
  ----------------------------------------------------
  V_exact[:, :2] = Hadamard signal  [spike, signal]
  signal rel_H:  ~1.0   signal rel_H: ~0.6
  tail   rel_H:  ~0     spike  rel_H: ~0.2, bulk tail rel_H: ~0.6
  entropy gap:   1.0    entropy gap (signal vs closest non-target): ~0

On static-cex the entropy term's preference for uniformity aligns with V_exact.
On residual-spiky-shocks the entropy term actively fights V_exact on both
columns, and - because the factor pulses make signal already non-uniform - even
the signal direction is substantially penalized.

So the answer to "isn't residual-spiky-shocks like static-cex?" is no:

  - In static-cex, the spike is in the non-signal tail and the signal is
    Hadamard-uniform. Entropy aligns with the target.
  - In residual-spiky-shocks, adding the rank-1 spike pushed sigma_spike
    slightly above sigma_signal, so the spike becomes V_exact[:, 0]. The
    entropy term now penalizes the target. And the remaining target column
    (the signal pulse) is also moderately concentrated, so it loses phi to
    tail-mixture uniform directions. Two-way loss.

This is consistent with the flipped-direction benchmark reading that combined
LOSES to iSVD on residual-spiky-shocks at every window. iSVD just follows
sigma and recovers the spike naturally; combined refuses to even consider it.


Revised diagnosis
-----------------
The original "noise diffuseness" framing conflated three separate effects.
The corrected picture, now informed by both score-component dumps:

  1. On residual-spiky-shocks, the failure has a two-way mechanism:

     (1a) V_exact[:, 0] is the rank-1 spike (sigma_spike = 1.05 > sigma_signal
          = 1.00). Its row-entropy rel_H ~ 0.2 is LOW. The entropy term
          penalizes it by 4x (phi=1.40 vs maximum 5.66). So combined cannot
          recover the top V_exact column even in principle, because its
          objective points away from it.

     (1b) V_exact[:, 1] is a factor signal pulse. Its rel_H ~ 0.6 is also
          below the maximum, so any "tail-mix that is uniform over the
          current 32-row block" direction beats it on phi while tying on
          gain^2.

     Both V_exact columns are actively penalized by the entropy term. The
     optimizer's subspace lands at principal-cosine ~0.4 from the oracle -
     maximally far, not near-miss. Replacing residual-panel's bulk noise
     with a spike did NOT rescue combined; it moved the failure point from
     (diffuse competitor wins phi) to (target columns both lose phi).

  2. On diffuse-diffuse, the failure is pooled-entropy degeneracy: a large
     submanifold of tail-direction combinations is exactly pooled-uniform
     over any given ~w+old_memory rows, so the entropy bonus does not select
     for Hadamard structure globally - it selects for "uniform over this
     particular pooled subset," which is non-unique and unrelated to signal
     alignment. Failure is strongest at small w (gain^2 sample noise is large
     and degenerate submanifold is relatively underconstrained) and
     diminishes as w grows.

  3. static-cex works because the tail columns are spike-localized AND
     V_exact[:, :2] is the Hadamard signal (spike tail at sigma=0.99 sits
     just below signal at 0.991). So the target has maximal row-entropy and
     the top competitor has near-zero row-entropy. Entropy aligns with gain
     on both target columns. Combined exploits a genuine, aligned
     signal-noise asymmetry.

The actual failure axis is "do the V_exact columns have maximal pooled row-
entropy compared to the tail-mixture submanifold the optimizer can reach."
Not "is noise diffuse." Failures occur when either (a) V_exact columns have
row-entropy lower than reachable tail mixtures (residual-spiky-shocks,
either column), or (b) V_exact columns tie with reachable tail mixtures on
entropy (diffuse-diffuse at small w).

Open questions
--------------

  - Is the pooled-entropy degeneracy fixable by changing what rows we pool
    over? Evaluating entropy over the FULL stream-so-far (oracle_sketch_all_
    seen_rows-like mode) instead of current_block + old_memory would shrink
    the degenerate submanifold back toward "uniform over all n rows," which
    only Hadamard-like directions satisfy. Cheap to try - would resolve the
    diffuse-diffuse small-w failure but would NOT help residual-spiky-shocks
    or risk-residual-panel: on those, V_exact columns themselves have below-
    maximum pooled rel_H regardless of which rows we pool over, because the
    factor construction's signal is time-concentrated and the spike is 20-row
    concentrated. The entropy term penalizes V_exact intrinsically.

  - Is the residual-panel failure curable by a different objective entirely?
    With V_exact columns intrinsically non-uniform in rows, gain^2 * pooled_
    entropy cannot align with the target. Candidate discriminators that do
    not depend on row-entropy of the target direction: column-side structure,
    temporal persistence across blocks, subspace-Rayleigh-quotient on the
    growing sketch without entropy weighting (which is basically iSVD - hence
    why iSVD wins here), or an entropy computed over something other than
    pooled rows.

  - Should spike_amp on residual-spiky-shocks be re-tuned so sigma_spike sits
    BELOW sigma_signal? Currently spike becomes V_exact[:, 0]. Lowering
    spike_amp below ~0.08 would push it below signal; then V_exact[:, :2]
    becomes [signal_1, signal_2] and the dump would test a different
    question: can combined at least recover the signal modes when the spike
    is a tail competitor (as originally intended)? Probably still fails
    because signal rel_H ~ 0.6 still loses to pooled-uniform tail mixtures,
    but worth confirming.

  - Does the diffuse-diffuse small-w failure also affect static-cex at very
    small w/n ratios? Worth a one-shot check; the Hadamard vs spike-tail
    entropy asymmetry should keep combined winning, but 32-row gain^2 noise
    alone could still hurt.
