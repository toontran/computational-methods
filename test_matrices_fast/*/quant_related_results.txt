### 1) Metrics (what “good” means)

* **Alignment (mean_align)** ≈ projected exact-vector norm; higher is better
  → **1 is perfect**, higher is better
* **Relative singular value error (mean_relerr_sval)**
  → measures spectral accuracy (scale)
* You explicitly care about **alignment**, so focus there

# 3) Critical separator regimes (important)

These are the **only places where behavior diverges meaningfully**

---

## (A) CEX / near-multiplicity structure

* Example: `static-cex`

* Interpretation:

  * Nearly equal singular values
  * Dense signal + structured tail (hard separation)
  * Matches your constructed adversarial matrix 

**Observed**

* restricted: **0.94 (good)**
* iSVD: **0.14 (catastrophic)**


**Conclusion**

* iSVD **fails completely**
* Restricted **preserves correct subspace**

**Reason (core insight)**

* iSVD relies on:

  * variance dominance
* CEX destroys:

  * separation in singular values
* Restricted exploits:

  * **structure across windows (entropy-like stability)**

---

## (B) Anisotropic tail / structured noise

* Example: `anisotropic-tail`

**Observed**

* restricted better (0.77 vs 0.45)


**Conclusion**

* Both struggle, but restricted degrades less
* Advantage when:

  * tail is **structured, not isotropic noise**

# 4) Core algorithmic difference (why this happens)

## iSVD (Incremental SVD)

* Tracks dominant directions via:

  * variance accumulation
* Assumes:

  * **spectral gap exists**
* Fails when:

  * eigenvalues are close
  * signal is “distributed” (not concentrated)

---

## Restricted method (your method)

* Works via:

  * compressed representation:
    [
    M_{\text{gain}} = \begin{bmatrix} S_{\text{prev}} V_{\text{prev}}^\top \ A_{\text{block}} \end{bmatrix}
    ]
  * iterative refinement in reduced space 

* Implicitly exploits:

  * **consistency across windows**
  * not just magnitude

---

## Key conceptual difference

| Property                  | iSVD           | Restricted           |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------- |
| Signal criterion          | large variance | persistent structure |
| Needs spectral gap        | yes            | no                   |
| Handles degeneracy        | poor           | strong               |
| Robust to structured tail | weak           | better               |

# 6) Bottom line (quant perspective)

* If your data behaves like:

  * **classical factor models / PCA regimes**
    → use iSVD (faster, same accuracy)

* If your data has:

  * **weak spectral gaps**
  * **structured tails**
  * **distributed signals across time**
  * **near-degenerate eigenvalues**

→ restricted is strictly better

---

# 7) Most important takeaway

The difference is not “streaming vs streaming” — both are.

The difference is:

> **variance-based tracking (iSVD)**
> vs
> **structure-consistency tracking (restricted)**

This only shows up when:

* eigenvalues don’t separate
* signal is not concentrated in magnitude

That is exactly the regime your CEX construction targets.
