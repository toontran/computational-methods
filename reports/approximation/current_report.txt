\section*{1. Where we are, and the current goal}

\paragraph{What changed about the objective (read this first).}
The companion derivation (\texttt{new\_approx\_combined.txt}) builds
$\operatorname{score}_w(v)$ as a \emph{lower bound} on $\|Av\|_2^2$
under assumptions (A1), (A2), and $\|N_wv\|_4\le\|Av\|_4$, using only
data seen through window $w$ (sketch $B$, current rows $A_w$, row
sample $R$). The argmax of a lower bound is not, in general, the
argmax of the bounded quantity. The present report changes the
demand: in the experiments below, with $r=2$, we now ask the score to
\emph{recover the top two right singular vectors $V_2$ of $A$}, i.e.\
to be argmax-correct, not just value-correct. We also relax the
streaming scope to allow a future half-window $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$. So
before reading on, note the two shifts relative to the derivation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Argmax-recovery, not lower-bounding.} M1 and M2 below
are points where the lower-bound's argmax disagrees with $V_2$. M3 is
the first repair signal for the single-window case where the argmax is
far from the projected oracle. None of these is a bug in the
inequality of \texttt{new\_approx\_combined.txt}; they are
consequences of using a lower bound for a task it was not derived to
solve.
\item \textbf{Future rows allowed.} M3 and the replacement family $S_6$
introduce $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$, the next half-window after the current
half-window block $A_w$. Thus the sweep treats $A_w$ itself as the
current half-window and pairs it with the following same-size
half-window. The original derivation excluded future rows. The
experiments below add them because the recovery task needs more than
past and current rows on some matrices.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Recap of the combined score.}
Specialising to the regime in which the earlier rows are measured at
the scale of $C_w$ (i.e.\ taking
$\mathrm{row}(N_w)\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{row}(C_w)$ in the base of the
derivation's correction factor, with
$h_2(N_wv)/\log\mathrm{row}(N_w)$ replaced by
$h_2(C_wv)/\log\mathrm{row}(C_w)$ in the exponent under (A2)), the
combined score from the derivation can be written
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w(v)
=\bigl(\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\bigr)\,\phi_w(v),
\qquad
\phi_w(v) :=
\left(\frac{n}{\mathrm{row}(C_w)}\right)^{
h_2(C_w v)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))},
\end{align}
where $n$ is the total number of rows of $A$,
$C_w=(R^\top, A_w^\top)^\top$ stacks the row sample $R$ with $A_w$,
$h_2(y):=4\log\|y\|_2-4\log\|y\|_4$.

This paragraph introduces a diagnostic normalisation of the entropy
multiplier used to discuss the claims below; it does not change the
per-block measurements in Appendix~A, which were collected with the
implemented multiplier from the derivation's notation at each block.
Concretely, starting from the
derivation's
$(\mathrm{row}(N_w)/n)^{-h_2(C_wv)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))}$ and
replacing $\mathrm{row}(N_w)$ by $\mathrm{row}(C_w)$ in that base gives
$(\mathrm{row}(C_w)/n)^{-h_2/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))}
=(n/\mathrm{row}(C_w))^{h_2/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))}$, the recap
form.
Equivalently, using the identity
$h_2(y)=-\log(\|y\|_4^4/\|y\|_2^4)$, this is the same object as the
derivation's $\|C_w v\|_4^{4c_w}\|C_w v\|_2^{-4c_w}$ once the
substitution is applied; the $(n/\mathrm{row}(C_w))^{\cdot}$ form is just the
entropy multiplier rewritten so its base is $\ge 1$, with the sign
of the exponent absorbing the sign of $c_w$. Since $h_2\ge 0$
always and $n\ge \mathrm{row}(C_w)$, the base
$n/\mathrm{row}(C_w)\ge 1$ and $\phi_w\ge 1$ in this
recap form, in agreement with the derivation's
$(\mathrm{row}(N_w)/n)^{-h_2/\cdots}$ (base $\le 1$, negative
exponent). The M1, M2, and M3 measurements below do not depend on
this substitution, and the empirical evidence in
Appendix~A is collected at intermediate blocks where
$\mathrm{row}(N_w)<n$ and $c_w<0$ in the derivation's notation.

\paragraph{Notation used by the diagnostics.}
Throughout this report, ``oracle'' (the top-$r$ right singular vectors
of $A$) is a global object defined from the full matrix; the score
sees only window-local data. ``Deflation'' below means orthogonal
projection of the search direction onto the orthogonal complement of
the previously accepted $\hat v^{(\sigma)}_j$ ($j<i$): identical to the
constraint $\hat v^{(\sigma)}_j{}^\top v=0$ in the derivation
(\texttt{new\_approx\_combined.txt}, eq.~for $v_j$). It does \emph{not}
mean deflating $A$ itself. Likewise, $V_{\mathrm{state}}$ refers to
the right factor $V$ of the carried sketch $B=SV^\top$ from the
derivation (the columns are $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ stored at the start of
the window); $\mathrm{span}(V_{\mathrm{state}})=\mathrm{rowspan}(B)$.
For any window-local search that uses $B$, $A_w$, and
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$, set
\begin{align}
B_{\mathrm{union}}:=\mathrm{rowspan}\!\begin{pmatrix}B\\ A_w\\ A_{\mathrm{fut}}\end{pmatrix}\subset\mathbb R^d.
\end{align}
This is the search subspace available from those three matrices.
Directions are extracted greedily: let $\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i$
denote the
$i$-th direction, defined by
\begin{align}
\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i \in
\arg\max_{v \in \mathbb S^{d-1},\ v \perp \hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_j\, \forall j < i}
\operatorname{score}_w(v).
\end{align}
The ``oracle'' is the top-$r$ right singular vectors $V_r$ of $A$;
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_i$ is the projection of the $i$-th
oracle column $v_i^\star$ onto the feasible set actually used at slot
$i$ --- i.e.\ onto the orthogonal complement of the previously
accepted directions $\{\hat v^{(\sigma)}_j\}_{j<i}$ and, for a
window-local search, also onto $B_{\mathrm{union}}$. We compare
against $\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_i$
rather than $v_i^\star$ because no window-local score can possibly
recover a direction outside its own search domain; the projected
oracle is the strongest target the score is allowed to be measured
against.

\paragraph{Two failures and one repair signal.}
Probing on a small set of test matrices identified two mechanisms by
which the combined score loses $\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_2$ (and
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_r$), plus one future-window signal that
repairs the leading-direction single-window failure.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(M1) $\phi_w$ ranks the combined argmax above the
oracle.} On \texttt{mixed-tail-sharp} at block 1, the score gap
between $\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_2$ and
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_2$ sits entirely inside $\phi_1$
($5.66$ vs.\ $3.70$, energy term flat at $0.819$--$0.833$). See
Appendix~A.1. \emph{Reconciliation with the derivation.} $\phi_w$
enters the lower bound $(\star)$ via assumption (A2), which equates
the (normalised) entropy of $C_w v$, $N_w v$, and $A v$. The
inequality $\|Av\|_2^2 \gtrsim \|N_w v\|_2^2 \cdot
(\mathrm{row}(N_w)/n)^{-h_2(N_w v)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(N_w))}$ is
correct as a value bound under (A2); M1 simply observes that
\emph{argmax}-ranking the bound is not the same as argmax-ranking
$\|Av\|_2^2$. In the recap form
$\phi_w=(n/\mathrm{row}(C_w))^{h_2(C_wv)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))}$,
larger $\phi_w$ means larger $h_2(C_wv)$, equivalently a
\emph{lower} $\ell_4/\ell_2$ concentration ratio. On this block the
score-favoured direction has the larger $\phi_w$ and beats the oracle
in the bound while contributing nothing extra to $\|Av\|_2^2$
globally.
\item \textbf{(M2) The sketch term aligns
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_2$ with $\mathrm{span}(V_{\mathrm{state}})$.}
Once $\|Bv\|_2^2 \gg \|A_wv\|_2^2$ (empirically once
$\rho_{\mathrm{past}}:=\|A_{1:w-1}\|_F^2/\|A_w\|_F^2\gtrsim 5$;
this $5$ is just where the energy ratio empirically tips on the test
matrices, not a theorem), the energy term reduces to
$\|Bv\|_2^2$, whose argmax over the deflation complement lies in
$\mathrm{span}(B)$. This is a directional dominance claim on the
retained sketch subspace, not a claim that $\|B\|_F^2$ matches the
full-history Frobenius norm; Appendix~A.2 reports both scales
separately. At the last measured block, block 31, on
\texttt{mixed-tail-sharp},
$\mathrm{state\_align}(\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_2)=0.989$ vs.\
$\mathrm{state\_align}(\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_2)=0.293$; a
joint Stiefel-2 optimisation returns the same point. See
Appendix~A.2. \emph{Reconciliation with the derivation.}
\texttt{new\_approx\_combined.txt} justifies $B=\Sigma V_{\mathrm{new}}^\top$
precisely because $\|Bv\|_2=\|P_U M_w v\|_2$ on
$\mathrm{span}(V_{\hat{}})$: the sketch is \emph{designed} to make
the energy term faithfully track the carried earlier-row sketch on the retained
right span. M2 is therefore not a defect of $B$. It is the
consequence of demanding argmax-recovery from a score whose energy
term, by construction, tracks $\mathrm{span}(V_{\mathrm{state}})$
rather than $V_r(A)$ once $\rho_{\mathrm{past}}$ grows.
\item \textbf{(M3) Add future rows to move the argmax toward the
oracle projection.}
At block 1 ($B=0$) on \texttt{diffuse-diffuse},
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_1$ has $\cos^2 < 0.05$ against either
exact oracle column and $0.096$ against
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_1$, while an oracle-warm
$\mathrm{HM}_2(u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}})$ isolation score
reaches $0.999$ against
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_1$. See Appendix~A.3. Thus M3 is
the repair signal for this failure: the current argmax is not near
the projected oracle because the score sees only one half-window when
$B=0$. Adding $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ lets the score test the stationarity
condition used in \texttt{new\_approx\_future\_hmean\_online.txt}:
the current and future per-row gains should agree for a real
population direction. The harmonic mean then penalises a direction
that is strong on $A_w$ but weak on $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$.
Uniform-row-space matrices like \texttt{diffuse-diffuse} show why
earlier rows plus current rows alone cannot distinguish population
directions from draw-specific directions.
\end{itemize}
Each claim rests on per-block measurements; the detailed numbers are
in Appendix~A.

\paragraph{The common thread (working hypothesis).}
The score can prefer directions whose window-local evidence looks
strong but is not oracle-identifying. M1 and M2 are failure modes of
the combined score. M3 is the corresponding fix direction for the
single-window argmax failure.
In M1 the score gap is in $\phi_w$, which rewards the visible
$h_2(C_wv)$ profile even when it does not correspond to
extra global $\|Av\|_2^2$ energy. In M2 the energy term is dominated by
$\|Bv\|_2^2$ once $\|Bv\|_2^2\gg\|A_wv\|_2^2$, so the argmax tracks the
leading directions of the carried sketch. M3 adds
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ so a candidate direction must have support in the
current and future half-windows, which moves the block-1 argmax back
toward the projected oracle on \texttt{diffuse-diffuse}.

What the newer forward-replication diagnostics reject is only the
narrower row-concentration version of this hypothesis. On the mixed-tail
failure set, the score-favoured directions do \emph{not} consistently
have elevated current-window top-row share relative to the oracle, so the
failure is not explained by a few rare rows in $A_w$ creating a spike that
then disappears on $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$. The verified signatures are instead
current--future energy imbalance ($u_{\mathrm{cur}}\gg u_{\mathrm{fut}}$
for the score-favoured direction while the oracle keeps
$u_{\mathrm{cur}}\approx u_{\mathrm{fut}}$) and carry pinning
($\mathrm{state\_align}$ near one for the score-favoured direction).
Thus the failure is unstable visible evidence, not current-window row
spikes alone.

\paragraph{The replacement family $S_6$.}
\textbf{Status note.} Unlike
$\operatorname{score}_w$, the family
$S_6$ below is \emph{not} the maximiser of any inequality on
$\|Av\|_2^2$; it has no analogue of (A1)/(A2) and no lower-bound
theorem behind it. It is a heuristic engineered to be
argmax-correct on representative test matrices. The reader should
not try to map $u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}}$
back onto $\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2$ and $\phi_w$: we are no longer
in the lower-bound framework. M1 and M2 motivate leaving the
combined score; M3 motivates adding the future-window stationarity
test. Together they led to a value-only,
analytically differentiable family that
(i) sees a future half-window $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ from after the
current one, (ii) avoids entropic gating, and (iii) uses a
rank-aware sketch term that does not collapse to
$\mathrm{span}(V_{\mathrm{state}})$. This was the best previous
operational replacement before the frame-level sufficiency sweep in
\S2--\S3; it is a baseline under diagnosis here, not a surviving
recommendation. The row sample $R$ used in the
derivation's $C_w=(R^\top,A_w^\top)^\top$ is intentionally not a slot
in these replacement scores: the replacement line discards row-sample
entropy evidence and substitutes current--future replication evidence
instead. The score is
\begin{align}
S_6(v):=\mathrm{HM}_3\!\bigl(u_{\mathrm{sk}}(v),\,u_{\mathrm{cur}}(v),\,u_{\mathrm{fut}}(v)\bigr),
\end{align}
where each slot is a normalised Rayleigh quotient
\begin{align}
u_{\mathrm{sk}}(v):=\frac{\|Bv\|_2^2}{w_{\mathrm{sk}}},\qquad
u_{\mathrm{cur}}(v):=\frac{\|A_wv\|_2^2}{\|A_w\|_F^2},\qquad
u_{\mathrm{fut}}(v):=\frac{\|A_{\mathrm{fut}}v\|_2^2}{\|A_{\mathrm{fut}}\|_F^2},
\end{align}
and $\mathrm{HM}_3$ is the harmonic mean
$3/(u_{\mathrm{sk}}^{-1}+u_{\mathrm{cur}}^{-1}+u_{\mathrm{fut}}^{-1})$.
Greedy extraction yields $\hat v^{(S_6)}_i$ analogously to
$\hat v^{(\mathrm{combined})}_i$. The harmonic mean's smallest-link
enforcer rewards directions that \emph{simultaneously} have non-trivial
$\|Bv\|_2^2$, $\|A_wv\|_2^2$, and $\|A_{\mathrm{fut}}v\|_2^2$ in the
sketch, current, and future windows --- ruling out $A_w$-only and
sketch-only directions in one shot. The weight $w_{\mathrm{sk}}$ is the
rank-$r$ truncation of $\|B\|_F^2$, i.e.\ $w_{\mathrm{sk}}:=\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2$
where $s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_r$ are the singular values stored in the
carried sketch $B=SV^\top$; this is the normalising constant that places each
$u_X$ in $[0,\sim 1]$.

\paragraph{The recent line of work: balancing $\mathrm{HM}_3$, and
why it hit a wall.}
$\mathrm{HM}_3$'s smallest-link property only fires correctly if the
oracle direction itself has roughly balanced
$(u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}})$. The oracle is
far from balanced where $S_6$ fails (the ratio
$u_{\mathrm{sk}}/u_{\mathrm{cur}}$ at the last measured block,
block 31, runs from $1.74\times$
to $412\times$ across the five matrices listed in Appendix~A.4's
slot-ratio table), so we tried a
per-direction reweighting --- the ``$E_2$ weighting'' --- that, inside
$u_{\mathrm{sk}}(v)=\|Bv\|_2^2/w_{\mathrm{sk}}$ only (the
$u_{\mathrm{cur}}$ and $u_{\mathrm{fut}}$ slots are unchanged),
replaces the scalar $w_{\mathrm{sk}}$ with $\sigma_{\ell(v)}^2$ for
$\ell(v):=\arg\max_{j\le r}(v_j^\top v)^2$ the sketch-direction index
most aligned with $v$ (here $v_j$ are the columns of
$V_{\mathrm{state}}$ and $\sigma_j=s_j$ the corresponding stored
singular values). The audit metric reports
success ($E_2$ drives the oracle's max/min ratio across the three
slots $(u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}})$ inside
$2.4\times$ on the regression matrices --- the three matrices on which the streaming
benchmark regressed under $S_6$, namely \texttt{diffuse-diffuse},
\texttt{residual-spiky-shocks}, and \texttt{mixed-tail-soft}; this
trio is the working set for §1's landscape probe and Appendix~A.4) but the streaming benchmark \emph{regresses}
sharply on the same matrices, and a landscape probe at the last
measured block, block 31, finds
the cause: under $E_2$, the score's argmax sits strictly above the
score at the oracle by $+0.041$, $+0.047$, $+0.006$ on
\texttt{diffuse-diffuse}, \texttt{residual-spiky-shocks},
\texttt{mixed-tail-soft}. The gap survives a high-budget joint sweep
($200$ random restarts, $\mathrm{maxit}=2000$,
$\hat v^{(S_6)}_1$ anchored at $\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_1$,
gap stays $\ge +0.122$ on \texttt{diffuse-diffuse}), ruling out
under-optimisation. The conclusion is structural: per-direction
reweighting balances the oracle's slot values but relocates the
score's actual peak away from the oracle. No further scalar weight
tuning fixes this. Detailed numbers, including the seven-matrix
capture table under $S_6$, $E_2$, \texttt{force\_oracle\_v2}
(abbreviated \texttt{force\_O2} in the appendix table; replaces only
$\hat v^{(S_6)}_2$ with $\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_2$ at every
block), and \texttt{force\_oracle\_frame} (abbreviated
\texttt{force\_frame}; replaces both directions with the oracle
rank-2 frame), are in Appendix~A.4.

\paragraph{The singular goal.}
The remaining question collapses to one:
\begin{quote}
\emph{``what minimal information, computable from window-local data,
makes the global oracle subspace the unique maximising subspace of the score
against every adversarial combination of component-local tails?''}
\end{quote}
Formally, for the rank-$2$ tests in this report: fix a feature set
$\mathcal E$ available at window $w$. In this report,
\emph{deployable window-local} features are
computed from $B$, the current block $A_w$, the next half-window
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$, and products of those same matrices. An explicit
$A^\top A$ or $V_r$ term is a \emph{diagnostic global} feature: it may
be used as a ceiling or sanity check, but not as an operational score.
Approximations to $A^\top A$ are only admissible after they are
constructed from window-local data and pass the same sufficiency test
as the value-only features. Given such an $\mathcal E$, define a
frame-level score $S_{\mathcal E}(Z)$ on pairs of orthonormal
directions $Z=[z_1,z_2]\in\mathrm{St}(d,2)$, and maximise it inside
the already defined search subspace $B_{\mathrm{union}}$. Let
$Q_{\mathrm{union}}$ denote any orthonormal
basis matrix for this subspace, so
$\mathrm{range}(Q_{\mathrm{union}})=B_{\mathrm{union}}$, and let
$P_{\mathrm{union}}=Q_{\mathrm{union}}Q_{\mathrm{union}}^\top$ be the
orthogonal projector onto $B_{\mathrm{union}}$. The projected oracle frame
$Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is obtained by taking a thin QR (equivalently,
the polar factor) of $P_{\mathrm{union}}V_2$; if
$\mathrm{rank}(P_{\mathrm{union}}V_2)<2$, the missing direction is
unreachable and the feature set fails the recovery test before
optimisation. With this definition, the test is
\begin{align}
Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}\;\stackrel{?}{=}\;\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{Z\in\mathrm{St}(d,2),\ \mathrm{range}(Z)\subset B_{\mathrm{union}}} S_{\mathcal E}(Z).
\end{align}
For right-rotation-invariant scores such as $\|A_XZ\|_F^2$, equality
means equality of the recovered two-plane; oriented-frame equality is
only meaningful for scores that break the $Z\mapsto ZQ$ symmetry. If
the projected oracle subspace is the maximiser, $\mathcal E$ is
sufficient and an operational score can be built around it. Otherwise
no slot reweighting recovers it and $\mathcal E$ must change.

\paragraph{Why this direction (H3) among possible solutions.}
Three broad directions are open for addressing the misleading-tail
problem; we choose the third.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1: keep $\{u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},
u_{\mathrm{fut}}\}$, design a smarter aggregator or weighting.}
The line that produced $E_2$ and the broader balance-tuning family.
The AB-03 closure (the high-budget joint sweep summarised in
Appendix~A.4; ``AB-03'' is the internal experiment ID for the
$E_2$-vs-$S_6$ landscape probe) refutes it as a controlling
principle: balance-tuning fails structurally
($\Delta_{S_6+E_2}\!\ge\!+0.122$ even at high optimisation budget
with the oracle anchor) because the feature set itself does not
distinguish the oracle from adversarial component-tail combinations.
Reweighting cannot create information the features do not carry.

\item \textbf{H2: replace value-only features with an explicit global
quantity (e.g.\ $A^\top A$ or its leading invariant subspace).}
Closes the gap by construction --- $A^\top A$'s leading invariant
subspace \emph{is} the oracle --- but abandons the streaming,
window-local, value-only constraints that motivated the score family.
Worth investigating eventually, but not before the cheaper options
are exhausted.

\item \textbf{H3 (chosen): treat the problem as one of evidence
sufficiency, sweep candidate features in increasing intrusiveness,
and use a frame-level audit to decide each one.}
H3 sits between H1 and H2: it preserves the value-only,
streaming-compatible setting but stops reweighting a fixed feature
set; instead, for each candidate addition $\Delta\mathcal E$, it
asks whether $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ becomes the maximiser on
$\mathrm{St}(d,2)\cap B_{\mathrm{union}}$ under joint optimisation.
Three properties of H3 motivate the choice:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Per-feature, falsifiable output.} A feature either makes
the oracle the argmax (the test passes) or it does not (the test
fails). There is no tuning loop and no audit metric whose
correlation with bench has to be assumed.
\item \emph{Separation of score-design failure from optimiser
failure.} Greedy and joint Stiefel ascent are run side by side
($j-g$ in Appendix~B); when $j-g$ is small both routines reach the
same maximum, so any residual $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}>0$ is a
property of the feature set, not the search.
\item \emph{Reusable screens.} The canonical screens used in H3
(oracle-vs-winner gap at vector and frame level, force-oracle
ceilings, anchor sensitivity, and the existing vector-level
row-cheat dominance screen --- checking that a vector score does not
collapse onto a direction that merely picks out a single high-norm row
of $A_w$ or $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$) are also the acceptance gates that any
future H1- or H2-style proposal must pass. The analogous frame-level
row-cheat screen is still open work.
\end{enumerate}
H3 also preserves the value-only, streaming, window-local
constraints that motivated the score family, and it requires no new
infrastructure beyond the Stiefel ascent already built for $S_6$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{2. Method: a sequential evidence-sufficiency sweep}

\paragraph{Lifting to frames.}
Throughout this section, $X\in\{\mathrm{sk},\mathrm{cur},\mathrm{fut}\}$
indexes the three sources, with $A_{\mathrm{sk}}:=B$,
$A_{\mathrm{cur}}:=A_w$ the current half-window block, and
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ the following same-size half-window. Extend
each $u_X$ to an orthonormal pair
$Z=[z_1,z_2]\in\mathrm{St}(d,2)$ via
\begin{align}
u_X(Z):=\frac{\|A_X Z\|_F^2}{\|A_X\|_F^2}
       =\frac{\|A_X z_1\|_2^2+\|A_X z_2\|_2^2}{\|A_X\|_F^2}.
\end{align}
A small bookkeeping change relative to the §1 vector definitions: the
sketch slot at the frame level uses the full $\|B\|_F^2$ as denominator
for symmetry with $\|A_w\|_F^2$ and $\|A_{\mathrm{fut}}\|_F^2$, whereas
the vector-level $u_{\mathrm{sk}}(v)$ in §1 uses the rank-$r$ truncation
$w_{\mathrm{sk}}=\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2$. Since the carried sketch
$B=SV^\top$ has exactly $r$ stored singular values, $\|B\|_F^2=
\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2=w_{\mathrm{sk}}$ \emph{identically} in this setting;
the two normalisations agree. We retain the $\|B\|_F^2$ notation at the
frame level for symmetry with the other two slots. Even were the two
constants to differ, they would scale $u_{\mathrm{sk}}$ uniformly in $v$
and so leave every argmax inside a fixed window unchanged.
This is invariant under $Z\mapsto ZQ$, $Q\in O(2)$, hence depends only on $\mathrm{span}(Z)$. The base frame score is
\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z):=\mathrm{HM}_3\!\bigl(u_{\mathrm{sk}}(Z),u_{\mathrm{cur}}(Z),u_{\mathrm{fut}}(Z)\bigr).
\end{align}

\paragraph{The four feature sets we tried, in order.}
We test increasingly intrusive feature sets and ask whether each makes $\hat Z^{(\sigma)}=Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$. Mapping back to the H1/H2/H3 taxonomy of §1: V1 and V2 are H3-style $\Delta\mathcal E$ additions (a new $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$-only feature; a new current--future cross feature); V3 is an optimisation-control baseline using the original H1 feature set $\{u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}}\}$ with no aggregator or weighting change, so it isolates the joint-optimisation question from the feature question; V4 is the H2 ceiling-diagnostic (oracle information injected explicitly to upper-bound how much closure any window-local feature could buy).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{V1.}] \textbf{HM with a future-direction term.}
\begin{align}
S_1(Z):=S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z)\cdot
\frac{\|A_{\mathrm{fut}}^\top A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z\|_F^2}{\|A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z\|_F^2}.
\end{align}
Multiplies the harmonic mean by a Rayleigh-style future-direction
strength, weighted by $A_{\mathrm{fut}}Z$. Tests whether
$Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ aligns with the leading singular directions of
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ alone.

\item[\textbf{V2.}] \textbf{HM with a current-future cross term.}
\begin{align}
S_2(Z):=S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z)\cdot
\frac{\langle A_{\mathrm{cur}} Z,\,A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z\rangle_F}
     {\|A_{\mathrm{cur}} Z\|_F\,\|A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z\|_F}.
\end{align}
This diagnostic uses the same-size-half-window convention:
$A_{\mathrm{cur}}$ and $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ have the same row count, and
the Frobenius inner product pairs row $i$ of the current half-window
with row $i$ of the future half-window. It boosts subspaces along which
$A_{\mathrm{cur}} Z$ and $A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z$ are aligned in
$\mathbb R^{n_w\times 2}$ under that row-paired convention. The
raw cosine factor is allowed to be negative; no clipping is applied.
In the reported windows the denominators are nonzero, and a zero
denominator would make the candidate frame inadmissible for this
diagnostic. Tests whether $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ sits where current and
future windows agree directionally.

\item[\textbf{V3.}] \textbf{HM, joint Stiefel optimization.}
\begin{align}
S_3(Z):=S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z),\qquad\text{maximised jointly over }Z\in\mathrm{St}(d,2).
\end{align}
Same feature set, but replaces the sequential ``rank-1, deflate, rank-1'' with a joint 2-frame ascent. Tests whether the per-direction decomposition was the bottleneck.

\item[\textbf{V4.}] \textbf{HM plus an oracle subspace reward.}
\begin{align}
S_4(Z):=S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z)+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2.
\end{align}
Adds a term rewarding $Z$ for lying in $\mathrm{span}(V_r)$. Uses information \emph{not} available from $B$, $A_w$, $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ alone---a diagnostic, not a deployable score. Quantifies how much oracle information must be injected before $\hat Z^{(S_4)}=Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Optimizer.}
For each of V1--V4 we maximise $S(Z)$ over 2-frames $Z$ whose range lies in $B_{\mathrm{union}}$. Write $Z=Q_{\mathrm{union}} W$ with $W\in\mathrm{St}(m,2)$, where $m=\dim B_{\mathrm{union}}$. The Euclidean gradient of every $u_X$ is closed-form,
\begin{align}
\nabla_Z u_X(Z)=\frac{2 A_X^\top A_X Z}{\|A_X\|_F^2},
\end{align}
so $\nabla S_{\mathrm{HM}}$ is closed-form by the chain rule on $\mathrm{HM}_3$, and likewise for $S_1,S_2,S_4$. Pull the gradient back to $W$ and run \emph{polar retraction} on $\mathrm{St}(m,2)$:
\begin{align}
W^+\leftarrow\mathrm{polar}\!\bigl(W+\eta\,(I-WW^\top)G_W\bigr),
\end{align}
where $G_W$ is the Euclidean gradient with respect to $W$ and $\eta$ is set by backtracking line search on the score. Random restarts are warm-started from (i) the feasible projected oracle $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}=\mathrm{polar}(P_{\mathrm{union}}V_2)$, (ii) the projection of the streaming state's leading directions into $B_{\mathrm{union}}$, and (iii) uniform Stiefel samples in the $Q_{\mathrm{union}}$ coordinates. For each matrix and each feature set we report two numbers side by side:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Greedy}: per-direction procedure (rank-1 ascent, deflate, rank-1 ascent on the orthogonal complement), yielding $\hat v^{(\sigma)}_1,\hat v^{(\sigma)}_2$;
\item \emph{Joint}: the joint $\mathrm{St}(d,2)$ ascent above, yielding $\hat Z^{(\sigma)}$.
\end{itemize}
The difference $j-g$ is the optimization audit: large means the per-direction decomposition left score on the table; near zero means both routines reach the same maximum and the gap to $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is a property of the score, not the optimizer.

\section*{3. Evidence and conclusion}

\paragraph{Headline outcome.}
On \texttt{diffuse-diffuse}, \texttt{residual-spiky-shocks}, and \texttt{mixed-tail-soft} at block 31, the four feature sets close as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{V1 (HM with future-direction term): fail.} The
$A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ direction term changes both
$S_1(Z_{\mathrm{oracle}})$ and the score at the non-oracle argmax, so
$\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ \emph{shrinks} numerically (compare V1 and V3
rows in Appendix~B.1) without making the oracle the maximiser. Because
V1 multiplies HM by a squared-energy scale, the V1 and V3 score values
should be compared as within-feature oracle-vs-winner gaps, not as
absolute cross-feature scores. The oracle does not align strongly
enough with the leading singular directions of $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$
alone.
\item \textbf{V2 (HM with current-future cross term): fail harder.} $S_2(Z_{\mathrm{oracle}})\approx 0$ on two of three matrices, so the oracle is actively \emph{anti-selected}: $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is not where $A_w Z$ and $A_{\mathrm{fut}} Z$ agree directionally under the normalised Frobenius inner product. ``Fail harder'' here means the feature is not merely uninformative but pathological --- it ranks $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ near the bottom rather than near the top.
\item \textbf{V3 (joint Stiefel ascent on plain HM): fail.} Joint matches greedy to within $|j{-}g|\le +0.0004$, so per-direction decomposition is not the bottleneck. $\hat Z^{(S_3)}$ remains strictly above $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ by $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}\in[+0.014,\,+0.032]$.
\item \textbf{V4 (HM plus $\lambda\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2$): partial closure at the span level.} On \texttt{residual-spiky-shocks} and \texttt{mixed-tail-soft}, $\lambda=0.1$ pushes both squared principal cosines into $[0.85,0.99]$ (specifically $\mathrm{pa}^2[0]\in\{0.99,0.99\}$ and $\mathrm{pa}^2[1]\in\{0.85,0.93\}$, see Appendix~B.2); \texttt{diffuse-diffuse} needs $\lambda=0.5$. A residual gap $\sim 0.003$--$0.005$ survives at $\lambda=0.5$ because $\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2$ is invariant under right-rotations of $Z$ inside $\mathrm{span}(V_r)\cap B_{\mathrm{union}}$.
\end{itemize}
The full V1--V3 score table and the V4 $\lambda$-sweep with principal-angle data are in Appendix~B.

\paragraph{Implication.}
The feature set $\{u_{\mathrm{sk}},u_{\mathrm{cur}},u_{\mathrm{fut}}\}$---even augmented with rotation-invariant terms built from $A_{\mathrm{fut}}$ alone or from a current-future cross product---is \emph{insufficient at rank 2 to identify $\mathrm{span}(V_2)$}. $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is defined by global geometry of $A^\top A$, while the available features are window-local energies. Closing the gap requires either
\begin{itemize}
\item (a) actual oracle-direction information ($V_4$-style; diagnostic only, not deployable), or
\item (b) a window-local quantity approximating the leading invariant subspace of $A^\top A$ from streaming data---e.g.\ a power-iteration-style inner product using stacked current-and-future rows in place of $A^\top A$. Any such quantity must itself pass the same evidence-sufficiency test (V3-style joint sweep) before entering the operational score.
\end{itemize}
The cheaper feature additions have been ruled out.

\paragraph{Status.}
The closure is now the operating principle for any future change to the
combined score: any new feature must first pass a joint
$\mathrm{St}(d,2)$ ascent against the oracle frame $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$,
with $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ as the argmax, before any operational
benchmark. Open work, in priority order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reconcile a small numerical discrepancy in the oracle-frame
score on \texttt{diffuse-diffuse} between an earlier landscape probe
(score at $\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_2$ logged at $\sim 0.297$,
not reproduced in the tables of this report) and the high-budget
probe in Appendix~A.4 ($\sigma(\hat v^{(\mathrm{oracle\,proj})}_2)
=0.2164$ under $S_6{+}E_2$). Likely a streaming-state seeding
difference; pin the RNG.
\item Build a frame-level row-cheat diagnostic to complement V4.
\item Investigate window-local approximations of the leading invariant
subspace of $A^\top A$, each gated by its own V3-style sufficiency
test before any aggregator change.
\end{enumerate}

$S_4(Z)=S_{\mathrm{HM}}(Z)+(\lambda/2)\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2$.
$\mathrm{pa}^2[i]\in[0,1]$ for $i\in\{0,1\}$ are the two squared
canonical cosines (sorted descending: $\mathrm{pa}^2[0]\ge
\mathrm{pa}^2[1]$) between $\mathrm{span}(\hat Z^{(\sigma)}_{\mathrm{joint}})$
and $\mathrm{span}(V_2)$; both equal $1$ iff the two 2-planes
coincide, $\mathrm{pa}^2[1]=0$ means one direction of $\hat
Z^{(\sigma)}_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is orthogonal to the oracle plane.

At $\lambda=0$, $\hat Z^{(\sigma)}_{\mathrm{joint}}$ on
\texttt{mixed-tail-soft} has $\mathrm{pa}^2[1]=0.01$: its second
direction is essentially \emph{orthogonal} to $\mathrm{span}(V_2)$.
By $\lambda=0.1$, the squared principal cosines on
\texttt{residual-spiky-shocks} and \texttt{mixed-tail-soft} reach
$0.93$--$0.99$ and the score gap shrinks to $+0.002$--$+0.007$;
\texttt{diffuse-diffuse} requires $\lambda\ge 0.5$. The residual gap
at $\lambda=0.5$ is structural: $\|V_r^\top Z\|_F^2$ is invariant
under right-rotations of $Z$ inside $\mathrm{span}(V_r)\cap
B_{\mathrm{union}}$, so within that 2-plane the HM landscape retains
its own argmax, generically distinct from the specific
orthonormalised $Z_{\mathrm{oracle}}$.
