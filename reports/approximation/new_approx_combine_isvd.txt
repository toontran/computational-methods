\paragraph{Combined score plus top singular directions.}
This variant of the combined-score method splits the retained sketch budget
$r$ into two parts,
\begin{align}
r=r_1+r_2,\qquad r_1,r_2\ge0.
\end{align}
The first $r_1$ retained directions are obtained by sequentially maximizing the
combined score of the previous note. The remaining $r_2$ directions are the
top right singular vectors of the stacked operator
\begin{align}
M_w:=
\begin{pmatrix}
B\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
restricted to the orthogonal complement of the score-selected directions.
This gives a hybrid basis: the combined score targets entropy-corrected
leverage, while the singular-value part guarantees coverage of the leading
quadratic energy of $M_w$.

\paragraph{Setup and notation.}
Let $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ be the full data matrix, let $A_w\in
\mathbb R^{k\times d}$ be the current window with $k=\mathrm{row}(A_w)$, let
$B\in\mathbb R^{r\times d}$ be the sketch carried from the previous window,
and let $R\in\mathbb R^{s\times d}$ be the old row memory. Write
\begin{align}
n_{\mathrm{seen}}&:=\text{\# raw rows represented by $B$ and $R$ before this window},
\\
n&:=\text{total \# rows in the full matrix or stream},
\\
p_w&:=\frac{n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k}{n},
\\
c_w&:=\frac{\log p_w}{2\log(s+k)},
\\
E_w(v)&:=\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2,
\\
C_w&:=
\begin{pmatrix}
R\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{(s+k)\times d}.
\end{align}
The combined score of the previous note is defined by
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w(v)
:=
E_w(v)\,p_w^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/(2\log(s+k))},
\end{align}
with pooled entropy proxy
\begin{align}
h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
=
-\log\left(\frac{\|C_wv\|_4^4}{\|C_wv\|_2^4}\right).
\end{align}
Assume $s+k>1$ and $0<p_w\le1$ when the correction is used. If no row memory
or previous sketch is available, take $R=\varnothing$, $s=0$, and $B=0$.

\paragraph{Closed-form rewrite of the entropy multiplier.}
The entropy multiplier can be turned into a pure monomial in the
$\ell_2$ and $\ell_4$ norms of $C_wv$. Write $y:=C_wv$. Substituting
$h_2^{(R,w)}(v)=-\log(\|y\|_4^4/\|y\|_2^4)$ into the multiplier gives
\begin{align}
p_w^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/(2\log(s+k))}
&=
\exp\!\left(
-\frac{h_2^{(R,w)}(v)}{2\log(s+k)}\log p_w
\right)
\\
&=
\exp\!\left(
-c_w\,h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
\right)
\\
&=
\exp\!\left(
c_w\log\!\frac{\|y\|_4^4}{\|y\|_2^4}
\right)
\\
&=
\left(\frac{\|y\|_4^4}{\|y\|_2^4}\right)^{c_w},
\end{align}
where the second line uses $c_w=\log p_w/(2\log(s+k))$ and the third line
uses $-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)=\log(\|y\|_4^4/\|y\|_2^4)$.

Because $y=C_wv$ is the stacked vector $(Rv;A_wv)$,
\begin{align}
\|y\|_2^2=\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2,
\qquad
\|y\|_4^4=\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4,
\end{align}
so the entropy multiplier admits the explicit form
\begin{align}
\phi_w(v)
:=
p_w^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/(2\log(s+k))}
=
\left(\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}\right)^{c_w}.
\end{align}
The combined score is therefore
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w(v)
=
E_w(v)\,\phi_w(v)
=
\left(\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)
\left(\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}\right)^{c_w}.
\end{align}
This is the form used in Step 1 below. Note $c_w\le0$ since $p_w\le1$ and
$s+k>1$, so $\phi_w(v)$ increases as the pooled responses become more
entropic (less concentrated).

\paragraph{Budget split.}
Fix integers $r_1,r_2\ge0$ with $r_1+r_2=r$. The allocation can be a fixed
ratio (for example $r_1=\lceil r/2\rceil$) or data-driven. A natural
data-driven rule is to set $r_1$ equal to the number of combined-score local
maxima whose relative score gain exceeds a tolerance, and to set
$r_2=r-r_1$. The construction below does not depend on how the split is made;
it requires only that $r_1+r_2=r$.

\paragraph{Step 1: combined-score directions.}
Compute $r_1$ orthonormal directions $v_1,\dots,v_{r_1}$ sequentially by
solving, for $j=1,\dots,r_1$,
\begin{align}
v_j
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ v_i^\top v=0\ \forall i<j}
\operatorname{score}_w(v).
\end{align}
Use the projected full-gradient ascent of the previous note: with
$Q_j:=[v_1,\dots,v_{j-1}]$ and tangent projection
$(I-Q_jQ_j^\top-vv^\top)$, take backtracking gradient steps on
$\operatorname{score}_w$ and re-project onto the feasible sphere. Collect the
result as
\begin{align}
V_1:=[v_1,\dots,v_{r_1}]\in\mathbb R^{d\times r_1}.
\end{align}
If $r_1=0$, set $V_1$ to the empty matrix.

\paragraph{Step 2: top right singular vectors of sketch plus window, orthogonal to $V_1$.}
Let $P_1:=I-V_1V_1^\top$ be the orthogonal projector onto the complement of
$\operatorname{span}(V_1)$. Consider the restricted operator
\begin{align}
M_wP_1=M_w-M_wV_1V_1^\top.
\end{align}
Compute the top $r_2$ right singular vectors of $M_wP_1$:
\begin{align}
V_2:=[u_{r_1+1},\dots,u_{r_1+r_2}]\in\mathbb R^{d\times r_2},
\end{align}
where $u_{r_1+1},\dots,u_{r_1+r_2}$ are unit-norm, mutually orthogonal, and
orthogonal to every column of $V_1$, and where the ordering is by
non-increasing singular value of $M_wP_1$. If $r_2=0$, set $V_2$ to the empty
matrix.

Equivalently, $V_2$ is the solution of
\begin{align}
\max_{V\in\mathbb R^{d\times r_2}}\|M_wV\|_F^2
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
V^\top V=I_{r_2},\ V_1^\top V=0.
\end{align}
Because the columns of $V_2$ live in $\operatorname{range}(P_1)$, they are
already orthogonal to $V_1$ by construction, so the full hybrid basis
\begin{align}
\widehat V_w:=[V_1,V_2]\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}
\end{align}
satisfies $\widehat V_w^\top\widehat V_w=I_r$.

\paragraph{Practical computation of Step 2.}
The top $r_2$ right singular vectors of $M_wP_1$ can be computed without
forming $M_wP_1$ explicitly. Let
\begin{align}
G:=M_w^\top M_w=B^\top B+A_w^\top A_w.
\end{align}
Since $P_1=I-V_1V_1^\top$ and $V_1^\top V_1=I_{r_1}$,
\begin{align}
(M_wP_1)^\top(M_wP_1)
=
P_1GP_1.
\end{align}
The right singular vectors of $M_wP_1$ are the eigenvectors of $P_1GP_1$
corresponding to its largest eigenvalues. Two implementations are convenient.

\emph{Projected Lanczos.} Apply Lanczos or block-Lanczos iteration to the
implicit operator $x\mapsto P_1(Gx)$ starting from a random
$\operatorname{range}(P_1)$-vector. Each matvec uses two matvecs with $M_w$
(one with $A_w$ and one with $B$, or alternatively one with $B$ stored in
the form $\Sigma V^\top$) and one rank-$r_1$ correction by $V_1$. Extract
the top $r_2$ Ritz pairs.

\emph{Deflated direct SVD.} When $(s+k)+r$ is small enough to form $M_w$
explicitly, compute the thin SVD of $M_w$, project the right singular vectors
by $P_1$, and orthonormalize. This is simpler to implement but costlier in
the streaming regime.

\paragraph{Assembling the retained right basis.}
The hybrid retained right basis is
\begin{align}
\widehat V_w=[V_1,V_2]\in\mathbb R^{d\times r},
\qquad
\widehat V_w^\top\widehat V_w=I_r.
\end{align}
The combined-score columns $V_1$ cover entropy-corrected leverage
directions; the singular columns $V_2$ cover the largest remaining
quadratic-energy directions of the sketch-plus-window.

\paragraph{Operator extraction from $\widehat V_w$.}
The remaining extraction is identical to the combined-score method. Form
\begin{align}
Y_w:=M_w\widehat V_w=[M_wV_1,\ M_wV_2],
\qquad
U_{\hat{},w}:=\operatorname{orth}(Y_w),
\end{align}
and compute the SVD of the left-projected restricted operator:
\begin{align}
U_{\hat{},w}^\top M_w
=
U_{\mathrm{small},w}\Sigma_wV_w^\top.
\end{align}
The sketch carried into the next window is
\begin{align}
B^{(w)}:=\Sigma_wV_w^\top\in\mathbb R^{r\times d}.
\end{align}
The row memory $R^{(w)}$ is updated from raw rows seen through window $w$,
not from sketch rows, exactly as in the combined-score method.

\paragraph{Step-2 efficiency in the stored-basis form.}
If the previous sketch is carried as
\begin{align}
B=SV^\top,\qquad V=[v_1,\dots,v_r],\qquad S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_r),
\end{align}
then
\begin{align}
B^\top B=VS^2V^\top,
\qquad
G=VS^2V^\top+A_w^\top A_w.
\end{align}
A matvec with $G$ costs one matvec with $A_w$, one matvec with $A_w^\top$,
and two rank-$r$ inner products with $V$. Composing with the projection
$P_1$ adds one rank-$r_1$ inner product per matvec. Hence one Lanczos step
on $P_1GP_1$ has cost dominated by the two matvecs with $A_w$.

\paragraph{First-window special case.}
If $B=0$, $R=\varnothing$, $s=0$, and $n_{\mathrm{seen}}=0$, then
$M_w=A_w$, $G=A_w^\top A_w$, and the hybrid basis is
\begin{align}
V_1:=[v_1,\dots,v_{r_1}],\qquad
V_2:=\text{top $r_2$ right singular vectors of }A_w(I-V_1V_1^\top),
\end{align}
where the $v_j$ solve the first-window combined-score problem
\begin{align}
v_j
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ v_i^\top v=0\ \forall i<j}
\|A_1v\|_2^2
\|A_1v\|_4^{4c_1}
\|A_1v\|_2^{-4c_1},
\qquad
c_1=\frac{\log(k/n)}{2\log k}.
\end{align}
The retained basis is again $\widehat V_1=[V_1,V_2]$, and the extraction
uses $Y_1=A_1\widehat V_1$, $U_{\hat{},1}=\operatorname{orth}(Y_1)$,
$U_{\hat{},1}^\top A_1=U_{\mathrm{small},1}\Sigma_1V_1^\top$, and
$B^{(1)}=\Sigma_1V_1^\top$.

\paragraph{Why orthogonalize the singular vectors against $V_1$.}
The combined-score directions in $V_1$ already lie in $\mathbb R^d$ and are
not in general singular vectors of $M_w$. Taking the raw top right singular
vectors of $M_w$ would typically produce columns that overlap with
$\operatorname{span}(V_1)$, wasting budget and making $\widehat V_w$
near-singular. Projecting $M_w$ by $P_1=I-V_1V_1^\top$ before taking its
top singular vectors ensures the two parts of $\widehat V_w$ are orthogonal
and that $V_2$ captures energy not already represented by $V_1$.

Projecting $M_w$ on the right (that is, restricting to
$\operatorname{range}(P_1)$) rather than on the left preserves the
interpretation: $\|M_wV_2\|_F^2$ is the largest Frobenius energy that can
be collected by $r_2$ right directions orthogonal to $V_1$. This is the
right quantity to maximize when the sketch $B^{(w)}=\Sigma_wV_w^\top$ is
meant to preserve $\|M_wv\|_2$ along retained directions.

\paragraph{Relation to pure variants.}
If $r_1=r$ and $r_2=0$, the method reduces to the combined-score method of
the previous note. If $r_1=0$ and $r_2=r$, the method reduces to the
standard Frequent Directions / incremental-SVD carry: the retained sketch
is the top-$r$ right singular factor of $M_w$, with no entropy correction.
Intermediate splits interpolate between these two extremes and let the
sketch simultaneously track leverage-heavy directions and leading-energy
directions.

\paragraph{Choosing the split adaptively.}
A simple adaptive rule is as follows. After solving for each combined-score
direction $v_j$, record the relative score gain
\begin{align}
\Delta_j
:=
\frac{\operatorname{score}_w(v_j)}
{\max_{i<j}\operatorname{score}_w(v_i)}.
\end{align}
Stop the score phase at the first $j$ with $\Delta_j\le\tau$ for a prescribed
tolerance $\tau\in(0,1)$, set $r_1:=j-1$ and $r_2:=r-r_1$. This keeps only
the combined-score directions that are noticeably distinct from the earlier
ones and lets the remaining budget go to singular directions, which are
cheap to compute.

\paragraph{Practical remarks.}
The combined-score step remains nonconvex, so multiple restarts are useful
for each $v_j$. The singular-direction step is convex in the
eigenvector-of-$P_1GP_1$ sense and usually converges in a handful of
Lanczos iterations. Numerically tiny denominators inside the score
(when $\|C_wv\|_2$, $\|C_wv\|_4$, or $E_w(v)$ is close to zero) should be
guarded as in the combined-score method. When $r_2>0$, the sketch
$B^{(w)}=\Sigma_wV_w^\top$ retains at least the top $r_2$ singular energy
of the sketch-plus-window minus any energy already in
$\operatorname{span}(V_1)$, so the hybrid method inherits the Frequent
Directions tail-error guarantee on the projected operator, while the
entropy-corrected $V_1$ part targets the sampling problems that a pure
singular-value sketch ignores.
