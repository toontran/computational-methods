\paragraph{Subset-mass upper-bound score, stated first.}
At window $w$, score a candidate right direction $v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ by
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
:=
\frac{\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2}{1-\psi_w(v)},
\end{align}
where the leak factor is
\begin{align}
\psi_w(v)
:=
\sqrt{
(n-n_{\mathrm{seen}}-k)\,
n^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/\log(s+k)}
},
\end{align}
and $h_2^{(R,w)}(v)$ is the pooled row-response collision entropy computed
from the raw rows currently available for entropy estimation,
\begin{align}
h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
:=
-\log\left(
\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}
\right)
=
4\log\|C_wv\|_2-4\log\|C_wv\|_4,
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
C_w:=
\begin{pmatrix}
R\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{(s+k)\times d},
\qquad
C_wv=
\begin{pmatrix}
Rv\\
A_wv
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
If this is the first window, take
$R=\varnothing$, $s=0$, $n_{\mathrm{seen}}=0$, and $B=0$. If this is a later
window with no old row sample, take only $R=\varnothing$ and $s=0$; the old
sketch $B$ and the count $n_{\mathrm{seen}}$ still describe the rows processed
before the current window. The score is well-defined only when
$\psi_w(v)<1$; see the practical-remarks paragraph for the treatment of the
vacuous regime.

\paragraph{Notation in detail.}
Let $\mathrm{row}(X)$ denote the number of rows of a matrix $X$. Let
\begin{align}
A \in \mathbb R^{n\times d}
\end{align}
be the full data matrix, let $A_w$ denote the current row-window, and let
$B \in \mathbb R^{r\times d}$ denote the sketch carried from the previous
windows. In addition to the sketch, carry a small row sample of old data
\begin{align}
R \in \mathbb R^{s\times d}.
\end{align}
Write
\begin{align}
A_w &\in \mathbb R^{k\times d},
\qquad
k=\mathrm{row}(A_w),
\\
n_{\mathrm{seen}}&:=\text{\# raw rows processed before this window},
\\
n&:=\text{total number of rows in the full matrix or stream},
\\
v&\in\mathbb S^{d-1}.
\end{align}
Use $[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}$ for row indices.
Denote the number of raw rows represented after the current window, the
seen-fraction, and the unseen complement-count by
\begin{align}
m_w
:=
n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k,
\qquad
p_w
:=
\frac{m_w}{n},
\qquad
q_w
:=
n-m_w
=
n(1-p_w).
\end{align}
Assume $s+k>1$ when the pooled entropy proxy is used, and assume
$0<m_w\le n$.

\paragraph{Explanation of the subset-mass upper bound.}
This note builds an \emph{upper} estimate for $\|Av\|_2^2$ from a
Cauchy--Schwarz subset-mass lemma and a dimension-normalized
entropy-preservation heuristic. The only data available at window $w$ are the
old sketch $B$, the current raw window $A_w$, and the old raw row memory $R$.
The sketch and current window provide an energy estimate, while the raw row
memory and current window provide the pooled row-response entropy proxy.

The underlying lemma is purely a statement about a probability distribution
\begin{align}
p_i
:=
\frac{(Av)_i^2}{\|Av\|_2^2},
\qquad
\sum_{i\in[n]}p_i=1,
\end{align}
and a subset $S\subseteq[n]$ with $|S|=m_w=n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k$
corresponding to the raw rows processed through the current window. The old
sketch summarizes the first $n_{\mathrm{seen}}$ processed rows, and the
current window contributes the next $k$ raw rows. Under this identification
the ``seen-row subset mass'' is
\begin{align}
Z_S
:=
\sum_{i\in S}p_i
\approx
\frac{\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2}{\|Av\|_2^2}
=
\frac{E_w(v)}{\|Av\|_2^2},
\end{align}
where $E_w(v):=\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2$ is the available seen-row energy
surrogate. The approximate equality becomes exact when the sketch $B$
preserves the old seen-row response energy on direction $v$.

\paragraph{Subset-mass lemma.}
Let $p=(p_1,\dots,p_n)$ satisfy $p_i\ge 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n p_i=1$, and let
$S\subseteq[n]$ with $|S|=m$. Let
$H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)
:=-\log(\sum_{i=1}^n p_i^2)
=4\log\|Av\|_2-4\log\|Av\|_4$.
Then
\begin{align}
Z_S
=
\sum_{i\in S}p_i
\ge
\max\!\left\{
0,\;
1-\sqrt{(n-m)\,e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}}
\right\}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Proof of the lemma.}
Let $S^c=[n]\setminus S$ with $|S^c|=n-m$ and $Z_{S^c}=1-Z_S$. Since
$p_i^2\ge 0$, the subset of squared masses lies below the total:
\begin{align}
\sum_{i\in S^c}p_i^2
\le
\sum_{i\in[n]}p_i^2
=
e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}.
\end{align}
By Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the constant-one vector on $S^c$,
\begin{align}
Z_{S^c}^2
=
\Big(\sum_{i\in S^c}p_i\cdot 1\Big)^2
\le
|S^c|\,\sum_{i\in S^c}p_i^2
=
(n-m)\sum_{i\in S^c}p_i^2.
\end{align}
Combining the two displays yields
\begin{align}
Z_{S^c}^2
\le
(n-m)\,e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
Z_{S^c}\le\sqrt{(n-m)\,e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}}.
\end{align}
Hence $Z_S=1-Z_{S^c}\ge 1-\sqrt{(n-m)\,e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}}$, and the
bound $Z_S\ge 0$ is trivial.\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{From the lemma to an upper bound on $\|Av\|_2^2$.}
Applying the lemma with $m=m_w=n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k$ and rearranging
$Z_S=E_w(v)/\|Av\|_2^2$ gives, whenever the right side is positive,
\begin{align}
\|Av\|_2^2
\le
\frac{E_w(v)}
{1-\sqrt{q_w\,e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}}}.
\end{align}
This bound requires one quantity not directly observable from the window plus
sketch: $H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)$, the full-matrix collision entropy on
direction $v$.

\paragraph{Dimension-normalized entropy heuristic.}
Approximate the full-matrix collision entropy by the pooled row-response
collision entropy, rescaled by the ratio of the logarithmic support sizes:
\begin{align}
\frac{H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}{\log n}
\approx
\frac{h_2^{(R,w)}(v)}{\log(s+k)}.
\end{align}
The pooled proxy uses the available rows $C_w=[R;A_w]$ of size $s+k$;
the full matrix has $n$ rows. Exponentiating the heuristic,
\begin{align}
e^{-H_2^{\mathrm{full}}(v)}
\approx
e^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)\,\log n/\log(s+k)}
=
n^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/\log(s+k)}.
\end{align}
Substituting into the upper bound and collecting constants gives
\begin{align}
\|Av\|_2^2
\;\lesssim\;
\frac{E_w(v)}
{1-\psi_w(v)},
\qquad
\psi_w(v)
=
\sqrt{q_w\,n^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/\log(s+k)}},
\end{align}
which is $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)$ as declared.

\paragraph{Simplified form.}
Define the constants
\begin{align}
q_w&:=n-n_{\mathrm{seen}}-k,
\\
\tau_w&:=\frac{\log n}{\log(s+k)},
\\
E_w(v)&:=\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2,
\\
y&:=C_wv.
\end{align}
Then
\begin{align}
\psi_w(v)
=
\sqrt{q_w}\,
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\tau_w}{2}\,h_2^{(R,w)}(v)\right),
\end{align}
and the score factors as
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
E_w(v)\,\gamma_w(v),
\qquad
\gamma_w(v)
:=
\frac{1}{1-\psi_w(v)}.
\end{align}
When $\psi_w(v)<1$, $\gamma_w(v)>1$ is a flatness-dependent inflation factor.
The inflation factor appears as a pole: small $h_2^{(R,w)}(v)$ drives
$\psi_w\to 1^-$ and $\gamma_w\to+\infty$.

\paragraph{Exact gradient.}
Let
\begin{align}
y_B:=Bv,
\qquad
z:=A_wv,
\qquad
y:=C_wv,
\end{align}
and assume $E_w(v)>0$, $\|y\|_2>0$, $\|y\|_4>0$, and $\psi_w(v)<1$. The
energy gradient is
\begin{align}
\nabla E_w(v)
=
2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv.
\end{align}
The pooled entropy gradient follows by differentiating
$4\log\|C_wv\|_2-4\log\|C_wv\|_4$:
\begin{align}
\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
=
4\,\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
-
4\,C_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right).
\end{align}
Here $x^{\odot 3}$ denotes componentwise cubing.
For the derivative of $\psi_w$, assume first that $q_w>0$. Since
$\log\psi_w(v)=\tfrac{1}{2}\log q_w-\tfrac{\tau_w}{2}h_2^{(R,w)}(v)$,
\begin{align}
\nabla\log\psi_w(v)
=
-\frac{\tau_w}{2}\,\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v),
\end{align}
so
\begin{align}
\nabla\psi_w(v)
=
\psi_w(v)\,\nabla\log\psi_w(v)
=
-\frac{\tau_w\,\psi_w(v)}{2}\,\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
The gradient of the denominator log is
\begin{align}
\nabla\log(1-\psi_w(v))
=
-\frac{\nabla\psi_w(v)}{1-\psi_w(v)}
=
\frac{\tau_w\,\psi_w(v)}{2(1-\psi_w(v))}\,\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
Taking logs of the score,
\begin{align}
\log\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
\log E_w(v)-\log(1-\psi_w(v)),
\end{align}
and differentiating,
\begin{align}
\nabla\log\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
\frac{\nabla E_w(v)}{E_w(v)}
-
\frac{\tau_w\,\psi_w(v)}{2(1-\psi_w(v))}\,\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{align}
\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
\left[
\frac{2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv}{E_w(v)}
-
2\kappa_w(v)
\left(
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
-
C_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
\right)
\right],
\end{align}
where the direction-dependent coupling coefficient is
\begin{align}
\kappa_w(v)
:=
\frac{\tau_w\,\psi_w(v)}{1-\psi_w(v)}
=
\frac{\log n}{\log(s+k)}\cdot
\frac{\psi_w(v)}{1-\psi_w(v)}.
\end{align}
The coefficient $\kappa_w(v)$ grows as $\psi_w(v)$ approaches $1$, so the
entropy-gradient part becomes dominant near the boundary of the non-vacuity
region.
If $q_w=0$, then $\psi_w(v)=0$ for every feasible direction and
$\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)=\nabla E_w(v)$.

\paragraph{Sign and monotonicity of the entropy term.}
The entropy contribution to the upper-bound gradient is
\begin{align}
-2\kappa_w(v)
\left(
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
-
C_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
\right)
=
2\kappa_w(v)
\left(
\frac{C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}
-
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
\right),
\end{align}
which pushes $v$ toward \emph{lower} $h_2^{(R,w)}(v)$ (since
$\kappa_w(v)>0$ whenever $\psi_w(v)\in(0,1)$). Thus maximizing this
upper-bound score favors higher-leakage directions inside the feasible
region: lowering the entropy increases $\psi_w(v)$, enlarges the denominator
inflation, and raises the score until the non-vacuity boundary is reached.

\paragraph{Expanded row-memory form.}
Because
\begin{align}
C_w^\top C_wv=R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv,
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}
=
R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3},
\end{align}
the pooled entropy gradient can be evaluated without explicitly forming
$C_w$:
\begin{align}
C_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
=
\frac{
R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3}
}{
\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4
},
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
=
\frac{R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv}
{\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2}.
\end{align}
Likewise,
\begin{align}
\psi_w(v)
=
\sqrt{q_w}\,
\left(
\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}
\right)^{\tau_w/2}.
\end{align}
The last identity uses
$e^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)}=\|C_wv\|_4^4/\|C_wv\|_2^4$ raised to the power
$\tau_w/2$, simplified via the pooled-norm expansions.

\paragraph{Stored-basis form.}
If
\begin{align}
B=SV^\top,
\qquad
V=[v_1,\dots,v_r],
\qquad
S=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_r),
\end{align}
then
\begin{align}
\|Bv\|_2^2
=
\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2(v_j^\top v)^2,
\qquad
B^\top Bv
=
\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2(v_j^\top v)v_j,
\end{align}
so the energy part of the subset-mass gradient is
\begin{align}
2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv
=
2\sum_{j=1}^r s_j^2(v_j^\top v)v_j
+2A_w^\top A_wv.
\end{align}
The pooled entropy part is independent of this stored-basis representation of
$B$, since $B$ does not appear in $C_w$.

\paragraph{Optimization problem.}
To extract a direction, solve
\begin{align}
\max_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ Q^\top v=0,\ \psi_w(v)<1}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v),
\end{align}
where the columns of $Q$ are the previously accepted directions. The extra
$\psi_w(v)<1$ constraint restricts the feasible set to directions on which
the upper bound is non-vacuous. In practice this constraint restricts
attention to directions whose pooled collision entropy exceeds the minimum
non-vacuity threshold
\begin{align}
h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
>
h_{\mathrm{crit}}
:=
\frac{\log q_w}{\tau_w}
=
\frac{\log q_w\cdot\log(s+k)}{\log n}.
\end{align}
Directions with $h_2^{(R,w)}(v)\le h_{\mathrm{crit}}$ are declared infeasible
under this score.

\paragraph{Projected full-gradient ascent.}
Use the exact gradient of the subset-mass upper-bound score. Project it onto
the feasible tangent space:
\begin{align}
g_{\mathrm{tan}}(v)
:=
\left(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top\right)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v).
\end{align}
Take
\begin{align}
\widetilde v^+=v+\eta\,g_{\mathrm{tan}}(v),
\qquad
v^+=\frac{(I-QQ^\top)\widetilde v^+}
{\|(I-QQ^\top)\widetilde v^+\|_2},
\end{align}
with $\eta>0$ chosen by backtracking line search on
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}$. Reject any step that leaves the
non-vacuity region $\psi_w(v)<1$.

\paragraph{Basis-expansion interpretation.}
If one is building a local search subspace rather than taking a pure
steepest-ascent step, the new direction added to the basis is the projected
full gradient
\begin{align}
g_{\mathrm{basis}}(v)
:=
\left(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top\right)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v).
\end{align}
In a reduced-subspace version with current local right search basis
$Z\in\mathbb R^{d\times q}$, $Z^\top Z=I$, the expansion residual after
solving inside $\operatorname{span}(Z)$ is
\begin{align}
r_Z(v)
:=
\left(I-ZZ^\top\right)
\left(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top\right)
\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v).
\end{align}
The local basis should be expanded when
$\|r_Z(v)\|_2
/
\|\left(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top\right)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)\|_2
>\tau$.

\paragraph{Operator extraction from the retained right subspace.}
After the scored directions and any auxiliary directions are assembled into an
orthonormal right basis $V\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$, form
\begin{align}
M_w:=
\begin{pmatrix}
B\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Y:=M_wV,
\qquad
U_{\hat{}}:=\operatorname{orth}(Y),
\end{align}
and compress $M_w$ to the left image of the retained right space,
\begin{align}
\widetilde M:=U_{\hat{}}^\top M_w
=U_{\mathrm{small}}\Sigma V_{\mathrm{new}}^\top.
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{orth}(Y)$ denotes any matrix with orthonormal columns
spanning the column space of $Y$. Equivalently,
$P_U:=U_{\hat{}}U_{\hat{}}^\top$ is the orthogonal projector onto the left
image of the retained right space.
The carried sketch is
\begin{align}
B_{\mathrm{next}}:=\Sigma V_{\mathrm{new}}^\top,
\end{align}
so the next window receives a compact right-action representation of the
compressed operator selected by the optimized basis $V$.

\paragraph{Why this is the sketch carried into the next window.}
For any direction $v$,
\begin{align}
\|B_{\mathrm{next}}v\|_2
=
\|\Sigma V_{\mathrm{new}}^\top v\|_2
=
\|P_UM_wv\|_2,
\end{align}
so $\|B_{\mathrm{next}}v\|_2^2$ equals the quadratic old energy on the
retained compressed operator. The row memory $R_{\mathrm{next}}$ supplies the
pooled row-level entropy proxy for the next window's
$h_2^{(R,w+1)}$.

\paragraph{First-window special case.}
If $B=0$, $R=\varnothing$, $s=0$, and $n_{\mathrm{seen}}=0$, then
\begin{align}
p_1=\frac{k}{n},
\qquad
q_1=n-k,
\qquad
\tau_1=\frac{\log n}{\log k},
\qquad
C_1=A_1,
\end{align}
and the first-window score reduces to
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
\frac{\|A_1v\|_2^2}
{1-\psi_1(v)},
\qquad
\psi_1(v)
=
\sqrt{n-k}\,
\left(
\frac{\|A_1v\|_4^4}{\|A_1v\|_2^4}
\right)^{\tau_1/2}.
\end{align}
The full gradient reduces to
\begin{align}
\nabla\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
=
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)
\left[
\frac{2A_1^\top A_1v}{\|A_1v\|_2^2}
-
2\kappa_1(v)
\left(
\frac{A_1^\top A_1v}{\|A_1v\|_2^2}
-
A_1^\top\!\left(\frac{(A_1v)^{\odot 3}}{\|A_1v\|_4^4}\right)
\right)
\right],
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
\kappa_1(v)=\frac{\tau_1\,\psi_1(v)}{1-\psi_1(v)}.
\end{align}
Compute retained directions $v_1,\dots,v_r$ sequentially by solving
\begin{align}
v_j
\in
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ v_i^\top v=0\ \forall i<j,\ \psi_1(v)<1}
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{UB}}(v).
\end{align}

\paragraph{Non-vacuity and the valid direction regime.}
The score is finite and positive only when $\psi_w(v)<1$, i.e.
\begin{align}
h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
>
h_{\mathrm{crit}}
:=
\frac{\log q_w}{\tau_w}.
\end{align}
This threshold statement assumes $q_w>0$. If $q_w=0$, all rows have been
processed, $\psi_w(v)=0$, and the subset-mass denominator is exactly $1$.
For the first window with $n=128$, $k=32$, $\tau_1=\log 128/\log 32=7/5$,
$q_1=96$:
\begin{align}
h_{\mathrm{crit}}
=
\frac{\log 96}{7/5}
\approx
\frac{4.564}{1.4}
\approx
3.26.
\end{align}
The first-window maximum pooled entropy is $\log k=\log 32\approx 3.47$, so
the feasible region is narrow and includes essentially only block-flat
directions. Spike-like directions (low $h_2^{(R,w)}$) are declared infeasible
by this score, implicitly filtering them out rather than scoring them low.
As $n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k$ grows, $q_w$ shrinks and the feasible region
widens.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the monotonicity flip.}
The subset-mass upper bound is a statement of the form
``the fraction of full-matrix energy captured by the seen rows is at
least $1-\psi_w(v)$'': flat directions leak little energy to the unseen
complement, while spike directions can leak essentially all of it if the
spike lies in the unseen block. Maximizing
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)=E_w(v)/(1-\psi_w(v))$
drives $v$ toward directions on which the block energy could plausibly come
from a much larger full-matrix energy, i.e.\ toward higher-leakage (spikier)
directions within the feasible region. An objective that instead maximizes a
lower estimate obtained by rewarding high pooled entropy would have the
opposite preference: it would favor flat directions because their energy is
less likely to be hidden in unseen rows.

The upper-bound score is therefore most naturally used either as a
\emph{diagnostic} on candidate directions (large
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}$ means the block evidence is
consistent with a large full-matrix energy, but does not imply it) or as a
\emph{gate}: the feasibility constraint $\psi_w(v)<1$ prunes directions on
which block evidence cannot support any bounded claim about $\|Av\|_2^2$.
One natural streaming use is therefore to optimize a separate lower-estimate
score for selecting directions, while using the upper-bound non-vacuity
condition as a gate on whether the available block evidence supports a bounded
claim.

\paragraph{Practical remarks.}
The objective is nonconvex in the feasible region, and becomes singular at
its boundary $\psi_w(v)=1$. Steps should be rejected or dampened when
$1-\psi_w(v)$ is numerically tiny; a practical guard is to replace the
denominator by $\max(1-\psi_w(v),\epsilon)$ for small $\epsilon>0$ during
line search, while keeping the feasibility check $\psi_w(v)<1$ on accepted
iterates. When $\|C_wv\|_2$ or $\|C_wv\|_4$ is numerically tiny, regularize
by adding $\delta\cdot\|v\|_2^2$ to both $\|C_wv\|_2^2$ and
$\|C_wv\|_4^4$, or reject the step.

The expanded gradient form avoids forming $C_w$ explicitly, but the pooled
response norms must still include both $Rv$ and $A_wv$.

The row memory should not be interpreted as a replacement for the old
sketch. The sketch $B$ supplies retained old energy, while $R$ supplies old
raw-row responses for the pooled entropy proxy. A pure reservoir sample
gives coverage, but it can miss rare large rows that dominate $\ell_4$
terms. A practical memory should therefore combine a uniform reservoir with
a small high-response set selected using current sketch directions.

The non-vacuity constraint $\psi_w(v)<1$ is more restrictive for the first
window and loosens as $p_w\to 1$. A safe implementation caps
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{UB}}(v)$ at a large finite value when
$\psi_w(v)$ approaches $1$ from below, and marks the direction as
``upper-bound inconclusive'' rather than selecting it on the strength of a
near-singular denominator.
