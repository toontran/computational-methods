\paragraph{Concentration lower-bound score, stated first.}
At window $w$, score a candidate right direction $v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$ by
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
:=
\frac{E_w(v)}{p_w}
\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}\right)_{\!+},
\end{align}
where
\begin{align}
E_w(v)
&:=
\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2,
\\
k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)
&:=
\frac{\|C_wv\|_2^4}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}
=
e^{h_2^{(R,w)}(v)},
\\
C_w
&:=
\begin{pmatrix}
R\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{(s+k)\times d},
\qquad
p_w:=\frac{n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k}{n},
\end{align}
and $c_\delta>0$ is a confidence multiplier controlling the failure
probability. The operator $(x)_+:=\max\{x,0\}$ clamps the score at zero when
the bound is vacuous. If this is the first window and no old row sample is
available, take $R=\varnothing$ and $s=0$; if no old sketch is available,
take $B=0$ and $n_{\mathrm{seen}}=0$.

\paragraph{Notation in detail.}
Let $A\in\mathbb R^{n\times d}$ be the full data matrix, let $A_w$ denote
the current row-window with $k$ rows, let $B\in\mathbb R^{r\times d}$ denote
the sketch carried from previous windows, and let $R\in\mathbb R^{s\times d}$
denote a raw row memory. Write $v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$, $x:=Av\in\mathbb R^n$,
$y:=A_wv\in\mathbb R^k$, and $T:=\|x\|_2^2=\|Av\|_2^2$. Denote the number of
raw rows represented after the current window and the seen-fraction by
\begin{align}
m_w:=n_{\mathrm{seen}}+k,
\qquad
p_w:=\frac{m_w}{n}.
\end{align}
Assume $s+k\ge 2$ so that the pooled participation ratio is well-defined and
$p_w\in(0,1]$.

\paragraph{Motivation: bound and argmax, simultaneously.}
The combined lower-bound score
$E_w(v)\cdot p_w^{-h_2^{(R,w)}(v)/(s+k)}$
uses entropy as a multiplicative reward, with an exponent $1/(s+k)$ whose
argmax is empirically dominated by the $E_w$ factor on structured matrices.
The subset-mass upper-bound score $E_w(v)/(1-\psi_w(v))$ is monotone in the
wrong direction: its argmax prefers low entropy. Neither is simultaneously a
tight numerical estimator of $\|Av\|_2^2$ and a selection criterion whose
argmax approaches the oracle direction.

This note derives an objective that is both a valid (high-probability)
numerical lower bound on $\|Av\|_2^2$ \emph{and} a selection criterion whose
argmax favors flat directions where the bound is informative, from the
standard unbiased plug-in estimator and a one-sided Chebyshev inequality.

\paragraph{Setup: unbiased block estimator.}
Specialize first to the one-block, no-memory case ($B=0$, $R=\varnothing$,
$s=0$, $n_{\mathrm{seen}}=0$) for clarity; the pooled form follows at the
end. Draw the $k$ window rows i.i.d.\ uniformly from the $n$ full rows (with
replacement; the with-/without-replacement distinction is lower order for
$k\ll n$). For any row $i$,
\begin{align}
\Pr[i\in B]=\frac{k}{n}=p_w.
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{align}
\mathbb E\!\left[\|A_wv\|_2^2\right]
=
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\cdot\Pr[i\in B]
=
p_w\,T,
\end{align}
so the rescaled block estimator
\begin{align}
S:=\frac{\|A_wv\|_2^2}{p_w}
\end{align}
satisfies $\mathbb E[S]=T$.

\paragraph{Variance in closed form.}
Write $\|A_wv\|_2^2=\sum_{j=1}^k\xi_j$ with $\xi_j=x_{I_j}^2$ for
i.i.d.\ uniform row indices $I_j\in[n]$. Each $\xi_j$ has
$\mathbb E[\xi_j]=T/n$ and
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}(\xi_j)
=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i x_i^4-\left(\frac{T}{n}\right)^2
=\frac{\|x\|_4^4}{n}-\frac{T^2}{n^2}.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)
=k\cdot\operatorname{Var}(\xi_j)
=p_w\!\left(\|x\|_4^4-\frac{T^2}{n}\right),
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}(S)
=\frac{1}{p_w^2}\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)
=\frac{1}{p_w}\!\left(\|x\|_4^4-\frac{T^2}{n}\right).
\end{align}
Using the full-matrix participation ratio
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x):=T^2/\|x\|_4^4$,
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}(S)
=\frac{T^2}{p_w}\!\left(\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}-\frac{1}{n}\right)
=T^2\cdot\frac{n-k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}{k\cdot k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}.
\end{align}
Sanity checks:
\begin{itemize}
\item Flat direction, $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=n$:
$\operatorname{Var}(S)=0$. The estimator concentrates perfectly, since every
row contributes identically.
\item Spike direction, $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=1$:
$\operatorname{Var}(S)=T^2(n-1)/k\approx T^2/p_w$. The standard deviation is
on the order of $T$ itself, so single-block estimation is uninformative.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{One-sided Chebyshev lower bound.}
By Chebyshev's inequality,
\begin{align}
\Pr[T\ge S+t]
\le\Pr\!\bigl[|S-T|\ge t\bigr]
\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(S)}{t^2}.
\end{align}
Setting the right-hand side equal to $\delta$ and solving,
$t=\operatorname{SD}(S)/\sqrt\delta$. Rearranging the event,
\begin{align}
\Pr\!\left[T\ge S-\frac{\operatorname{SD}(S)}{\sqrt\delta}\right]
\ge 1-\delta.
\end{align}
Wait---this is the wrong side; for a lower bound on $T$ we want the upper
tail $\Pr[S-T\le-t]$. By symmetry of Chebyshev in $|S-T|$, the same
inequality applies:
\begin{align}
\Pr\!\left[T\ge S\cdot\left(1-\frac{\operatorname{SD}(S)/S}{\sqrt\delta}\right)\right]
\ge 1-\delta,
\end{align}
provided the right-hand side is treated as a lower estimate rather than a
numerical evaluation of $T$. Using the variance formula above,
\begin{align}
\frac{\operatorname{SD}(S)}{T}
=\sqrt{\frac{n-k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}{k\cdot k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)}}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Plug-in estimator.}
Both $T$ and $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)$ are unobservable. Replace $T$ by $S$
(making the confidence width self-referential) and replace
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)$ by the block participation ratio
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y):=\|y\|_2^4/\|y\|_4^4$ where $y=A_wv$. Under the
dimension-normalized entropy heuristic
$h_2(x)/\log n\approx h_2(y)/\log k$, these plug-ins agree to leading order
for flat directions and both collapse to $1$ for concentrated spike
directions, so the flat/spike scaling of the confidence width is preserved.

For $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)\ll n$, drop the $n-k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ correction
in the numerator to get the compact form
\begin{align}
\frac{\operatorname{SD}(S)}{S}
\;\approx\;
\sqrt{\frac{1}{p_w\,k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)}},
\end{align}
and the Chebyshev LB becomes
\begin{align}
T
\;\gtrsim\;
S\cdot\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)}}\right),
\qquad
c_\delta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_w\,\delta}}.
\end{align}
Clamping at zero to avoid reporting negative lower bounds yields the
one-block score
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
=
\frac{\|A_wv\|_2^2}{p_w}
\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)}}\right)_{\!+}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Interpretation and argmax behavior.}
The score has three regimes determined entirely by $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Flat direction} ($k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)\approx k$):
$c_\delta/\sqrt{k}$ is small, so the multiplicative correction is near $1$
and $\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{conc}}\approx\|A_wv\|_2^2/p_w\approx T$.
The bound is tight and the value agrees with the naive unbiased estimator.
\item \textbf{Moderately concentrated}
($c_\delta^2<k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)<k$): the correction discounts the estimate
but remains positive. The bound is informative, with width proportional to
$1/\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}$.
\item \textbf{Spike direction} ($k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)\le c_\delta^2$):
$c_\delta/\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}}\ge 1$, the bracket clamps to $0$, and the
direction is declared \emph{vacuous}. The single-block data does not
support any positive lower bound on $T$; the score refuses to commit.
\end{itemize}
The argmax of $\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{conc}}$ therefore selects
directions on which the block evidence is both high-energy \emph{and}
sufficiently spread. Spike-like directions are filtered out not by an
adversarial penalty but by the statistical impossibility of certifying their
energy from one block.

\paragraph{Fundamental obstruction to single-block tight bounds on spikes.}
The variance formula $\operatorname{Var}(S)=T^2(n-k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x))/(k\cdot
k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x))$ shows that the standard deviation of the unbiased
estimator $S$ is $\Omega(T)$ whenever $k_{\mathrm{eff}}(x)=O(1)$. Any
estimator formed from one block of size $k<n$ inherits this variance floor,
so no single-block two-sided bound can be tight on a pure spike direction.
The concentration score captures this obstruction by design: it is tight
where Chebyshev is informative and explicitly vacuous where it is not.

\paragraph{Tighter confidence constants via Bernstein.}
The Chebyshev constant $c_\delta=(p_w\delta)^{-1/2}$ scales polynomially in
$1/\delta$. For small target failure probabilities, Bernstein's inequality
for bounded i.i.d.\ summands tightens this to
\begin{align}
c_\delta^{\mathrm{Bern}}
=
\sqrt{2\log(1/\delta)\cdot\frac{1-p_w}{p_w}}
+
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{3}\cdot\frac{\max_i x_i^2/T}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}(y)}},
\end{align}
scaling as $O(\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})$ up to the leverage term
$\max_i x_i^2/T$. The leverage term can be bounded in practice by the
block-observed leverage $\max_j y_j^2/\|y\|_2^2$ when the block is large
enough to contain the dominant row with high probability; otherwise the
Chebyshev form is preferred because Bernstein becomes dominated by the
leverage term.

\paragraph{Pooled streaming form with sketch and row memory.}
Restoring the general window $w$, replace the single-block energy by the
seen energy surrogate $E_w(v)=\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2$ and the block
participation ratio by the pooled one,
\begin{align}
k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)
:=
\frac{\|C_wv\|_2^4}{\|C_wv\|_4^4},
\qquad
C_w=
\begin{pmatrix}
R\\
A_w
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
The unbiased estimator becomes $E_w(v)/p_w$; its variance is reduced
relative to one block because $R$ supplies additional independent row
samples. Substituting into the Chebyshev LB and plugging in the pooled
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}$ gives
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
=
\frac{E_w(v)}{p_w}
\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}\right)_{\!+}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Exact gradient.}
Let $y_B:=Bv$, $z:=A_wv$, $y:=C_wv$, and assume $E_w(v)>0$, $\|y\|_2>0$,
$\|y\|_4>0$, and $c_\delta<\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}$ (bracket
strictly positive). Write
\begin{align}
L(v):=\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}\right).
\end{align}
The energy gradient is $\nabla E_w(v)=2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv$. The pooled
entropy gradient is
\begin{align}
\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v)
=
4\,\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
-
4\,\frac{C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4},
\end{align}
and since $k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)=e^{h_2^{(R,w)}(v)}$,
\begin{align}
\nabla\log k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)
=
\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
Differentiating $L$,
\begin{align}
\nabla L(v)
=
\frac{c_\delta}{2\,\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}\,
\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
Taking logs of the (positive-bracket) score,
$\log\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)=\log E_w(v)-\log p_w+\log L(v)$,
and differentiating,
\begin{align}
\nabla\log\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
=
\frac{\nabla E_w(v)}{E_w(v)}
+
\frac{1}{L(v)}\,\frac{c_\delta}{2\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}\,
\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v).
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{align}
\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
=
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v)
\left[
\frac{2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv}{E_w(v)}
+
2\lambda_w(v)
\left(
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
-
\frac{C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}
\right)
\right],
\end{align}
with direction-dependent coupling
\begin{align}
\lambda_w(v)
:=
\frac{c_\delta}{L(v)\,\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}}
=
\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)}-c_\delta}.
\end{align}
The sign of the entropy contribution coincides with
$\nabla h_2^{(R,w)}(v)$, so the gradient pushes $v$ toward \emph{higher}
pooled entropy. $\lambda_w(v)$ is strictly positive in the feasible region
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)>c_\delta^2$ and diverges as the direction
approaches the vacuity boundary, so the entropy reward intensifies as the
confidence width approaches its maximum.

\paragraph{Expanded row-memory form.}
Because
$C_w^\top C_wv=R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv$ and
$C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}=R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3}$,
the pooled gradient can be evaluated without forming $C_w$:
\begin{align}
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
=
\frac{R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv}{\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2},
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\frac{C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}
=
\frac{R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3}}
{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Optimization problem and feasibility.}
Extract directions sequentially by solving
\begin{align}
\max_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ Q^\top v=0,\
k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)>c_\delta^2}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v),
\end{align}
where the columns of $Q$ are previously accepted directions. The feasibility
constraint $k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)>c_\delta^2$ is equivalent to
$h_2^{(R,w)}(v)>2\log c_\delta$ and has a meaningful minimum at the
confidence threshold. For $n=128$, $k=32$, $s=0$, and $\delta=0.1$:
$c_\delta=(p_w\delta)^{-1/2}=(0.25\cdot 0.1)^{-1/2}=\sqrt{40}\approx 6.32$,
so $c_\delta^2\approx 40$, requiring
$k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)\gtrsim 40$. Since the first-window pooled
pool has only $k=32$ rows, the constraint is unsatisfiable under Chebyshev
at $\delta=0.1$ on this problem size, and a Bernstein constant must be used
or $\delta$ relaxed.

\paragraph{Projected full-gradient ascent.}
Within the feasible set, use the exact gradient projected onto the tangent
space of the sphere and the column complement of $Q$:
\begin{align}
g_{\mathrm{tan}}(v)
=
\left(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top\right)
\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{conc}}(v).
\end{align}
Backtracking Armijo on $\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{conc}}$ yields a
monotone-ascent solver. Reject any step that crosses the feasibility
boundary $k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w)}(v)=c_\delta^2$: the step either lands in
the clamped region (where the score is constantly zero) or runs into the
non-differentiable join.

\paragraph{Frame-level variant.}
For rank-$r$ extraction, the frame estimator
$S_{\mathrm{frame}}:=\sum_{j=1}^r\|A_wv_j\|_2^2/p_w$ has variance
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Var}(S_{\mathrm{frame}})
=
\frac{1}{p_w^2}\,k\,\operatorname{Var}_i
\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^r(A v_j)_i^2\right),
\end{align}
which involves the frame row-response $\sum_j x_{i,j}^2$ instead of the
single-direction $x_i^2$. When the retained directions share spike rows,
the inter-direction correlations reduce the frame variance below the sum of
per-direction variances, so a frame-level concentration LB can be tighter
than $\sum_j\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{conc}}(v_j)$. This yields a
coupled Stiefel objective
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{frame,conc}}(V)
=
\frac{\sum_j E_w(v_j)}{p_w}
\left(1-\frac{c_\delta}{\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w,r)}(V)}}\right)_{\!+},
\end{align}
with $V=[v_1,\dots,v_r]$ on the Stiefel manifold and frame-level
participation ratio
\begin{align}
k_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(R,w,r)}(V)
:=
\frac{\left(\sum_i(\sum_j (C_wV)_{i,j}^2)\right)^2}
{\sum_i(\sum_j (C_wV)_{i,j}^2)^2}.
\end{align}
The derivation is unchanged: Chebyshev on the frame-level unbiased
estimator, variance via the frame row-response, plug-in using pooled
quantities.

\paragraph{Practical remarks.}
The confidence constant $c_\delta$ must be chosen so that the feasible
region contains plausibly-informative directions; with Chebyshev on small
pools this can be restrictive, and Bernstein or Hoeffding forms are
preferable when the block-observed leverage
$\max_j y_j^2/\|y\|_2^2$ is small. The clamp $(\cdot)_+$ should be treated
as a soft gate in continuous optimization: replace it by the smoothed
alternative $(\cdot)\cdot\sigma(\alpha\cdot\cdot)$ with $\sigma$ a sigmoid
and $\alpha$ large, or carry the pre-clamp score plus a separate feasibility
flag.

The concentration score is not a replacement for the combined lower-bound
score; it is a complement. The combined score extracts per-direction
information from the full window participation-ratio reward; the
concentration score refuses to commit to directions where the single-window
evidence is statistically insufficient. A natural two-stage pipeline is
therefore: (i) use $\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{conc}}$ to lock flat
directions where the bound is tight, then (ii) use
$\operatorname{score}^{\mathrm{comb}}$ (or its oracle-informed variants) on
the residual operator to hunt spike directions that require pooled
multi-window evidence.
