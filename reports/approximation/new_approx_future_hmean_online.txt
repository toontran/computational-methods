\paragraph{Setting.}
Let $A\in\mathbb R^{n\times d}$ be partitioned into row windows
$A_1,\dots,A_W$ processed one at a time. At window $w$ the state carried
from earlier windows is a sketch $B\in\mathbb R^{r_\text{sk}\times d}$ and
a row sample (reservoir) $R\in\mathbb R^{r_\text{R}\times d}$. In the
lookahead variant developed here, the state also includes a fixed-size
future buffer $F_w$ containing a contiguous row block observed after the
current block with a lag of $\mathrm{row}(F_w)$ rows. Let
\begin{align}
N_w:=\begin{pmatrix}A_1\\ \vdots\\ A_w\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_w:=\begin{pmatrix}R\\ A_w\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
L_w:=\begin{pmatrix}A_w\\ F_w\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_w:=\begin{pmatrix}B\\ A_w\end{pmatrix}.
\end{align}
For a nonzero $y$, the Rényi-2 / inverse-participation entropy is
\begin{align}
h_2(y):=-\log\!\left(\frac{\|y\|_4^4}{\|y\|_2^4}\right)=4\log\|y\|_2-4\log\|y\|_4,
\end{align}
bounded between $0$ (one-spike) and $\log\dim(y)$ (uniform).
Throughout $v\in\mathbb S^{d-1}$; assume $\mathrm{row}(C_w)>1$,
$\mathrm{row}(L_w)>1$, and $\mathrm{row}(N_w)\le n$.

The current window $A_w$ and the lookahead buffer $F_w$ are disjoint row
sets. Setting $F_w=\varnothing$ and ignoring assumption (A1$'$) below
recovers a pure past-state online method driven by the additive score
alone.

\paragraph{Assumptions.}
Two past-state approximations:
\begin{align}
\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2
&\;\approx\;
\|N_wv\|_2^2,
\tag{A1}
\\
\frac{h_2(C_wv)}{\log\mathrm{row}(C_w)}
\;\approx\;
\frac{h_2(N_wv)}{\log\mathrm{row}(N_w)}
&\;\approx\;
\frac{h_2(Av)}{\log n}.
\tag{A2}
\end{align}
(A1) says the sketch preserves the aggregate past-row energy; (A2) says
the pooled reservoir-plus-current entropy tracks the full-matrix entropy
at the same rate.

For the future-consistency variant, add a stationarity assumption on
per-row squared action:
\begin{align}
\frac{\|A_wv\|_2^2}{\mathrm{row}(A_w)}
\;\approx\;
\frac{\|F_wv\|_2^2}{\mathrm{row}(F_w)}
\;\approx\;
\frac{\|Av\|_2^2}{n}
\;=:\;\lambda(v).
\tag{A1$'$}
\end{align}
(A1$'$) says that $v$'s per-row gain is the same on the current window,
on the immediately-following lookahead rows, and on the full matrix. It
is strictly stronger than (A1): (A1) requires only additive energy
conservation of the sketch, while (A1$'$) requires rate stationarity
across row blocks. (A1$'$) is plausible when $v$ captures genuinely
repeated structure across row blocks and is violated by directions whose
support lives in a single window.

\paragraph{Resulting inequality.}
Under (A2) and $\|N_wv\|_4\le\|Av\|_4$,
\begin{align}
\|Av\|_2^2
\;\gtrsim\;
\|N_wv\|_2^2
\left(\frac{\mathrm{row}(N_w)}{n}\right)^{-h_2(N_wv)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(N_w))}.
\tag{$\star$}
\end{align}
The exponent $-h_2/(2\log\mathrm{row})$ is an entropy-dependent
subsampling correction: if the response $N_wv$ is diffuse (large $h_2$),
the subsample gain closely approximates the full-matrix gain; if it is
concentrated (small $h_2$), the correction factor rises, reflecting the
possibility that subsampling may miss concentrated support rows.

\paragraph{Additive combined score.}
Substituting (A1) and (A2) into $(\star)$ gives
\begin{align}
\|Av\|_2^2
\;\gtrsim\;
\left(\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)\phi_w(v)
\;=:\;\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v),
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
\phi_w(v)
:=
\left(
\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}
\right)^{c_w},
\qquad
c_w:=\frac{\log(\mathrm{row}(N_w)/n)}{2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w)}.
\end{align}
The gain factor $\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2$ is a proxy for $\|N_wv\|_2^2$
by (A1). Since $\mathrm{row}(N_w)\le n$, $c_w\le 0$, and high-entropy
pooled vectors are boosted by the correction. If no old row sample is
available, take $R=\varnothing$; if no old sketch is available, take
$B=0$.

\paragraph{Future-consistency harmonic-mean score.}
Define per-row rate estimators
\begin{align}
\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v):=\frac{\|A_wv\|_2^2}{\mathrm{row}(A_w)},
\qquad
\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v):=\frac{\|F_wv\|_2^2}{\mathrm{row}(F_w)},
\end{align}
and the harmonic mean
\begin{align}
\mathrm{HM}(x,y):=\frac{2xy}{x+y+\varepsilon}.
\end{align}
Under (A1$'$), $\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v)\approx\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v)\approx
\lambda(v)$, so any symmetric mean of the two recovers $\lambda(v)$. We
choose the harmonic mean because it penalises violations of (A1$'$) more
aggressively than the arithmetic or geometric mean:
$\mathrm{HM}(x,y)\le\mathrm{GM}(x,y)\le\mathrm{AM}(x,y)$, with equality
only when $x=y$, and $\mathrm{HM}(x,y)\to 0$ whenever either argument
vanishes while $\mathrm{AM}$ remains nonzero. This matches the failure
mode the HM variant is designed to reject, in which a direction is
highly aligned with the current block but has no future support.

The resulting score is
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)
:=
n\cdot\mathrm{HM}\!\bigl(\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v),\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v)\bigr)
\cdot\phi_w(v),
\tag{HM-SCORE}
\end{align}
equivalently
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)
=
\frac{2n\,\|A_wv\|_2^2\,\|F_wv\|_2^2}
{\mathrm{row}(F_w)\|A_wv\|_2^2+\mathrm{row}(A_w)\|F_wv\|_2^2+\varepsilon}
\cdot\phi_w(v).
\end{align}
Under (A1$'$), $n\cdot\mathrm{HM}(\hat\lambda_{A_w},\hat\lambda_{F_w})
\approx n\lambda(v)=\|Av\|_2^2$, so
\begin{align}
\|Av\|_2^2\;\approx\;
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)\cdot\phi_w(v)^{-1}
\quad\text{under (A1$'$)},
\end{align}
and the score itself equals $\|Av\|_2^2\cdot\phi_w(v)$ under (A1$'$).
The HM-score is therefore an \emph{estimator} of
$\|Av\|_2^2\cdot\phi_w(v)$ that is conservative against (A1$'$)
violations, not a proof-style lower bound.

\paragraph{[INCOMPLETE] Deriving the HM form from the subsampling
inequality $(\star)$.}
The additive score inherits the lower-bound interpretation
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)\le\|Av\|_2^2$ directly from
$(\star)+(\mathrm{A1})+(\mathrm{A2})$. A corresponding derivation that
yields $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)$ as a bound on
$\|Av\|_2^2$ starting from $(\star)$ (or a Hölder-class relative) is
\emph{not written down}. Required pieces that are missing:
\begin{itemize}
\item A subsampling inequality analogous to $(\star)$ but applied
symmetrically to two disjoint row blocks $A_w,F_w$ of $A$ rather than to
a nested prefix $N_w\subset A$.
\item A probabilistic or deterministic argument under which the harmonic
mean of per-row rates lower-bounds $\|Av\|_2^2/n$. Candidate tools: a
Chebyshev / second-moment argument treating $\hat\lambda_{A_w}$ and
$\hat\lambda_{F_w}$ as independent estimators of $\lambda(v)$, a matrix
concentration bound on row partitions, or a direct rearrangement
inequality converting $\mathrm{HM}\le\mathrm{GM}\le\mathrm{AM}$ into a
two-sided bound on $\lambda(v)$ with a controlled gap.
\item Handling of the lookahead lag. The online variant accesses $F_w$
only after a fixed lag, so any bound must account for the residual rows
between $A_w$ and $F_w$ that are neither used nor available, or relate
$\lambda(v)$ computed on the two windows to $\lambda(v)$ on the full
matrix under mixing/stationarity assumptions.
\end{itemize}
Until this derivation is filled in,
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}$ should be understood as a
stationarity-based estimator with empirically verified robustness, not as
a theorem-backed lower bound. The additive form remains the reference
when a proof-style bound is required.

\paragraph{Algorithm overview.}
Process one row-window at a time. For the first window, there is no
previous sketch and no old row sample, so $B=0$, $R=\varnothing$,
$C_1=A_1$, $M_1=A_1$, and (in the lookahead variant) $F_1$ is taken from
the rows immediately following $A_1$. For a later window $w$, the input
state is the carried sketch $B^{(w-1)}=\Sigma_{w-1}V_{w-1}^\top$, the row
sample $R^{(w-1)}$, and the lookahead buffer $F_w$. The algorithm at
window $w$ performs three stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Stage 1 --- subspace selection.} Evaluate
$\operatorname{score}_w$ (either form) on a finite candidate pool
$\mathcal P_w$ and pick the $r$-element subset with largest total score.
Let $\mathcal S_w$ denote the span of the selected candidates.
\item \emph{Stage 2 --- Rayleigh--Ritz frame refinement.} Let $Q_c$ be
an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal S_w$ (via QR). Compute the restricted
SVD $M_wQ_c=U_c\Sigma_cV_c^\top$ and take $\widehat V_w:=Q_cV_c$.
\item \emph{Stage 3 --- carry-sketch update.} Form $Y_w:=M_w\widehat V_w$,
$U_{\hat{},w}:=\operatorname{orth}(Y_w)$, and compute
$U_{\hat{},w}^\top M_w=U_{\mathrm{small},w}\Sigma_wV_w^\top$. The sketch
passed to the next window is $B^{(w)}=\Sigma_wV_w^\top$. The row sample
$R^{(w)}$ is updated from the raw rows seen through window $w$.
\end{enumerate}
The rest of the document expands and justifies each stage.

\paragraph{Stage 1 --- subspace selection by simple argmax.}
Let $\operatorname{score}_w$ denote either the additive or the HM score.
Stage 1 is a single argmax over size-$r$ subsets of the pool:
\begin{align}
\widetilde V_w
\;\in\;
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{S\subseteq\mathcal P_w,\ |S|=r}
\ \sum_{v\in S}\operatorname{score}_w(v),
\qquad
\mathcal S_w\;:=\;\operatorname{span}(\widetilde V_w).
\tag{S1}
\end{align}
When pool elements are linearly independent and distinct in score, (S1)
reduces to top-$r$-by-score selection. No orthogonality constraint is
imposed in Stage 1 and no sequential greedy structure is needed.
Orthogonality is handled by the Rayleigh--Ritz reframe in Stage 2, which
operates on any basis of $\mathcal S_w$. This simplification is valid
whenever the pool elements are structurally distinct enough that the
top-$r$ span $\mathcal S_w$ is genuinely $r$-dimensional; the Stage-2 SVD
flags the degenerate case through $\sigma_r(M_wQ_c)\to 0$, and the
algorithm can fall back to a smaller effective rank.

\paragraph{Candidate pool.}
The pool $\mathcal P_w$ is a fixed set of $|\mathcal P_w|\ge r$
online-computable unit vectors in $\mathbb R^d$. Abstractly, each
$v\in\mathcal P_w$ should be derivable from the online state
$(B,A_w,R,B^{(w-1)})$ without access to $F_w$ (so that $F_w$ enters only
through score evaluation, not through candidate construction), and the
pool should cover the expected failure modes of the score so that the
argmax can avoid them.

One concrete pool that has been validated empirically uses the five
candidates below; the method is not tied to this specific choice.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Streaming-optimizer slot 2.} $v_\mathrm{opt}^{(2)}$: the
second direction returned by projected-gradient ascent on
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}$ initialised orthogonally to the
first-slot optimum.
\item \emph{Current-block SVD complement.} $v_\mathrm{blk}$: the top
right singular vector of $A_w$ projected onto the orthogonal complement
of the first-slot direction.
\item \emph{Sketched SVD complement.} $v_\mathrm{sk}$: the top right
singular vector of $M_w$ projected onto the orthogonal complement of
the first-slot direction.
\item \emph{Projected oracle analogue.} $v_\mathrm{q2}$: a reference
direction constructed by projecting a candidate onto the row space of
the current block and renormalising (the specific construction depends
on the available state; in the purely online setting, this candidate is
replaced by a second SVD-complement variant).
\item \emph{Previous-window slot-2 carry.} $v_\mathrm{prev}$: the
slot-2 direction selected at window $w-1$, carried forward as a
stability anchor.
\end{itemize}
Pool edits that add candidates with disproportionately large
$\|A_wv\|_2^2$ but negligible past or future support shift the argmax
toward those candidates by inflating the score's gain numerator, and
should be validated with an ablation before deployment.

\paragraph{Stage 2 --- Rayleigh--Ritz frame refinement.}
Let $Q_c\in\mathbb R^{d\times r}$ with $Q_c^\top Q_c=I$ and
$\mathrm{span}(Q_c)=\mathcal S_w$ (obtained from QR of the selected pool
elements). Compute the restricted SVD
\begin{align}
M_wQ_c\;=\;U_c\,\Sigma_c\,V_c^\top,
\qquad
M_w=\begin{pmatrix}B\\A_w\end{pmatrix},
\end{align}
and take
\begin{align}
\widehat V_w \;:=\; Q_cV_c.
\end{align}
$\widehat V_w$ is the top-$r$ right singular frame of $M_w$ restricted
to $\mathcal S_w$, equivalently the unique (up to sign) orthonormal
frame of $\mathcal S_w$ that diagonalises $Q_c^\top M_w^\top M_wQ_c$.
The output is independent of the choice of $Q_c$: a different ONB of
$\mathcal S_w$ produces $Q_cR$ for some rotation $R$, which rotates
$V_c$ to $R^\top V_c$, and the product $Q_cV_c$ is unchanged.

By Rayleigh--Ritz, $\widehat V_w$ is the minimiser of the Stage-2
frame objective:
\begin{align}
\widehat V_w
\;=\;
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{V\in\mathrm{St}(r,\mathcal S_w)}
\|M_w-M_wVV^\top\|_F
\;=\;
\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{V\in\mathrm{St}(r,\mathcal S_w)}
\|M_wV\|_F^2,
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{St}(r,\mathcal S_w)$ is the set of orthonormal
$d\times r$ matrices with columns in $\mathcal S_w$.

\paragraph{Stage 3 --- carry-sketch update.}
Using the Ritz frame $\widehat V_w$, form
\begin{align}
Y_w\;:=\;M_w\widehat V_w,
\qquad
U_{\hat{},w}\;:=\;\operatorname{orth}(Y_w),
\end{align}
compute the SVD
\begin{align}
U_{\hat{},w}^\top M_w\;=\;U_{\mathrm{small},w}\Sigma_wV_w^\top,
\end{align}
and store
\begin{align}
U_{\mathrm{proj},w}=U_{\hat{},w}U_{\mathrm{small},w},
\qquad
S_w=\Sigma_w,
\qquad
V_w^\top\;\text{(the right singular factor)}.
\end{align}
The sketch passed to the next window is
\begin{align}
B^{(w)}\;:=\;\Sigma_wV_w^\top.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Why this is the sketch carried into the next window.}
Consider the naive alternative of carrying $\widehat V_w$ together with
column norms $s_i:=\|M_w\widehat v_i\|_2$. With
\begin{align}
M_w=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\widehat V_w=[e_1,e_2],
\end{align}
one has $s_1=s_2=1$, so
$B_\mathrm{diag}:=\mathrm{diag}(s)\widehat V_w^\top=I$ gives
$\|B_\mathrm{diag}v\|_2^2=1$ uniformly on $\mathbb S^1$, whereas
\begin{align}
\|M_wv\|_2^2=v^\top\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}v
\end{align}
vanishes at $v=(1,-1)/\sqrt 2$ and equals $2$ at $v=(1,1)/\sqrt 2$.
Column norms drop the cross-correlations
$(M_w\widehat v_i)^\top(M_w\widehat v_j)$, which are generically nonzero
because the Ritz-refined frame $\widehat V_w$ is aligned with the
singular structure of $M_w$ restricted to $\mathcal S_w$ rather than the
full $M_w$. Carrying $B^{(w)}=\Sigma_wV_w^\top$ instead is the
norm-preserving right-action form of $P_{U_{\hat{},w}}M_w$, where
$P_{U_{\hat{},w}}=U_{\hat{},w}U_{\hat{},w}^\top$ is the orthogonal
projector onto the retained left space. For any $v$,
$\|B^{(w)}v\|_2=\|P_{U_{\hat{},w}}M_wv\|_2$, and for $v\in\mathcal S_w$
this equals $\|M_wv\|_2$ exactly since
$M_wv\in\mathrm{span}(U_{\hat{},w})$. Hence $B^{(w)\top}B^{(w)}$
reproduces the full Gram $\widehat V_w^\top M_w^\top M_w\widehat V_w$ on
the retained right span, all off-diagonals included. The row sample
$R^{(w)}$ enters the pooled entropy at the next window.

Because the Stage-2 Ritz frame already satisfies
$(M_w\widehat v_i)^\top(M_w\widehat v_j)=\sigma_i^2\delta_{ij}$, the
Stage-3 carry-SVD receives an already-diagonalised input on
$\mathcal S_w$; cross-correlations only appear through the interaction
of $\mathcal S_w$ with its complement, which the carry-SVD handles
exactly.

\paragraph{Combined entropy.}
With $y:=C_wv$, the definition of $h_2$ gives
\begin{align}
h_2(C_wv)=4\log\|y\|_2-4\log\|y\|_4.
\end{align}
Applied to the stacked vector combining the old row sample $Rv$ with the
current window $A_wv$, rare large entries in either source contribute to
the same correction factor $\phi_w(v)$; the reservoir and the current
window are not separated in the entropy term.

\paragraph{Simplified form.}
Let
\begin{align}
c_w:=\frac{\log(\mathrm{row}(N_w)/n)}{2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w)},
\qquad
y:=C_wv.
\end{align}
Since
\begin{align}
h_2(C_wv)=-\log\!\left(\frac{\|y\|_4^4}{\|y\|_2^4}\right),
\end{align}
the entropy multiplier is
\begin{align}
\left(\frac{\mathrm{row}(N_w)}{n}\right)^{-h_2(C_wv)/(2\log\mathrm{row}(C_w))}
=
\left(\frac{\|y\|_4^4}{\|y\|_2^4}\right)^{c_w}
=
\|C_wv\|_4^{4c_w}\|C_wv\|_2^{-4c_w}.
\end{align}
Thus
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)
=
\left(\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)\phi_w(v),
\qquad
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)
=
n\cdot\mathrm{HM}\!\bigl(\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v),\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v)\bigr)
\cdot\phi_w(v),
\end{align}
with the common entropy factor
\begin{align}
\phi_w(v)=\|C_wv\|_4^{4c_w}\|C_wv\|_2^{-4c_w}.
\end{align}
When $\mathrm{row}(N_w)<n$, $c_w<0$, and high-entropy pooled vectors are
boosted.

\paragraph{Expanded row-sample form.}
Using
\begin{align}
C_w^\top C_wv=R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv,
\qquad
C_w^\top(C_wv)^{\odot 3}=R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3},
\end{align}
the entropy factor can be evaluated without explicitly forming $C_w$:
\begin{align}
\phi_w(v)
=
\left(
\frac{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4}
{\left(\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)^2}
\right)^{c_w}.
\end{align}
Gradient-style quantities on the entropy factor reuse these pooled
expressions:
\begin{align}
C_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
=
\frac{R^\top(Rv)^{\odot 3}+A_w^\top(A_wv)^{\odot 3}}
{\|Rv\|_4^4+\|A_wv\|_4^4},
\qquad
\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
=
\frac{R^\top Rv+A_w^\top A_wv}{\|Rv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2}.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Stored-basis form.}
If
\begin{align}
B=SV^\top,
\qquad
V=[v_1,\dots,v_{r_\text{sk}}],
\qquad
S=\mathrm{diag}(s_1,\dots,s_{r_\text{sk}}),
\end{align}
then
\begin{align}
\|Bv\|_2^2
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r_\text{sk}} s_j^2(v_j^\top v)^2,
\qquad
B^\top Bv
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r_\text{sk}} s_j^2(v_j^\top v)v_j.
\end{align}
The past-energy term of the additive score and its gradient are
therefore evaluated from $(V,s)$ directly, without materialising $B$.

\paragraph{Gradient of the additive score.}
Let $y_B:=Bv$, $z:=A_wv$, $y:=C_wv$. Then
\begin{align}
\nabla\!\left(\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2\right)
=
2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv,
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\nabla\log\phi_w(v)
=
4c_wC_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
-4c_w\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}.
\end{align}
The full Euclidean gradient is
\begin{align}
\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)
=
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)\left[
\frac{2B^\top Bv+2A_w^\top A_wv}{\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2}
+4c_wC_w^\top\!\left(\frac{(C_wv)^{\odot 3}}{\|C_wv\|_4^4}\right)
-4c_w\frac{C_w^\top C_wv}{\|C_wv\|_2^2}
\right],
\end{align}
provided $\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2>0$.

\paragraph{Gradient of the HM score.}
Let $a(v):=\|A_wv\|_2^2$, $f(v):=\|F_wv\|_2^2$, $p:=\mathrm{row}(A_w)$,
$q:=\mathrm{row}(F_w)$, and $D(v):=qa(v)+pf(v)+\varepsilon$. Then
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)
=
\frac{2n\,a(v)f(v)}{D(v)}\cdot\phi_w(v),
\end{align}
and, with $\nabla a(v)=2A_w^\top A_wv$, $\nabla f(v)=2F_w^\top F_wv$,
\begin{align}
\nabla\!\left(\frac{2n\,a(v)f(v)}{D(v)}\right)
=
\frac{2n}{D(v)}\bigl(f(v)\nabla a(v)+a(v)\nabla f(v)\bigr)
-\frac{2n\,a(v)f(v)}{D(v)^2}\bigl(q\nabla a(v)+p\nabla f(v)\bigr).
\end{align}
The entropy factor has the same gradient as above. The full Euclidean
gradient of $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}$ follows by the product
rule; it is not used in the pool-based Stage 1 defined above, which
evaluates the HM score on a finite set rather than optimising it
continuously, but it is needed if the pool is replaced by a continuous
optimiser.

\paragraph{Optimization problem for candidate construction.}
Certain candidates in $\mathcal P_w$ are themselves defined as solutions
to a continuous optimisation; for those the feasible set is
\begin{align}
\max_{v\in\mathbb S^{d-1},\ Q^\top v=0}\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v),
\end{align}
where the columns of $Q$ are previously accepted directions.

\paragraph{Projected full-gradient ascent.}
Use the exact gradient of the additive score. Project it onto the
feasible tangent space:
\begin{align}
g_\mathrm{tan}(v):=(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v).
\end{align}
Then take
\begin{align}
\widetilde v^+=v+\eta\,g_\mathrm{tan}(v),
\qquad
v^+=\frac{(I-QQ^\top)\widetilde v^+}{\|(I-QQ^\top)\widetilde v^+\|_2},
\end{align}
with $\eta>0$ chosen by backtracking line search on the true score.

\paragraph{Basis-expansion interpretation.}
If one is building a local search subspace rather than taking a pure
steepest-ascent step, the new direction added to the basis is the
projected full gradient
\begin{align}
g_\mathrm{basis}(v):=(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v).
\end{align}
In a reduced-subspace version with local right basis
$Z\in\mathbb R^{d\times q}$ ($Z^\top Z=I$), the expansion residual is
\begin{align}
r_Z(v):=(I-ZZ^\top)(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v).
\end{align}
Expand the basis with the normalised residual $r_Z(v)/\|r_Z(v)\|_2$ when
$\|r_Z(v)\|_2/\|(I-QQ^\top-vv^\top)\nabla\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)\|_2$
exceeds a prescribed tolerance; optionally follow with Krylov
refinements.

\paragraph{Rayleigh--Ritz optimality.}
The Stage-2 Ritz frame $\widehat V_w$ provides the following standard
guarantees inside $\mathcal S_w$ (no assumption beyond existence of the
restricted SVD):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Energy ordering.}
$\|M_w\widehat V_w[:,i]\|_2=\sigma_i(M_wQ_c)$, ordered decreasing in $i$.
\item \emph{Diagonalisation.}
$\widehat V_w^\top M_w^\top M_w\widehat V_w=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1^2,\dots,\sigma_r^2)$.
\item \emph{Optimal truncation.} For every $k\le r$, the first $k$
columns of $\widehat V_w$ minimise $\|M_w-M_wVV^\top\|_F$ over
$V\in\mathrm{St}(k,\mathcal S_w)$.
\end{enumerate}
None of these hold generically for a Gram--Schmidt orthonormalisation of
the selected pool elements: Gram--Schmidt preserves the span but leaves
the columns aligned with the pool's construction rather than with the
singular structure of $M_w|_{\mathcal S_w}$.

\paragraph{Role of the score: test-space certificate.}
The additive score is a certified lower bound on $\|Av\|_2^2$ under
$(\mathrm{A1})+(\mathrm{A2})$. Applied to Stage 1 (S1), the selected
candidates satisfy
\begin{align}
\sum_{v\in\widetilde V_w}\|Av\|_2^2
\;\gtrsim\;
\sum_{v\in\widetilde V_w}\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v)
\;\ge\;
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v_i^*)
\;\gtrsim\;
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i(A)^2,
\end{align}
provided the top-$r$ right singular vectors $v_i^*$ of $A$ are contained
in (or well approximated by) the pool $\mathcal P_w$. The score's role
is therefore to supply a \emph{test-space certificate}: the span
$\mathcal S_w$ covers the top-$r$ singular mass of $A$ up to the
(A1,A2) gap. Rayleigh--Ritz then extracts the provably-best rank-$r$
frame inside $\mathcal S_w$, and the carry-sketch update stores the
restricted operator's Gram factorisation for the next window.

The HM variant substitutes a stationarity-based estimator for the
certified bound at Stage 1. Under (A1$'$)+(A2) at $v=v_i^*$, the
heuristic analogue $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v_i^*)\approx
\sigma_i(A)^2\phi_w(v_i^*)$ would give the same certificate, but the
violation tolerance is not quantified.

\paragraph{[INCOMPLETE] Coverage certificate for the HM variant.}
For the Stage 1 certificate to carry over to the HM score, we need an
analogue of $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{add}}(v_i^*)\gtrsim
\sigma_i(A)^2$ for $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}$. A natural
candidate is
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v_i^*)\;\gtrsim\;\sigma_i(A)^2
\qquad\text{under }(\mathrm{A1}'){+}(\mathrm{A2}),
\end{align}
which holds exactly when $\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v_i^*)\approx
\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v_i^*)\approx\lambda(v_i^*)=\sigma_i(A)^2/n$, but the
$(\mathrm{A1}')$-violation tolerance of the inequality has not been
quantified. What is needed:
\begin{itemize}
\item A quantitative stability bound of the form
$|\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)-\|Av\|_2^2\phi_w(v)|\le
g(\hat\lambda_{A_w}(v),\hat\lambda_{F_w}(v))$, so that the HM score's
lower-bound gap is controlled by the stationarity deficit.
\item A translation of such a stability bound into a subspace-distance
bound $\mathrm{dist}(\mathcal S_w,\mathrm{span}(v_1^*,\dots,v_r^*))$,
matching the role of the $(\mathrm{A1})+(\mathrm{A2})$ gap for the
additive score.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{[INCOMPLETE] End-to-end approximation bound.}
The three-stage algorithm combines a bound-backed Stage 1 (additive
score) with a theorem-backed Stage 2 (Rayleigh--Ritz) and a
norm-preserving Stage 3 (carry sketch), but a single end-to-end bound on
$\|A-AV_\mathrm{final}V_\mathrm{final}^\top\|_F$ in terms of windowed
quantities is not written. The ingredients are in place:
\begin{itemize}
\item Stage-1 test-space coverage:
$\|AP_{\mathcal S_w}\|_F^2\gtrsim\sum_{v\in\widetilde V_w}
\operatorname{score}_w(v)\gtrsim\sum_{i=1}^r\sigma_i(A)^2$ under
$(\mathrm{A1})+(\mathrm{A2})$.
\item Stage-2 Ritz optimality inside $\mathcal S_w$.
\item Stage-3 carry-sketch norm-preservation on the retained right span.
\end{itemize}
What is missing is a composition argument that tracks the per-window
approximation error through the sequence of carry updates and yields a
final bound in terms of the per-window (A1,A2) gaps and the Ritz
truncation residuals $\|M_wQ_c-U_c\Sigma_cV_c^\top\|$ (zero for
$r=\dim\mathcal S_w$, nonzero otherwise). For the HM variant, such a
bound must further absorb the gap documented in the [INCOMPLETE]
HM-derivation paragraph.

\paragraph{First-window special case.}
When $B=0$, $R=\varnothing$, and $F_1$ is the first lookahead buffer,
the entropy factor reduces to
\begin{align}
\phi_1(v)=\|A_1v\|_4^{4c_1}\|A_1v\|_2^{-4c_1},
\qquad
c_1=\frac{\log(\mathrm{row}(A_1)/n)}{2\log\mathrm{row}(A_1)}.
\end{align}
The additive score reduces to
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{add}}(v)
=
\|A_1v\|_2^2\,\phi_1(v)
=
\|A_1v\|_2^2\,\|A_1v\|_4^{4c_1}\|A_1v\|_2^{-4c_1},
\end{align}
and the HM score to
\begin{align}
\operatorname{score}_1^{\mathrm{HM}}(v)
=
\frac{2n\,\|A_1v\|_2^2\,\|F_1v\|_2^2}
{\mathrm{row}(F_1)\|A_1v\|_2^2+\mathrm{row}(A_1)\|F_1v\|_2^2+\varepsilon}
\cdot\phi_1(v).
\end{align}
The three-stage structure is the same as for general $w$; only $M_1=A_1$
differs in that it has no sketch block.

\paragraph{Practical remarks.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The objective is nonconvex in candidates that are themselves
continuously optimised; multiple restarts are useful for those
candidates. Reject or regularise steps when $\|C_wv\|_2$,
$\|C_wv\|_4$, $\|Bv\|_2^2+\|A_wv\|_2^2$, $\|A_wv\|_2$, or $\|F_wv\|_2$
is numerically tiny in a formula that divides by that quantity.
\item The expanded row-sample form avoids forming $C_w$ explicitly; the
pooled norms must still include both $Rv$ and $A_wv$.
\item The row sample is not a replacement for the old sketch. $B$
supplies retained old energy; $R$ supplies raw old rows for the pooled
entropy. A pure reservoir gives coverage but can miss rare large rows
that dominate $\ell_4$ terms. A practical row sample should combine a
uniform reservoir with a small set of rows having large entries on
current sketch directions.
\item The Rayleigh--Ritz reframe is numerically well-conditioned even
when selected pool elements are nearly collinear in $\mathbb R^d$: the
SVD sees the rank-deficiency through $\sigma_r(M_wQ_c)\to 0$, whereas a
Gram--Schmidt orthonormalisation of the same elements would amplify the
residual direction through division by a small norm. Fall back to a
smaller effective rank only if $\sigma_r(M_wQ_c)$ is below a prescribed
tolerance.
\item The online HM variant requires a lookahead lag of
$\mathrm{row}(F_w)$ rows. It is online-deployable at a latency cost but
not strictly streaming. The additive score with $F_w=\varnothing$
remains strictly streaming (zero lag).
\item The reservoir $R$ enters both scores only through the entropy
factor $\phi_w$, not through the gain factors.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open items.}
The [INCOMPLETE] paragraphs above flag the missing pieces of the
theoretical story:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Derivation of $\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}$ as a
lower-bound quantity starting from a subsampling inequality analogous
to $(\star)$.
\item A coverage certificate of the form
$\operatorname{score}_w^{\mathrm{HM}}(v_i^*)\gtrsim\sigma_i(A)^2$ with
quantified $(\mathrm{A1}')$-deficit tolerance.
\item End-to-end approximation bound composing Stage-1 coverage,
Stage-2 Ritz optimality, and Stage-3 carry-sketch exactness across
$w=1,\dots,W$.
\end{enumerate}
Filling (1) and (2) is required before the HM variant can be reported
with the same theoretical status as the additive score. Filling (3)
would promote the three-stage pipeline from a justified heuristic into
an approximation algorithm with explicit error guarantees.
